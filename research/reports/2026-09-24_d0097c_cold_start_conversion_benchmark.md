# D-0097C｜Cold-Start Conversion / Personality-Tone Benchmark

# Executive Conclusion

For an unknown author:

> **the reader does not owe the book patience.**

Long-run architecture can be excellent and still never be discovered.

High-conversion openings usually give at least two of these immediately:
- unmistakable protagonist;
- unmistakable tone;
- one-line premise;
- immediate fruit;
- strong relationship chemistry;
- quotable/retellable behavior;
- visual reversal.

The best cold-start formula is not “more noise”.

It is:
> **high identity density + low comprehension cost + fast proof.**

# 2. Five Cold-Start Archetypes

## A. Personality-Comedy
Reader wants:
> “I want to see this person react again.”

Needs:
- stable logic;
- repeatable behavior;
- serious consequence beneath joke.

## B. Personality-Intensity
Reader wants:
> “this person is dangerous/strange enough that I want to watch.”

Good for darker books.
No jokes required.

## C. Relationship-Chemistry
Reader wants:
> “put these two people in the same room again.”

Useful where protagonist alone is quieter.

## D. Competence/Attitude
Reader wants:
> “watch this person handle a hard situation in a satisfying way.”

Risk:
> generic competent MC.

Needs a private bias/quirk to differentiate.

## E. Spectacle/Premise
Reader wants:
> “show me that impossible thing again at larger scale.”

Needs quick transition into character/asset or it becomes a demo.

# 3. Unknown-Author Gate by Length

## First ~500 words
Need at least one:
- impossible premise in action;
- protagonist behavior signature;
- social reversal;
- striking danger.

Reader should not be waiting for setup to finish.

## ~2k words
Reader should know:
- what MC wants now;
- how MC differs from generic MC;
- what the book feels like tonally;
- first clear promise of reward.

## ~5k words
Need:
- first real fruit or irreversible cost;
- recurring relationship seed;
- book-specific scene, not generic genre scene.

## ~10k words
Need:
- Book Contract shadow;
- next tangible target;
- evidence the premise can produce more than one trick;
- at least one non-MC worth seeing again.

# 4. Personality Signature Test

A protagonist needs:

1. desire;
2. behavioral bias;
3. flaw/oddity;
4. social style;
5. repeatable scene-generation source.

Example function:
> “counts every debt, refuses ambiguous favors”
is better than:
> “smart and cautious.”

Because the first automatically creates dialogue and choices.

# 5. Humor Matrix

## Humor helps when it:
- reveals character;
- reveals the world;
- changes social position;
- creates relationship chemistry;
- releases pressure before harder stakes;
- makes exposition enjoyable.

## Humor hurts when it:
- is random meme insertion;
- makes every speaker same voice;
- cancels consequences;
- requires protagonist to become stupid;
- pauses plot for performance;
- repeats one joke without escalation.

# 6. Long-Run Comedy Requirement

A funny opening is only commercial architecture if Ch100 still has fuel.

Sustainable comedy engines:
- stable worldview conflict;
- role/status mismatch;
- recurring relationship;
- absurd but consistent institution;
- protagonist habit that creates real consequences.

Unsustainable:
- catchphrase;
- insult spam;
- hot memes;
- everyone misunderstanding the same thing forever.

# 7. Why “Cheap” Sometimes Converts

Commercially useful “cheap-looking” tools:
- blunt title;
- chapter title as punchline/promise;
- direct protagonist boast;
- fast face-slap/social reversal;
- explicit ability ceiling;
- conversational narration.

They work when:
> they reduce uncertainty and deliver identity.

They fail when:
> promise > actual story.

# 8. Serious Books Can Use the Same Functions

A serious book can still:
- give MC a hard private rule;
- use dry humor;
- make a relationship immediately charged;
- use vivid social embarrassment;
- make the first payoff fast;
- give a quotable non-comic line.

Cold-start identity is not equal to comedy.

# 9. Transfer Rules for Sheqi

1. No generic “competent reasonable man” as default.
2. First 3 chapters must contain a behavior only this MC would do.
3. At least one recurring relationship should have chemistry by Opening.
4. Tone should be recognizable without the title.
5. If a funny/manic version retains stakes and improves continuation, it may beat the restrained version.
6. Comedy must come from Concept/world/personality logic.
7. One-line premise and one-line personality should both be possible.
8. Do not expose deep lore before identity.
9. Immediate fruit should arrive before trust is earned.
10. Architecture must protect the personality engine after scale expands.

# 10. Cold-Start PASS Standard

PASS if a clean reader can answer after Ch1–3:

- Who is this person?
- What weird/strong bias does he have?
- What does he want right now?
- What did he gain/lose?
- Who else do I want to see?
- What is the tone?
- What do I want next?

If the answer to “who is he?” is only:
> “the guy with X ability,”

cold-start character is weak.

# 11. Final Principle

> **An unknown author cannot ask the reader to wait until the architecture becomes impressive.**

The opening must already contain:
> premise + person + payoff + flavor.

The long-run must then prove:
> the flavor was not a trick.
