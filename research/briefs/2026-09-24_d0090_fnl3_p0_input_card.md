# Book Desire Thesis

After monster tides destroy buildings, some ruins condense usable building cores.

Other survivors scavenge:
- medicine;
- food;
- batteries;
- weapons.

The protagonist sees the larger asset:
> **the hospital itself.**

He has practical engineering/site experience and has already learned the cost of losing a home that cannot move.

His long desire:
> build a home disaster cannot leave behind.

# Protagonist Lens

When everyone looks at loot, he notices:
- what still works;
- who is needed to make it work;
- what it costs to move;
- what must be sacrificed to keep it.

Do not explain this as a doctrine.
Show it in his choice.

# P0 Promise

By the end:
> Reader must receive undeniable proof that a whole building/function can become part of a mobile base.

Keep the shelf contrast simple:
> others take the medicine; he goes after the hospital.

# Human Consequence

The first building asset must already be tied to one concrete survival/life problem.

Examples of function only:
- someone needs treatment;
- a professional refuses to leave;
- the asset cannot function without a person;
- taking it means giving something else up.

Choose one naturally.
Do not solve base society in P0.

# Tone

Fanqie-primary.

Immediate, physical, survival-level.
The weird scale advantage should be understood without terminology.

# Hard No

- no giant system panel;
- no list of future buildings;
- no “building core encyclopedia”;
- no ordinary space-ring storage;
- no revenge-neighbor/safehouse imitation;
- no full base management tutorial.
