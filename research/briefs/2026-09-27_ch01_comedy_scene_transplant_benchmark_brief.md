# 1. Research method: 1:1 beat shadow first, originality second

The author explicitly wants calibration against real successful scenes before AI optimization.

For each benchmark scene:

### Phase A｜Original beat strip
Describe the scene at beat/function level, in exact causal order:
1. normal premise;
2. normal expected behavior;
3. protagonist’s first abnormal-but-self-consistent interpretation;
4. first concrete action;
5. immediate outside reaction;
6. escalation;
7. second actor inheritance / one-up if present;
8. visible scene deformation;
9. authority / bystander consequence;
10. callback / exit.

Do NOT copy source prose.
Do NOT reproduce signature dialogue verbatim.
Do NOT produce a publishable near-copy of a distinctive copyrighted scene.

### Phase B｜1:1 functional shadow
For calibration only:
- preserve the SAME NUMBER of major beats where practical;
- preserve the SAME escalation topology;
- preserve the SAME “who reacts to whom” pattern;
- deliberately do NOT optimize for originality yet;
- replace setting, objects, stakes and actions with project-native equivalents.

Label these:
> SHADOW / NOT FOR PUBLICATION

Purpose:
> test whether the benchmark’s actual comedy force survives in this project.

### Phase C｜Distance rewrite
Only after the shadow works:
- change event shell;
- reorder beats;
- change relationship topology;
- replace visual business;
- ensure the final candidate is not a scene copy.

This sequence is mandatory.
Do not skip directly from benchmark analysis to “inspired original” and then claim equivalent effect.

# 3. Scene-card fields

For every scene, record:

- Work / arc / chapter if verifiable
- Evidence strength:
  - FULL READ
  - PUBLIC EXCERPT
  - OFFICIAL SYNOPSIS
  - SECONDARY DISCUSSION
- One-sentence scene image:
  > What would a Reader tell a friend happened?
- Why it is funny BEFORE explanation
- First laugh timing
- Physical / visual absurdity
- Verbal joke contribution
- Value-system inversion
- Second-actor inheritance
- Authority / bystander reaction
- Scene deformation:
  > how much the location / procedure / rules physically changed
- Consequence persistence
- Seriousness:
  > do characters themselves think they are joking?
- Remove-dialogue test:
  > if all punchlines vanish, what funny image remains?
- “Would this still be funny in a dry plot summary?” YES / PARTIAL / NO

Do not use abstract praise such as:
> 有反差 / 有脑洞 / 很搞笑
without identifying the physical image and causal chain.

# 6. Special hypothesis to test

Current hypothesis:

> Controlled sparring may be causally excellent but visually comedy-poor.

Test against alternatives rather than assuming it.

Specific alternatives worth serious testing:

## A. Padded obstacle / pursuit course
Modern safe obstacle threatens the same movement transition.
Potential visual rhyme:
> foam / safety arm at the exact angle -> ancient real weapon at the exact angle.

## B. Public safety demonstration
Everyone is there to learn how protection stops problems.
Cheng Ye is the one student trying to make his known problem appear fully.
Potential comedy:
> the demonstration succeeds; Cheng Ye asks to make the “problem” worse / later.
Public audience creates normal-world contrast.

## C. Inverted-score practical
Normal students optimize green/high scores.
Cheng Ye wants a legitimate red/abnormal record because that unlocks useful correction / follow-up.
A second actor may inherit this goal and begin sacrificing their own score to force Cheng Ye’s fault.
Potential comedy:
> both students become increasingly satisfied as the official score looks worse.

## D. Literal no-retreat setup
Cheng Ye physically alters a legal safe setup to remove his habitual escape route.
Potential image:
> everybody else uses cushions / safe lane to avoid faults; he asks for a padded block specifically so his body cannot automatically take the half-step exit.

Test if this remains Cheng Ye or becomes forced clowning.

# 7. Flash-entry requirement

Any viable modern scene must make the ancient entry feel CAUSED, not inserted.

Before recommending a shell, answer:

1. What exact modern physical moment triggers overlap?
2. What is the immediately matching ancient image?
3. Can the ancient image be recognized in <200–300 Chinese characters?
4. Does the modern scene remain physically active while the flash occurs?
5. Does the flash convert a comic/safe object into a lethal ancient counterpart?
6. Is the emotional flip stronger than in V17.1?

Best-case pattern:

> modern scene makes Reader laugh at a safe version of a problem;
> ancient overlap reveals the same geometry / timing under lethal stakes;
> the same visual suddenly stops being funny.

# 8. Anti-self-deception gates

A candidate may NOT be called “same effect as benchmark” unless:

- the 1:1 shadow is shown side-by-side;
- a dry summary is visibly funny;
- at least one physical image is inherently comic;
- at least one other person inherits or reacts in a way that enlarges the scene;
- the location / procedure changes because of protagonist behavior;
- a neutral Reader would not need an essay explaining why the scene is funny.

Explicit fail condition:

> If the analysis is funnier than the prose premise, the premise fails.
