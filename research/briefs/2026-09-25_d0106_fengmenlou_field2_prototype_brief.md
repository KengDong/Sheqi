# Mandatory Execution Tests

## 1. Visual identity
Reader remembers:
> the vertical apartment / doors / night evacuation,
not a list of rules.

## 2. Fair play
The key reinterpretation must have planted evidence.

Hard:
- lethal behavior is observable before payoff;
- at least one clue for the winning interpretation appears before the solution;
- midpoint reinterpretation must reuse known evidence;
- no last-minute secret clause created only to make Cheng Ye look smart.

Reader may fail to solve it, but after reveal should be able to think:
> “原来前面有这个。”

## 3. Cheng Ye｜specificity + visible competence fantasy
Prove:
- personality;
- competence;
- flaw;
- one decision only he would make;
- one visible action his current returner experience lets him do better than an ordinary smart survivor;
- at least one other character notices, trusts, resists, or changes behavior because of that demonstrated difference.

If the same plot works unchanged with a generic smart MC:
> rewrite.

## 4. Co-lead
Before writing prose, privately define exactly ONE named co-lead with:
- non-rule life desire;
- behavioral bias;
- one annoying/charming trait;
- a competence Cheng Ye lacks;
- friction with Cheng Ye;
- a different instinct about which returned rules are acceptable;
- one reason they cannot simply become Cheng Ye's follower;
- a reality-side reason to recur.

On page the co-lead must:
- win one scene;
- lose one scene;
- want something independent of Cheng Ye.

Do not expose a character card to the reader.

## 5. Multi-axis difference from R1
Field #2 must not merely swap “真名规则” for “门规则.”

Compared with R1 / the Opening experience, deliberately differ on at least THREE axes:
- story genre;
- spatial topology;
- cast size;
- tempo;
- dominant emotion;
- immediate human objective.

The reader should FEEL a different kind of story, not just parse a different noun.

## 6. Human objective
At least one person desperately wants something besides “live.”

## 7. R1
“天黑后不能说真名” may matter once in a fresh way.
It must NOT solve the Field.

## 8. Cost
No clean perfect win.
Some meaningful cost/loss must already be visible.

Allowed cost is broader than death:
- injury;
- lost trust;
- lost asset;
- separation;
- public blame;
- wrong/ugly tradeoff;
- moral debt.

## 9. Future fragment
ONE physical clue only.
Do not explain failed futures.

## 10. Dual continuation appetite
Without writing the reality aftermath, the prototype must plant both appetites:
1. “I want to know what this Field / its eventual returned rule will do to ordinary reality.”
2. “I want to keep reading another Rule Field because the Field side itself is fun.”

The prototype may stop at an early/mid-Field payoff, but the return-choice consequence cannot disappear from reader memory.

## 11. Tone
Tension first.
Humor may arise from people.
No gag over fresh injury/death.

## 12. Language
Plain Chinese.
No terminology pile.
No rule-lawyer lecture.
No AI-style sentence that explains what the scene has already shown.
