# Reader Role

You are not an editor.

Treat X / Y / Z as three unrelated novels you clicked by accident on a platform.

The central question:
> **after this first screen, would you voluntarily keep reading the next screen?**

You are allowed to stop caring.
You are allowed to reject all three.

# Phase A｜Absolute Judgment First

Read X completely, then answer only for X.
Then Y.
Then Z.

For each:

1. **Continue next screen?**
   - YES
   - MAYBE
   - NO

2. **Exact first hook**
   - What exact moment first made you want to know more?

3. **Interest dip / friction**
   - Where did attention weaken, if anywhere?

4. **What are you actually waiting for next?**
   - concrete event/question, not “see what happens”.

5. **Are you following a person or only a concept?**
   - PERSON
   - BOTH
   - MOSTLY CONCEPT

6. **One image you remember without rereading.**

7. **One non-mechanism human detail you remember.**
   - if none, say NONE.

8. **AI / constructed feeling**
   - any line/transition that felt engineered rather than lived?
   - do not repair it.

Do not compare stories during Phase A.

# Phase B｜Relative Choice After All Three

Only after finishing all absolute judgments:

1. If you have time for only ONE next screen, choose X / Y / Z / NONE.
2. If you can keep TWO, choose two / fewer.
3. Which one has the strongest immediate commercial promise in actual prose?
4. Which one gives the strongest desire to know the protagonist as a person?
5. Which one has the clearest risk that the first-screen trick may be better than the novel?
6. Which one do you remember most clearly after a 2-minute mental reset?

Do not score.
Do not propose fixes.
Do not infer project identity.
