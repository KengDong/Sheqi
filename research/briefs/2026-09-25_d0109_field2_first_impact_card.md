# D-0109｜Field #2 First-Impact Card
## 目标：不是“规则设计不错”，而是Reader第一次亲眼看见这本书为什么不普通

# 0. Role

《封门楼》 remains an INTERNAL prototype codename.

This card freezes:
- first visual impact;
- first rule spectacle;
- first human pain;
- first Cheng Ye trust signal;
- first co-lead friction;
- first macro-scale shadow.

It does NOT freeze:
- final building/project name;
- full Field solution;
- final Returned Rule;
- canon co-lead identity;
- full Field ending.

# 1. Highest First-Impact Standard

After reading the first 1–2 prototype chapters, a cold reader should be able to retell something close to:

> “现实里那里明明还只是工地，他进去以后却是一栋已经住了很多年的四十层住宅楼。”
>
> “然后一声响，整栋楼的门一扇接一扇全关了，外面的人再也打不开。”
>
> “男主不是永远猜对，他会提前留一条‘如果我猜错了怎么办’的退路。”

If the reader mainly remembers:
> “有条门规则挺聪明。”

then FIRST IMPACT FAILS.

# 2. First Shock｜Impossible Normality, Not Monster Spectacle

Entry should begin from an ORDINARY present-day construction project.

Before transition, Reader should understand:
- the address is real;
- construction is incomplete;
- the residential building should not yet exist.

After transition:
> the completed residential tower already stands there.

Do NOT make it a gothic haunted mansion.

Make it disturbingly lived-in:
- warm windows;
- laundry;
- delivery boxes;
- shoe racks;
- property notices;
- children's objects;
- cooking smells / TV / plumbing / elevator sounds.

The shock is:
> **this impossible future has ordinary life inside it.**

Avoid:
- instant blood wall;
- giant monster at entry;
- “welcome to the dungeon” narration;
- lore explanation.

# 3. Reader-Facing Building Name

Writer must choose ONE believable ordinary residential/project name.

Rules:
- sounds like a real commercial/residential project;
- no symbolic “诡/封/亡/劫/禁/门” naming;
- no obviously thematic four-character author label;
- present construction project and future completed tower use the SAME ordinary name.

Characters naturally say:
> “这栋楼 / 这边 / 小区 / 工地 / X栋”

not:
> “封门楼.”

The internal filename/codename may remain 《封门楼》.

# 4. Second Shock｜The Building Closes Itself

Do NOT explain rules first.

Let the rule become visible through event.

Preferred sensory grammar:
- one bell / chime / building signal;
- one door closes;
- then another;
- then the sound runs vertically through the tower;
- hundreds of ordinary doors click shut in sequence.

The building should briefly feel like:
> a forty-floor organism sealing its cells.

Only AFTER the event should characters begin inferring what happened.

# 5. Rule Simplicity at First Contact

Early reader-facing rule experience should stay extremely simple:

> once a door is fully closed, people outside cannot open it.

Do NOT foreground all three prototype clauses immediately.

The first chapter should not feel like:
> “read the rules, then solve.”

It should feel like:
> “people are trapped by something painfully ordinary.”

# 6. First Human Pain｜One Door, One Person We Care About

Avoid anonymous casualty montage.

Before or immediately after the mass closure, establish ONE small human desire.

Preferred shapes:
- parent outside / child inside;
- daughter outside / elderly parent inside;
- rescuer outside / injured person inside.

The locked door should become emotionally legible through one specific relationship.

Hard:
> a familiar domestic door failing is more important than a large body count.

No fresh-death gag.
No five-character tragedy pileup.

# 7. Co-Lead Entrance｜Action Before Introduction

The co-lead must first be remembered for DOING something.

Preferred function:
> emergency medicine / rescue / triage / disaster response.

Entrance requirement:
- Cheng Ye wants one more piece of certainty / a safer test;
- co-lead acts because waiting will cost a life;
- the action works;
- Reader sees immediately that this person has real competence independent of Cheng Ye.

Do NOT introduce with:
- biography paragraph;
- profession card;
- admiration of Cheng Ye;
- “我叫X，我是医生/消防员” unless organically forced.

# 8. Cheng Ye First Trust Signal｜Wrong Guess, Survived

Within the early prototype, Cheng Ye must make or endorse ONE interpretation that proves incomplete/wrong.

Before testing it, he has already created a fallback.

Required structure:
1. evidence suggests interpretation A;
2. Cheng Ye refuses to stake everyone on A;
3. he creates a minimum-cost test + fallback;
4. A proves wrong/incomplete;
5. fallback visibly prevents a catastrophic result.

Reader payoff:
> **“他不是神，但跟着他不容易因为一次猜错就死。”**

Do NOT:
- hide information from Reader;
- reveal that Cheng Ye secretly knew the answer all along;
- make other characters stupid so his fallback looks smart.

# 9. Cheng Ye Command Moment｜Chaos Becomes Executable

Before prototype stop, give ONE short scene where:
- many people panic / shout / move at once;
- Cheng Ye does not make a speech;
- he reduces the situation into concrete roles/actions;
- people begin moving because the plan is legible.

Examples of function:
- who observes;
- who holds a door;
- who moves the injured;
- what signal means abort;
- where the fallback is.

Target feeling:
> chaos becomes manageable when Cheng Ye takes control.

But:
> the co-lead must still disobey or override him once and be right.

# 10. “Cost Has a Name” Scene｜Do Not Rush It Too Early

The line/function:
> “哪两户？说门牌。”

is a key Cheng Ye brand beat.

Do NOT waste it in the first few pages.

Use it only after the Field has created enough pressure that:
> sacrificing sealed households sounds genuinely reasonable.

Then Cheng Ye forces abstraction back into:
- specific apartment numbers;
- specific people;
- explicit decision ownership.

His insistence must also create a problem:
> delay / friction / lost opportunity / anger.

Otherwise it becomes moral grandstanding.

# 11. Third Shock｜This Place Belongs to Something Larger

Do NOT explain:
- failed futures;
- Second Reality;
- branch theory;
- world convergence.

The tower itself already proves:
> something is wrong with time/reality.

Add only ONE further spatial/macroscopic sensation in the prototype if needed:
- a road outside continues where present reality has no road;
- another distant structure should not exist;
- the tower's view aligns with a future-planned geography.

Treat it as the SAME impossible-place clue family, not a lore packet.

Reader reaction should be:
> “这栋楼不是随机生成的，它好像属于另一座真实城市。”

Not:
> “I understand the cosmology now.”

# 12. Impact Rhythm

Preferred sequence:

## Beat A
present construction site / impossible transition.

## Beat B
ordinary lived-in future tower.

## Beat C
mass door closure spectacle.

## Beat D
one concrete trapped-person problem.

## Beat E
co-lead acts and wins.

## Beat F
Cheng Ye runs a cautious inference/test.

## Beat G
his assumption fails, fallback pays.

## Beat H
larger human conflict escalates.

## Beat I
Cheng Ye forces cost onto concrete households/decision owners.

## Beat J
one macro-geography shadow.

Do not mechanically make each beat one chapter.
Natural prose rhythm wins.

# 13. What “Shock” Does NOT Mean

Do NOT optimize for:
- more corpses;
- gore;
- louder monsters;
- more rules;
- larger terminology;
- immediate world exposition;
- protagonist omniscience;
- nonstop cliffhangers.

Target:
> **recognition shock + visual memory + human helplessness + strategic trust + world-scale implication.**

# 14. Prototype Stop Condition

The 3–5 chapter prototype should stop only after at least ONE meaningful payoff has landed.

A valid stop should leave THREE simultaneous desires:

1. **Local**
   > “这栋楼接下来怎么活着出去？”

2. **Human**
   > “程野和这个搭档继续合作会变成什么样？”

3. **Macro**
   > “这栋不该存在的楼，到底属于哪里？”

If only #1 survives:
> the Field may be good but the BOOK identity is underperforming.

# 15. Later Reader Memory Test

The voluntary reader gate must later test UNPROMPTED recall.

After reading, ask only broad recall first:
> “不翻文本，你现在最记得哪几个画面/人/东西？”

Desired spontaneous recall includes at least TWO of:
- the not-yet-built but already inhabited tower;
- mass door closure;
- one human relationship split by a door;
- Cheng Ye's fallback after a wrong assumption;
- the co-lead's decisive rescue;
- the sense that the building belongs to a larger impossible city.

Do NOT prompt these before free recall.

# Final Rule

> **The rule is not the brand.**
>
> **The impossible lived-in future + the people trapped inside it + Cheng Ye's way of surviving uncertainty are the brand.**
