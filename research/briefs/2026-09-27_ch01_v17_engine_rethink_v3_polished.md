# 0. Core correction

The modern-half engine must not merely be:
> system protects Cheng Ye -> Cheng Ye wants to continue.

That remains a useful beat, but it is not rich enough to carry the first half of Chapter 1.

The stronger engine is:

> Reader + peer misclassify Cheng Ye's behavior
> -> both act according to that wrong model
> -> the test escalates naturally
> -> safety interruption forces the truth out
> -> earlier behavior is reinterpreted
> -> one supervised repeat now has a new relationship context
> -> dual-world overlap produces a much larger second reclassification.

This gives Chapter 1 TWO reversals:
- modern social reveal;
- dual-world reciprocal-sight reveal.

# 1. The key new decision: Reader must share the misread

Do NOT tell Reader at the start:
> Cheng Ye came here specifically to reproduce his five-year 0.11s defect.

If Reader knows that immediately, only Zhou Hang can misunderstand him.
The mini-story loses its reversal.

Instead, begin with visible behavior:

> Cheng Ye chooses a noticeably well-trained peer as his partner.

No explanation yet.

Reader can reasonably infer:
- he wants a strong opponent;
- he may be competitive;
- he may want to prove something;
- he is confident enough not to choose an easier partner.

Zhou Hang can make a similar inference.

Only after the first safety interruption do both Reader and Zhou learn:
> Cheng Ye selected strong pressure because he wanted his own recurring transition fault to appear cleanly.

This revalues:
- why he chose Zhou;
- why he did not retreat;
- why he seemed willing to let the exchange become harder.

# 2. Container

Use:
> pre-enrollment freshman movement/adaptation screening.

Only enough institution to support the event:
- same-realm students are paired for a standardized pressure segment;
- both students receive baseline movement data;
- safety monitoring can stop an exchange when strain crosses threshold;
- a flagged student may receive one supervised targeted repeat / localization segment.

Do NOT build:
- ranking;
- public scores;
- talent ceremony;
- multiple stations;
- menus;
- rulebook reading;
- medical-diagnostic equivalence.

The test is a social/action container, not the point of the chapter.

# 3. Actor goals

## Cheng Ye
Hidden-from-Reader-at-first goal:
> he has a recurring transition problem that appears only under real pressure.
> He wants a partner who will not unconsciously ease off before the problem appears.

Important:
- he is not trying to lose;
- he is not trying to injure himself;
- he is not trying to exploit a loophole;
- he wants a real sample.

## Zhou Hang
Independent goal:
> he has spent real effort/private training improving his movement and wants to see whether it holds under a standardized live-pressure segment.

He wants:
- a complete usable run;
- a partner who takes the segment seriously;
- no joke match / no deliberate accommodation.

This gives him a reason to accept Cheng Ye and later to increase pressure.

## Staff / safety system
Goal:
> valid baseline + no unnecessary injury.

It does not need personality comedy.
It only needs to be competent.

# 4. Opening beat

Open in motion, not registration.

Recommended function:
- pairing is happening or about to happen;
- Cheng Ye notices Zhou's movement quality;
- he approaches / chooses Zhou.

Reader question:
> Why did he deliberately choose the better-trained one?

Do NOT answer immediately.

Do NOT open with:
- diagnostic price;
- 0.11 number;
- Cheng Ye's biography;
- free-test policy explanation;
- “他和别人不一样” narration.

# 5. First exchange: build the shared wrong model

During the first standardized pressure segment:

1. Zhou treats the test seriously because he wants a clean, complete run.
2. Cheng Ye repeatedly refuses the comfortable early retreat when pressure closes in.
3. Zhou interprets that as:
   > Cheng Ye wants the exchange to stay hard / does not want him easing off.
4. Zhou responds by giving genuine pressure.
5. Cheng Ye sees exactly what he wanted:
   > this person will not politely let him out.
6. Cheng Ye therefore does not signal Zhou to back off.
7. Each person's reasonable response reinforces the other's model.

Important:
> do not explain the misunderstanding while it is happening.

Reader should simply see two freshmen taking the test more and more seriously.

# 6. Safety interruption

The exchange crosses the screening's strain threshold before Cheng Ye's exact transition fault is cleanly captured.

Safety system / staff stops the segment.

This is not the joke by itself.
It is the trigger for the first reveal.

Zhou's result is also incomplete because the segment was stopped.
This matters:
> Zhou now has his own reason to care about what just happened.

# 7. First reveal: reclassify Cheng Ye

After the stop, Zhou or staff naturally questions why Cheng Ye refused the obvious retreat / kept returning to the same bad transition.

Cheng Ye reveals, without a speech:
> he was waiting for his own fault to appear.

Only now introduce:
- the recurring pressure-only transition problem;
- approximate 0.11s if useful;
- he has been trying to obtain a clean sample.

This should make the opening choice click:
> he picked Zhou because Zhou looked trained enough not to release pressure accidentally.

Do NOT have narration announce:
> “原来他不是好胜。”

Let the other character's reaction and the earlier action reclassify him.

# 8. Why the reveal is funny/interesting

Remove every witty line.

The event still reads as:
> one student thought the other had deliberately chosen a strong partner because he wanted a hard contest;
> that student therefore became increasingly serious;
> only after the safety stop did he learn he had been selected precisely because Cheng Ye trusted him to expose Cheng Ye's own failure.

That is an event-level joke/character reveal.

It also creates a relationship fact:
> Zhou has now been involuntarily told, through action, that Cheng Ye rates his pressure as useful.

This can produce mild pride / irritation / curiosity without dialogue comedy.

# 9. The supervised repeat must serve BOTH people

Do not make Zhou remain only to help Cheng Ye.

Because the first segment stopped early:
- Cheng Ye still lacks the clean abnormal sample;
- Zhou still lacks the complete standardized run he wanted.

A targeted supervised repeat can therefore serve both.

This is crucial:
> after the first reveal, the two people are no longer accidentally cross-purposed.
> They knowingly agree to one more real exchange for separate but compatible reasons.

That is relationship progression inside one chapter.

Staff authorizes one focused repeat because:
- there is a real flagged transition;
- both students' initial data is incomplete;
- safety monitoring remains active.

No rule loophole.
No hidden special mode.
No long disclaimer.

# 10. Second exchange: now the reader knows the goal

The second exchange should be shorter than the first.

Reader now understands:
- what Cheng Ye is waiting for;
- what Zhou wants;
- what counts as success for each.

Therefore:
> do not re-explain the test.

Move quickly to the bottleneck.

Cheng Ye reaches the known transition under real pressure.

This time he deliberately does not use his habitual early escape.

Then the rain appears.

# 11. Dual-world collision

At the bottleneck:
- modern hall remains visible;
- rain / old courtyard / Ji Changgeng overlap;
- Ji is already injured, pursued, protecting/checking something important;
- Ji reaches the same movement transition under a much harsher constraint.

Key:
> Ji's solution exists for HIS survival.
> It is not a tutorial.

Ji cannot afford the safe modern half-beat.
He carries unfinished movement / spiritual flow directly into the next step.

Effect:
- earlier completion.

Cost:
- visible bodily strain.

Cheng Ye sees only enough to recognize:
> there is another route through this exact moment.

He copies once because current pressure is already on him.

It works.
It hurts.
Modern instrumentation records it.

# 12. Second reveal: reclassify the anomaly

At first, Cheng Ye / Reader may still classify what happened as:
- stress hallucination;
- historical echo;
- some recorded movement residue;
- unknown overlap.

Do NOT debate possibilities.

Just leave the classification open.

Then:
> rain remains.

Ji finishes his immediate survival action / checks what he protects.

Only then:
> he notices Cheng Ye.

He turns.
His gaze finds Cheng Ye.
He reacts to Cheng Ye's presence.
He asks/mouths:
> “你是谁？”

This is the chapter's larger reversal.

It reclassifies everything:
> not passive history;
> not a one-way replay;
> there is an actor on the other side.

# 13. Minimal verification

After overlap ends, only verify what strengthens the contradiction:

1. modern device truly recorded Cheng Ye's abnormal movement;
2. historical lineage contains an early matching form;
3. one visual identity marker matches the young man;
4. minimal identity/time:
   - Ji Changgeng;
   - roughly 4,100 years ago;
   - connected to early Flowing Cloud Step lineage.

Do not provide:
- later cultivation realm;
- lifespan;
- full biography;
- long database sequence;
- theories.

End on:
> the archive says ancient;
> the remembered gaze says live.

# 16. Hard anti-design guards

Do NOT:
- explain Cheng Ye's private goal before the first reveal;
- make Zhou stupid for misreading him;
- make Cheng Ye intentionally deceive Zhou;
- make staff incompetent;
- use “everyone else wants X, only Cheng Ye wants Y” thesis narration;
- create multiple safety stops;
- create a special hidden mode solely for Cheng Ye;
- preserve old money beats out of nostalgia;
- use the ancient scene as a technique lesson;
- explain the social misunderstanding after Reader already gets it;
- force Ji earlier by character count if the modern mini-story is already paying.
