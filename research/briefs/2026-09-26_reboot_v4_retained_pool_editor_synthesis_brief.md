# 2. Normalization Before Comparison

Before cross-pool comparison, normalize every pool into the same seven fields:

1. **Primary Reader Fantasy**
   - one sentence only;
   - no vehicle words standing in for fantasy.

2. **First Payment**
   - what is the earliest observable moment Reader receives the promised experience?

3. **Compounding Asset**
   - what survives victory and changes the next default state?

4. **Long-Run Loop**
   - what repeats without resetting the book?

5. **HIT-vs-WEAK Delta**
   - what divergence is actually observed;
   - separate mechanism evidence from market-causal proof.

6. **Newcomer Burden**
   - domain research;
   - logic continuity;
   - scene generation;
   - character/relationship burden;
   - long-run escalation.

7. **False Imitation Trap**
   - what an inexperienced writer is most likely to copy from the surface while missing the actual Reader function.

No prose ranking is allowed during normalization.

# 4. Cross-Pool Synthesis Questions

Answer these without ranking:

## A. What is the exact Reader payment?
Do not say:
- 爽;
- 打脸;
- 升级;
- 变强;
- 有系统.

State the exact psychological / social / ownership / autonomy transaction.

## B. What is the first irreversible state change?
Identify what first stops the story from resetting to Ch1.

## C. What actually compounds?
Separate:
- numbers;
- spectacle;
- one-off reaction;
from:
- reusable relationships;
- reputation;
- rights;
- owned abnormality;
- private infrastructure;
- institutional memory.

## D. Why do weak books fail even when the premise is correct?
Look for:
- delayed ownership;
- reset loops;
- world amnesia;
- one-off cases;
- upgrade menus;
- reaction-shot economy;
- future-promise debt;
- protagonist agency held by mentor/system;
- hierarchy disappearing after first win.

## E. What execution burden is specific to a newcomer?
Do not write generic “writing is hard.”
Identify what must be remembered chapter after chapter.

## F. What market functions appear repeatedly valuable but under-supplied?
Only describe reader functions.
Do NOT convert them into novel concepts.
