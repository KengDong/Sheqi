# D-0114｜High-Intensity Field Benchmark Scan

# Author Reopen Trigger

Author rejects:
- Field #1 as a name/identity-centered mini-system;
- current Field #2 / “封门楼” as low-end / low-danger;
- Fields where rule cleverness replaces mortal pressure.

New target:
> **每个大Field本身都要是一场疯狂、夸张、可怕、连续濒死的完整灾难。**
>
> Returned Rule is only one extractable residue from that disaster.

# External Reader / Craft Signals

## 1. Horror drops when the threat becomes too knowable / too human
Reader feedback on 《诡舍》 repeatedly praises earlier doors for strong horror and criticizes later arcs when:
- the threat becomes overly interactive / humanlike;
- the danger narrows to one known entity;
- human scheming replaces supernatural pressure.

Design implication:
> Every major Field needs at least one non-negotiable, non-human catastrophe that cannot be talked down or fully controlled.

## 2. Death count alone does not create quality
Negative feedback also appears when:
- death is arbitrary;
- survivors receive no discoverable clues;
- exit danger is inserted as plot-kill after characters already did everything right.

Design implication:
> Near-death must be frequent, but survival must remain fair-play.
> After revelation, the reader should be able to say:
> “原来前面有迹象。”

## 3. Strong Fields combine horror + logic + reversals
Reader feedback on highly rated 《诡舍》 arcs emphasizes:
- short / dense pacing;
- strong horror;
- logical survival line;
- repeated reversals / death-game tension.

## 4. Impossible objective must be legible
A craft review on infinite-flow design makes a useful point:
> the reader must understand early why the objective looks nearly impossible;
> final victory should feel miraculous AND logically earned.

## 5. Layered recontextualization drives long-form curiosity
Reader feedback on 《十日终焉》 frequently values:
- immediate lethal pressure;
- logic games;
- larger-world questions;
- earlier material becoming later foreshadowing.

Design implication:
> Field #1 must plant objects / places / dates / behaviors whose meaning changes later.

# D-0114 Field OS V2

## Rule 1｜Catastrophe First, Returned Rule Last
A major Field starts from:
> one giant survival catastrophe.

It does NOT start from:
> “what rule do we want to export?”

Returned Rule should usually represent:
> one survivable law / tool / compromise discovered inside the catastrophe.

Target share:
> Returned Rule-specific content should occupy only ~10–20% of the Field's felt experience.

## Rule 2｜One Governing Catastrophe + Related Threat Ecology
Do not use 10 unrelated gimmick rules.

Use:
- ONE governing catastrophe;
- 3–4 lethal manifestations that all grow from it.

Example threat axes:
- environment / space;
- non-human hostile ecology;
- human exploitation;
- time / resource collapse.

## Rule 3｜At Least Four Different Near-Death Modes
For a major Field, Cheng Ye should come close to death multiple times for DIFFERENT reasons.

Suggested ladder:
1. physical / environmental;
2. bad inference / hidden condition;
3. human betrayal or hostile intelligence;
4. finale where earlier solution no longer works.

Hard:
> not four repetitions of “monster nearly catches him.”

## Rule 4｜Safety Must Decay
Every discovered safe method should eventually expose a new vulnerability.

Reader rhythm:
> relief -> new understanding -> safety becomes trap -> forced movement.

No permanent safe room for half the arc.

## Rule 5｜Threat Must Exceed Human Negotiation
At least one central threat:
- cannot be bribed;
- cannot be convinced;
- cannot be frightened;
- cannot be completely gamed by personality reading.

This preserves horror.

## Rule 6｜Fair-Play Terror
Deaths / near-deaths must not be pure lottery.

Before a lethal condition pays off:
> plant physical / behavioral / temporal evidence.

Reader may miss it.
Writer may not invent it afterward.

## Rule 7｜Impossible Goal Visible Early
Within the early phase, Reader should know the Field's impossible-looking objective.

Examples:
- cross a city that is disappearing district by district;
- evacuate a tower while lower floors are being erased;
- reach one train through mutually incompatible city versions.

## Rule 8｜Human Stakes Are Not the Threat Ceiling
Human conflict adds pressure.
It must NOT replace:
> the Field itself.

## Rule 9｜Field Must Transform Cheng Ye
Field #1 especially:
- Cheng Ye begins more optimistic / humorous / assumes people can cooperate;
- he retains warmth toward allies;
- one or more Field events teach him that confirmed predators must be handled differently;
- enemy-mode ruthlessness is born from specific cost, not retroactive backstory.

## Rule 10｜Hidden Spine Seeds
Field #1 should plant:
- 1 HARD future-geography clue;
- 1 recurring date / time anomaly;
- 1 evidence of prior human interaction / Returner history;
- optionally 1 person/object whose meaning explodes later.

Do not explain any of them.

# Kill Tests

Reject a Field if:
- it can be summarized as “the name dungeon / the door dungeon”;
- the scary part is mostly people debating rules;
- Cheng Ye is rarely in physical mortal danger;
- the biggest threat can be negotiated with;
- the Returned Rule is the whole premise;
- all safe methods work forever;
- later twists require unplanted mechanics;
- the Field would be boring without its future lore.
