# REBOOT-V4 Remaining Blindspots Audit
## 即使“只选热门题材”，还有什么会让我们继续选错

# 0. Bottom line

The new validated-chassis rule fixes a major failure:
> do not invent an unproven mother market.

But it is NOT sufficient by itself.

A current hot chassis can still be a bad entry for a first-time unknown author if:
- the lane is overcrowded;
- the trend is already late-cycle;
- the direct reader pool is misidentified;
- only veteran authors can win there;
- the monetization/platform route is mismatched;
- the concept has no substitution-breaking premium;
- the protagonist fantasy is weak;
- the first three must-see scenes are not desirable.

Therefore a second gate is required:
> **MARKET ENTRY QUALIFICATION**

# 1. Missing: demand size vs competition density

“热门” answers:
> many readers exist.

It does NOT answer:
> how many competing books are fighting for the same readers.

Need:
- current strong-book count;
- recent new-book influx;
- title/premise convergence;
- how easy it is for a new book to look interchangeable.

A huge but overcrowded lane can be worse for a newcomer than a slightly smaller but less commoditized lane.

# 2. Missing: trend freshness / market lifecycle

Current success can mean:
- rising demand;
- stable evergreen demand;
- already-saturated late cycle.

Need distinguish:
> validated and still open
from
> validated but exhausted.

Do not chase a pattern merely because last year's winners still sit on charts.

# 3. Missing: subgenre / exact reader-pool resolution

“玄幻”“都市超凡”“历史” are too broad.

Need define:
> the smallest commercially meaningful reader pool.

Examples:
- urban supernatural monster hunting;
- folk-horror competence;
- rule survival mystery;
- modern cultivation social satire;
- family cultivation;
- lord-building;
- male romance campus reversal.

Direct comps must compete for the SAME appetite,
not merely share a bookstore category.

# 4. Missing: newcomer proof as a hard requirement

Veteran hits prove ceiling.
They do NOT prove newcomer accessibility.

For each market entry:
- identify current/recent newcomer / low-author-credit breakout;
- identify what allowed entry without brand;
- separate author-brand-dependent successes.

Default:
> if the lane has only veteran proof, mark HIGH ENTRY RISK.

# 5. Missing: market demand vs recommendation-system fit

A good book can still fail if packaging and early behavior do not fit platform distribution.

Need platform-specific entry research:
- Qidian paid-reading / follow / chapter economics;
- Fanqie free-reading / recommendation / completion behavior;
- title/blurb/cover norms;
- early chapter length / update rhythm;
- what public metrics actually matter.

Do NOT decide one root story must serve both platforms identically.

# 6. Missing: monetization fit

Highest goal includes:
> sustained income.

Therefore ask:
- does the reader fantasy support long paid reading / ad-supported long reading?
- does it create repeat chapter value?
- does it encourage binge or only curiosity click?
- does it require expensive research / rewrite burden that destroys output economics?

Market heat without monetizable continuation is insufficient.

# 7. Missing: fantasy bundle, not just one fantasy

Hits often stack 2–3 mutually reinforcing desires.

Example pattern:
> power growth + status reversal + forbidden access.

Need record:
- PRIMARY fantasy;
- SECONDARY fantasy;
- relationship/emotional amplifier.

A concept with only one thin pleasure may burn out quickly.

# 8. Missing: protagonist fantasy

Even inside a good market:
> “Would I want to be / follow THIS protagonist?”

Need separate:
- world fantasy;
- protagonist fantasy.

A hot monster-hunting market can still fail if protagonist is just a competent operator.

# 9. Missing: substitution test / competition differentiation

Before concept forge, ask:
> why would a reader leave the current top 5 books in this lane to try ours?

Need:
- one-sentence substitution breaker;
- what ours gives that direct comps do not;
- whether difference is meaningful or cosmetic.

“same genre + new job” is NOT enough.

# 10. Missing: must-see-scene desire before engine design

Before Reservoir, before long-run engine:

Every concept must produce:
> 3 concrete scenes the author genuinely wants to read.

Hard:
- no architecture language;
- no “this compounds later” justification.

If the scenes themselves are not desirable:
> DROP.

# 11. Missing: packaging truth / promise alignment

A title can create curiosity by promising a different book.

Need verify:
> title/blurb/cover promise == repeated chapter pleasure.

A one-time clever contrast is not enough.

# 12. Missing: failure corpus / near-miss graveyard

Matched controls must include:
- books in same lane;
- similar packaging;
- similar opening speed;
- but ordinary/weak outcomes.

Question:
> why did they fail despite having the fashionable features?

This protects against cargo-culting current hits.

# 13. Missing: author fit, but only after market proof

Author fit matters:
- willingness to read the genre for months;
- ability to judge scenes;
- tolerance for research;
- comfort with romance / politics / horror / comedy / history.

But:
> author fit must NOT create market proof.

Use it only after:
> market entry is validated.

# 14. Missing: compliance / platform-policy risk

Before serious investment:
- platform content policy;
- sensitive historical/political content;
- explicit sexual content risk;
- gore/horror limits;
- derivative/IP risk.

A commercially attractive concept that is hard to publish is not a valid first-book bet.

# 15. Missing: real-human evidence later

AI can diagnose:
- confusion;
- continuity;
- obvious boredom risk.

But before launch:
> several real target readers should do voluntary stop/continue reading.

AI must not become the final taste oracle again.

# 16. New mandatory sequence

Validated Mother Chassis
-> AUTHOR WANT/MAYBE
-> MARKET ENTRY QUALIFICATION
   - exact reader pool
   - demand + competition
   - trend lifecycle
   - newcomer proof
   - platform route
   - monetization fit
   - failure controls
-> direct hit deep read
-> small concept forge
-> AUTHOR concept ranking
-> 3 must-see scenes
-> micro-prose
-> AUTHOR continue/stop
-> P0
-> only then structural investment.

# One sentence

> **“热门题材”只是准入证；真正值得下注，还要证明这个细分读者池现在有需求、没被挤爆、新人进得去，而且我们有一口别人没有的主菜。**
