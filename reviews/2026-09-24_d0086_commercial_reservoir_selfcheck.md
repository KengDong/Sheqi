## 2. Reward Sameness Audit

### Strongest resistance
- **CR3:** rewards naturally diversify into version war, propagation, battle adaptation, organization, standard-setting, user conflict, and old-version reuse.
- **CR4:** growth shifts among cultivation, migration, population, industry, route politics, sacrifice, ecology, and governance.
- **CR2:** physical assets quickly become relationship, legal, operational, security, and identity problems.

### Manageable but visible sameness risk
- **CR1:** repeated scar invocation/combination is a stable grammar; variety survives because healing, relationships, breakthrough design, public status and institutional ethics keep changing the experience.
- **CR8:** “get another building” is a powerful early dopamine loop, but old module/personnel/route combinations can replace new-building acquisition for meaningful stretches.
- **CR5:** “unlock another facility” is the most obvious early trap; the candidate survives only when maintenance, permissions, public cost and old-node reuse become primary story generators.

### Hard warnings
- **CR6:** despite real-world consequences and domain politics, the core asset loop remains strongly “bring another rule / recombine rules.”
- **CR7:** **REWARD SAMENESS FAILURE.** The dominant question remains “what rule gets deleted next?” and escalation itself creates reward inflation.

## 3. Premium Survival Audit

- **CR1:** PASS. Later cultivation still depends on scars as body history, cost, power and ethics.
- **CR2:** STRONG PASS. The better the no-identity infrastructure works, the more sharply normal identity and intimacy matter.
- **CR3:** STRONG PASS. Scale amplifies rather than dilutes “other people really refining my method.”
- **CR4:** STRONG PASS. Larger territory makes mobility, route choice, movable-vs-fixed assets and migration politics more important.
- **CR5:** PASS. Must remain “city as body/public power,” not ordinary ability combat.
- **CR6:** PASS. Reality-domain rules become more central, not less.
- **CR7:** PASS in a dangerous sense: Premium strengthens until it pushes the story toward abstract fundamental laws.
- **CR8:** STRONG PASS if mobility remains mandatory; failure mode is settling into ordinary base management.

No candidate failed because its Premium simply vanished. CR7's problem is the opposite: Premium escalation becomes self-destructive.

## 4. Commercial Compounding Audit

### Very strong
- **CR3:** early tiny user improvements can become high-level standards; user population, branches and distribution nodes remain reusable assets.
- **CR8:** hospital/supermarket/power/repair/bridge modules change one another's value across many later situations.

### Strong
- **CR1:** early scars change healing, battle style, breakthroughs, institutions and later heavenly tribulations.
- **CR2:** keys/cash/processes/team independence reappear as later security, ownership, community and governance problems.
- **CR4:** early branch veins, mobile industries and route knowledge become high-level resilience assets.
- **CR5:** old maintenance knowledge, low-tech nodes and early district work can become city-level resilience.

### Strong but complexity-limited
- **CR6:** old rules genuinely recombine and can scale to domain politics, but the state space becomes cognitively expensive.
- **CR7:** old exemptions persist, but later fundamental exemptions threaten to overshadow early fruits.

## 5. Case-Supply Dependency Audit

- **LOW:** CR1, CR2, CR3
- **LOW–MEDIUM:** CR4
- **MEDIUM:** CR5, CR8
- **MEDIUM–HIGH:** CR6
- **HIGH:** CR7

Key distinction:
- CR5/CR8 *can* keep going with old infrastructure/assets if the author resists novelty collecting.
- CR6/CR7 structurally need continuing rule-field supply to deliver the signature reward loop.
- CR7 additionally needs the new deletable rules to keep escalating, which drives abstraction.

## 6. No-Rescue Check

No candidate received:
- a new hidden system;
- a replacement protagonist power;
- a terminal conspiracy;
- a new universal organization;
- a rewritten root rule.

Specific restraint evidence:
- CR1 never adds new source tribulations; all late situations reuse existing scars plus the protagonist's own normal tribulations.
- CR2 never fixes recognition; infrastructure only routes around identity dependence.
- CR3 never adds a direct “crowd XP” system; only verified real practice feeds method evolution.
- CR4 never converts the vein into a pet/system panel.
- CR5 does not grant unrelated personal superpowers; minimal field survival is treated only as ordinary operational competence.
- CR6 does not add a rule-deletion or authoring system; the asset remains imported rules.
- CR7 is allowed to fail naturally rather than being saved by arbitrary deletion restrictions invented in this phase.
- CR8 does not add a second crafting/game system; growth remains building cores + people + base operation.

## 7. Hard Findings For editor_in_chief

Without making a cut:
- CR3 and CR4 show the cleanest evidence that **early commercial fruit naturally becomes later story infrastructure**.
- CR1 and CR2 show strong Premium-preserving compounding without relying on endless external object supply.
- CR5 remains commercially vivid but its natural variety thins earlier than the top reservoirs unless maintenance/public-power stories carry real weight.
- CR8 proves a meaningful non-collection second gear, but the new-building temptation remains structural.
- CR6 retains Premium and compounding, yet has a real **new-rule supply + memory-load** problem.
- CR7 hits a genuine structural wall at 24: **reward sameness, case-supply dependence, and escalation-to-abstraction converge.**

These are diagnostics only. The next authority is editor_in_chief / Commercial Reservoir Cut.
