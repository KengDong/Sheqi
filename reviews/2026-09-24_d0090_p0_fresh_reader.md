# Phase A｜Absolute Judgment

## X

### 1. Continue next screen?
**YES**

### 2. Exact first hook
The first moment that genuinely pulled me in was not the road-maintenance setup itself, but the instant the black mass crushed the parked e-bike and the woman with the child had nowhere left to retreat. At that point the scene stopped being “storm emergency work” and became an immediate unknown-threat problem.

The stronger lock-in arrived when the first streetlight turned and then all seventeen lights aimed at the black mass. That was the moment I knew I wanted the next beat rather than merely appreciating the scene.

### 3. Interest dip / friction
The weakest stretch for my attention was the early drain-clearing detail: water depth, the clogged drain, the plastic bag / leaves / food box. It is grounded and readable, but before the thing in the water appears I am still waiting for the novel-specific reason to care.

After the black mass appears, attention does not materially drop.

### 4. What am I actually waiting for next?
I am waiting for one concrete answer:

> Why did the seventeen streetlights stop targeting the creature and turn toward Xu Jin?

I also want to know whether the lights are obeying him, identifying him, or treating him as the next threat.

### 5. Person or concept?
**BOTH**, but the immediate pull is still slightly more concept-driven.

Xu Jin earns some personal interest because he is competent under pressure, notices the electrical danger, runs toward the woman and child, and acts before he understands the thing. I am not only following a power reveal. But the strongest reason I click the next screen is still the city-infrastructure anomaly.

### 6. One image remembered without rereading
Seventeen pale streetlights pinning the black shape in the flooded road, then slowly turning past it until every lamp is aimed at Xu Jin.

### 7. One non-mechanism human detail remembered
The little girl grabbing Xu Jin as she runs past and digging her fingernails into the back of his hand.

### 8. AI / constructed feeling
There is no obvious AI-flatness in the prose.

The ending is visibly engineered for a first-screen cliff: creature trapped → lights do not reset → all lights rotate toward protagonist. I can feel the author arranging those beats for maximum visual payoff, especially the one-by-one lamp turn, but it still reads as a deliberate cinematic construction rather than synthetic filler.

## Y

### 1. Continue next screen?
**YES**

### 2. Exact first hook
The first real hook for me came before the supernatural reveal:

> “散修租得起的时候，哪儿都能住；别人一说要用，哪儿都不是你的。”

That made the eviction feel like part of a life rather than a generic opening inconvenience.

The plot hook fully arrived when the measuring nail kept pointing toward Lu Zhaochuan after he moved away from the hill. From there I wanted to know what, exactly, was following him.

### 3. Interest dip / friction
The slowest point is the packing inventory around the water jar, rice bags, shovel, nails, clothes and poor spirit sand. It is useful because it establishes how little he owns, but this is the section where I am least urgently pulled forward.

Once the dry well makes a sound and the nail points toward him, the prose becomes steadily more gripping.

### 4. What am I actually waiting for next?
I am waiting to see where Lu Zhaochuan can physically take this departing spirit vein, and what happens when the people who just claimed the hill realize the supposedly worthless vein has crossed their boundary with him.

A second immediate question is whether the moving vein can actually solve the water / shelter problem for Tao-shen and the child.

### 5. Person or concept?
**BOTH**, with the strongest person-following impulse of the three screens I read.

The concept matters, but I already understand something about Lu Zhaochuan’s habits: he keeps a cracked door board because it can still block rain, plans around an elderly woman’s bad leg, calculates water before distance, and has been displaced often enough to recognize the pattern without dramatizing himself.

### 6. One image remembered without rereading
The thin wet trace crossing the newly planted “青鹤门封地” sign and continuing after the wheelbarrow.

### 7. One non-mechanism human detail remembered
Lu Zhaochuan saving the broken door board because, at the next place, it can be propped between two trees so they do not have to sleep directly under the open sky.

### 8. AI / constructed feeling
No obvious AI-mechanical feeling.

The line about places successively “belonging” to Zhao, Zhou, and then someone else is clearly thematic writing, but it feels earned by his repeated displacement rather than inserted as a slogan.

The final “把锅也带上 / 我们不去找井了” is a designed closing beat, but it grows naturally out of the water problem established at the start.

## Z

### 1. Continue next screen?
**YES**

### 2. Exact first hook
The first strong hook is the doctor’s answer that medicine alone is not enough:

> she still needs oxygen, suction, monitoring and preferably an operating table — what can he carry away?

That instantly reframes the looting scene.

The commercial hook lands completely at:

> “你拿你的。”  
> “我要这栋楼。”

From that line onward I know exactly what fantasy this screen is selling, and the prose pays it off immediately instead of postponing it.

### 3. Interest dip / friction
There is almost no pure pacing dip.

The main friction is different: several pieces arrive with visible functional precision. A bystander conveniently names the “楼核”; the vehicle already has an empty fixed mount; the doctor has just listed the exact hospital functions that then appear in the transformed cabin. I remain engaged, but I can see the construction underneath the scene more clearly than in Y.

### 4. What am I actually waiting for next?
First, I want to know whether Lao Zhou survives now that the operating environment exists.

Immediately after that, I am waiting for the next concrete rule of the mobile base:

> If a hospital core can turn the old engineering cabin into a functioning medical space, what other parts of a building can Chen Yi take, and what is the cost / limit of carrying them?

I also expect the people outside, who just watched the transformation, to stop treating the core as useless compared with medicine.

### 5. Person or concept?
**MOSTLY CONCEPT**.

Chen Yi is decisive and his choice is tied to saving Lao Zhou, so he is not empty. But after one screen I know the fantasy and operating logic much more vividly than I know Chen Yi’s personality outside crisis execution.

### 6. One image remembered without rereading
White hospital tiles growing out across the battered tool cabin while oxygen ports pop from the wall and a surgical lamp lowers at the far end.

### 7. One non-mechanism human detail remembered
To fit the wounded Lao Zhou into the engineering vehicle earlier, they had already thrown away half a truckload of steel cable.

### 8. AI / constructed feeling
There is no broad AI-prose feeling, but this screen has the strongest “designed demonstration” sensation.

The doctor’s equipment list, the instant public naming of the core, the empty mounting seat, and the transformation producing exactly the facilities just requested all align very cleanly. It is satisfying, but I am most aware here that the first screen is proving a product promise.

That does not stop me from continuing.

# Phase B｜Relative Choice

## 1. If I can continue only ONE next screen
**Z**

Reason: it gives the clearest immediate transaction between problem, protagonist choice, unusual resource, and payoff. I understand the fantasy without needing explanation, and the screen ends with both a human stake (the wounded man) and an expandable question (what else can be taken / carried).

## 2. If I can keep TWO
**Z + Y**

Z stays for the strongest immediate commercial propulsion.

Y stays because it creates the strongest combined desire to follow both the unusual premise and the protagonist’s actual life.

I would still voluntarily continue X in isolation, but if forced to cut to two, X is the one I drop.

## 3. Strongest immediate commercial promise in actual prose
**Z**

The prose does not merely announce a premise; it demonstrates it within the screen:

people fight over portable medicine → Chen Yi wants the building-level resource → the old vehicle becomes a usable medical space → the injured companion can now be treated.

The promise is unusually legible after one screen.

## 4. Strongest desire to know the protagonist as a person
**Y**

I want to know what kind of life Lu Zhaochuan builds when his pattern has been “keep moving because nothing is really yours,” and now the land itself begins moving with him.

His treatment of objects, distance, water, Tao-shen and the child gives me more personal texture than the other two screens.

## 5. Clearest risk that the first-screen trick may be better than the novel
**X**

The final image is extremely effective, but after this screen I have the least visibility into the durable life / relationship / progression engine behind that image.

My next click is strongly secured by one question — why the lights turned toward him — which means the first-screen cliff itself is doing a large share of the work.

## 6. Most clearly remembered after a short mental reset
**X**

The image that comes back first is not a sentence or mechanism label. It is the flooded street with the black shape pinned by seventeen lamps, followed by every lamp turning toward Xu Jin.

That is the most singular visual memory of the three.
