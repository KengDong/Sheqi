**YES**

The promised practical problem was actually paid off.

**RISE**

The warehouse emergency is sufficiently settled that I do not need another warehouse beat immediately. The ending shifts my attention to the harder ongoing problem: how this man actually lives when the world, including his wife, no longer connects him to his identity.

The final move from “prove who I am” to “build things that still work when nobody knows who I am” gives the next chapter a forward direction rather than only preserving the original mystery.

### 9. Clearest repetition risk

The clearest long-term risk is a repeated cycle of:

**an identity-dependent system fails -> a previously prepared offline / redundant asset is revealed -> that asset solves the immediate problem -> the value of preparedness is demonstrated again.**

In this arc the sequence stays engaging because the wife, workers, business losses, and warehouse emergency create human consequences around it. But the pattern itself becomes visible by the later half of the arc.

### 10. What remains in memory without rereading?

The strongest remembered image is:

**everyone else is bidding desperately for refrigerated storage during the outage, while the small aluminum “017” tag opens twelve pallet positions that were paid for a year earlier.**

The opening image of his wife holding a fruit knife on him is also very sticky, but the 017 payoff is the image that best captures what this arc became.

That feels designed rather than accidental, but it does not make me stop reading because the wife relationship, the staff reactions, and the possibility of actual business loss keep the sequence attached to people and consequences rather than reading like a pure feature demonstration.
