# 1. Why No Cut

The Character Gate was designed to detect:
- protagonist satellites;
- gratitude-NPCs;
- no offscreen agency;
- no horizontal relationships;
- no leverage over protagonist;
- relationship reset;
- ordinary chapters requiring fresh novelty.

None of the four ACTIVE candidates showed a structural zero on those axes.

All four produced:
- 1 protagonist with a non-gimmick behavior engine;
- 4 recurring core people;
- at least 3 horizontal non-protagonist links;
- 12 old-state Character Collision events;
- 3 protagonist-absent simulations;
- 5 ordinary-chapter tests;
- operational relationship memory.

Therefore a forced 4 -> 3 cut would be quota-driven rather than evidence-driven.

# 2. Evidence Weight Warning

Character Collision is still:
> **design evidence, not prose evidence.**

It proves:
> a plausible relationship engine can be constructed.

It does NOT yet prove:
- the characters feel alive on the page;
- voices are distinct in continuous prose;
- chemistry is fun rather than mechanically correct;
- readers remember or miss these people;
- the protagonist is actually magnetic.

Therefore:
> do not crown based on Character Collision quality.

P0 / P1 must test whether the designed relationship engine survives prose.

# 3. Candidate Verdicts

## A01｜ACTIVE STRONG

Character design successfully converts:
> tenants / maintenance / regulation / capital

from management units into actors with independent leverage.

Strongest proof:
> protagonist success increases other people's bargaining power.

Carry-forward watch:
- do not turn every scene into negotiation;
- do not make protagonist the universal responsible adult;
- provisional cast must feel like people, not governance functions.

## A02｜ACTIVE STRONG

Character design avoids:
> smart engineer vs stupid residents.

Conflicts can arise from:
- dignity;
- property;
- medical need;
- labor rights;
- fairness;
- privacy;
- family priorities.

Strongest proof:
> technical correctness can lose socially without anyone being stupid.

Carry-forward watch:
- prose must not become policy meeting / engineering explanation;
- protagonist competence needs personality and appetite, not only responsibility.

## A04｜ACTIVE STRONG

Character design avoids:
> rescue -> gratitude -> ally.

Survivors and teammates can:
- deny;
- resent;
- protect reputation;
- pursue career;
- disagree professionally;
- act without protagonist.

Strongest proof:
> old incidents return through people, not merely maps.

Carry-forward watch:
- rescue scenes must not become procedural templates;
- emotional pressure must not rely on one tragic speech per incident.

## B10｜ACTIVE BUT WARNING

Character design successfully creates:
- fixed repairer;
- fixed teams;
- supplier relationships;
- low-budget buyer network;
- bypass/credit/bargaining routes.

This meaningfully reduces:
> one new customer = one new transaction card.

However the visible action grammar still naturally compresses toward:
> inspect / repair / value / trade.

This is now a prose-risk, not a design-risk.

Carry-forward watch:
> if P0/P1 still feels like “合理经营者处理一单生意”, demote quickly rather than adding stranger equipment.

# 4. False-Negative Check

Do NOT demote A04 merely because it was not a Shelf Top-3 leader:
> its strongest axis is scene tension / human stakes, which Shelf under-measures.

Do NOT demote B10 merely because its action grammar is riskier:
> Character Collision showed real fixed-network supply.

Do NOT promote A01 to Primary merely because it currently leads packaging:
> its character engine is plausible but still untested in prose.
