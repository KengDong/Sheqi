# 2026-09-27｜Ch01 Writing Process Retrospective After Author Feedback

status: APPROVED PROCESS LEARNING
scope:
> chapter-prose workflow / anti-regression
> not a declaration that V16 prose is approved

## 1. Core failure was process, not isolated wording

The recurring problem across V10–V16 was not simply:
- one bad joke;
- one slow action;
- one weak opening;
- one short dialogue.

The deeper failure:
> the writing process repeatedly optimized local craft dimensions before asking whether the whole chapter still read like a natural, finished novel.

This produced local improvements that damaged global reading:
- clearer mechanics but slower camera;
- more “personality” but more visible author intention;
- more jokes but less event comedy;
- more payoff setup but weaker surprise;
- tighter dialogue rules but increasingly engineered dialogue;
- stronger explanation but less fiction.

Future work must treat this as a system-level production failure.

---

## 2. “Author intention showing” is a major red flag

Example failure pattern:
> “程野刷到这张单时，先看的其实是三百二。”

Why it fails:
- it exists mainly to tell Reader that the author is intentionally withholding the 800;
- it announces the design move instead of letting the scene naturally produce it;
- it creates “此地无银三百两” effect;
- Reader notices construction before story.

General rule:
> if a sentence exists mainly to protect a later payoff, hide information, prove a character trait, or signal a setup, it must still be something the character would naturally think/do in that moment.

Test:
> If the author’s design goal were removed, would this sentence still exist?

If no:
> rewrite the scene, not the sentence.

---

## 3. Do not pre-spend surprise

The +800 reward failed in earlier drafts because Cheng Ye:
- saw it too early;
- mentally budgeted it;
- planned behavior around it;
- treated it as part of expected income.

That converted:
> unexpected gain

into:
> deferred payment.

General rule:
> surprise payoffs must remain structurally unavailable to both protagonist expectation and reader expectation until the story earns them.

Do not “hide by mentioning lightly.”
If the reward is meant to surprise:
> let the chapter work without foregrounding it.

---

## 4. “Funny sentence” is not “funny fiction”

Failure examples:
- 怕自己洒;
- 飞剑没学现代教材;
- 私教费改善教练生活;
- 老毛病适应环境不错.

These can sound clever in isolation, but:
> deleting them does not alter the scene.

The stronger target learned from successful light-xianxia:
> setup fact -> sincere sideways interpretation -> actual action -> consequence -> callback / reversal.

Humor should occupy plot state.

“十一单半” worked better because:
- it came from a real past event;
- it changed how Cheng Ye thinks about platform classification;
- it can influence later behavior;
- it reveals work history and personality simultaneously.

Hard rule:
> before keeping a humorous beat, ask what changes because this happened.

If answer is “nothing”:
> it is decoration, not event comedy.

---

## 5. Cheng Ye must be recognizable through behavior, not adjectives

Repeated weak state:
> normal / smart / practical / light / resource-aware.

These traits are too generic.

Reader should recognize Cheng Ye through recurring behavior:
- he notices concrete constraints;
- he reads rules literally but not maliciously;
- he tries valid sideways routes;
- he is willing to test on himself;
- he stores useful residue instead of demanding full certainty;
- he can be loose in ordinary moments;
- when danger becomes real, humor drops.

Important lesson:
> personality cannot be added by inserting “Cheng Ye-like” observations every few paragraphs.

It must affect:
- what he notices;
- what he ignores;
- what he asks;
- what he actually does;
- what consequence he accepts.

Name-removal test:
> if “程野” is removed from a paragraph, can Reader still identify who would behave that way?

---

## 6. Stop over-shooting action

Author feedback:
> some process description feels like a Douyin shot staying too long.

Recurring failure:
- first exchange / second exchange / third exchange;
- repeated foot / shoulder / qi / replay detail;
- ancient scene split into many micro-shots;
- archive reveal delivered card by card.

Why it fails:
> Reader understood the functional result earlier than the camera moved on.

Target:
> explain only until Reader can predict the next causal step.

Then move.

Action description should prioritize:
1. what changed;
2. why it mattered;
3. what choice it caused.

Not:
> complete physical recording of every intermediate step.

Camera test:
> Has Reader already understood the action’s function?
If yes, leave the shot.

---

## 7. But do not confuse “faster” with “shorter”

Past correction created another failure:
> subtitle-card prose.

Short fragments:
- one-word dialogue;
- one-sentence beats;
- micro-paragraph suspense;
- “问题出来了。”
- “他看懂了。”
- “雨。”
- “谁？”

These imitate speed visually but often reduce narrative flow.

Target:
> fewer narrative units with more causal content.

One paragraph should often carry:
> stimulus -> interpretation -> choice/consequence.

Hard dialogue gate remains:
- no ordinary 1–4-character standalone replies;
- no ping-pong shorthand;
- brevity only when pressure itself justifies it.

---

## 8. Dialogue must belong to actors, not to the outline

Recurring problem:
> supporting characters speak because the writer needs to explain or transition.

Zhou Hang:
- is a paying buyer;
- cares about his own test and report;
- should not diagnose Cheng Ye;
- should not become protagonist assistant.

Coach Shao:
- cares about valid data and safety;
- can interpret traces;
- should not become lore delivery / mentor voice.

Dialogue test:
> If Reader were not listening, would this person still say this now for their own goal?

If no:
> dialogue is functional machinery.

---

## 9. Whole chapter before local prose

The biggest repeated production mistake:
> polishing a local paragraph before re-evaluating chapter-level causality and reading experience.

Mandatory future order:

### Pass 0｜Whole chapter intent
Define:
- Reader promise;
- emotional progression;
- protagonist decision chain;
- major payoff;
- final question.

### Pass 1｜Scene duration
For every scene:
- what state changes?
- how long does this deserve?
- what can be skipped?

### Pass 2｜Character continuity
Check Cheng Ye from first paragraph to last:
- same person?
- behavior causes plot?
- does humor come from decisions?

### Pass 3｜Event comedy
Map:
- setup;
- interpretation;
- action;
- consequence;
- callback.

### Pass 4｜Information release
Check:
- what is known too early?
- what surprise has been pre-spent?
- what explanation should occur only after payoff?

### Pass 5｜Prose
Only now write sentences.

### Pass 6｜Line audit
Every paragraph:
- necessary?
- person-specific?
- advances?
- camera overstays?
- author intention exposed?
- dialogue sounds human?
- explanation replacing scene?

### Pass 7｜External benchmark
Have an isolated editor judge the finished chapter as a whole against real finished/popular fiction.

Do not let the writer self-certify.

---

## 10. Do not over-fragment review workflow either

Another process mistake:
> reacting to the writing problem by splitting review into too many specialist windows.

Author correction:
> one independent holistic benchmark editor is preferable.

Reason:
- too many specialists recreate local optimization;
- synthesis becomes another mechanical merge problem;
- whole-reading feel is lost.

Preferred structure:
1. writer;
2. one independent holistic benchmark editor;
3. one rewrite based on ranked P0/P1/P2 findings.

Only split specialist windows if a genuinely isolated research question cannot be answered holistically.

---

## 11. Benchmark learning must be functional, not cosmetic

When studying 《谁让他修仙的！》 or other strong novels, do NOT extract:
- sentence quirks;
- joke count;
- paragraph length alone;
- isolated “style tricks.”

Extract:
- how a funny event begins;
- when characters act on mistaken/sideways logic;
- where camera moves on;
- what information is withheld;
- what ordinary actions establish character;
- how consequences return later.

Benchmark question:
> What did the author choose NOT to explain or show?

This is as important as what they did show.

---

## 12. New hard anti-regression questions

Before any future Ch1 rewrite is shown, answer YES to all:

1. Does the opening work even if later payoff mechanics are hidden?
2. Is any surprise reward being mentally pre-spent?
3. Can at least 3 Cheng Ye behaviors be recognized without his name?
4. Does at least one humorous event change later action?
5. Can every supporting-character line be justified by that character’s own goal?
6. Is any action still being shown after Reader already understands the result?
7. Are there any “clever” sentences that exist only to sound clever?
8. Are there any short dialogue/card rhythms used to fake speed?
9. Does the ancient overlap arrive through action rather than explanation?
10. Does the chapter end with a stronger concrete question than it began with?
11. Has a separate holistic editor read the whole chapter without prior-draft contamination?

If any answer is NO:
> do not deliver.
