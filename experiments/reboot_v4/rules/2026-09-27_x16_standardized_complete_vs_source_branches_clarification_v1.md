# Core correction

Avoid contradiction:
> "modern version is complete"
while
> "ancient person shows an unknown missing move."

New rule:
> modern public technique can be fully mature / complete AS A STANDARDIZED SYSTEM,
> while still omitting source-era experimental branches, niche transitions, body-specific variants, and historically abandoned routes.

"Complete" != "contains every route ever explored."

# 1. Three layers of a technique

## Layer 1｜source logic
The creator's deepest question:
- why this movement exists;
- what force / meridian / body problem it solves;
- what tradeoff each transition makes.

This can be richer than modern textbooks.

## Layer 2｜source branches
During creation there may be:
- experimental variants;
- body-specific routes;
- high-risk high-control transitions;
- terrain / weapon / combat-specific branches;
- branches later discarded for mass adoption.

Many never enter modern standard teaching.

## Layer 3｜modern standardized complete edition
Centuries of development produce a mature version optimized for:
- safety;
- teachability;
- broad compatibility;
- measurable performance;
- certification;
- mass education.

It is genuinely "complete" for its design goal.

But standardization necessarily compresses / removes:
- rare branches;
- personalized paths;
- dangerous edge cases;
- founder-only experiments.

# 2. Opening implementation

The protagonist is using:
> a mature standardized movement system.

His problem:
> one transition is safe and correct,
but poorly matched to his individual rhythm / body.

During source bleed he sees:
> the young source-era practitioner use an experimental transition branch.

It is NOT a "future move the modern world somehow forgot existed entirely."

Instead:
> it belongs unmistakably to the same technique family,
but takes a route no current registered teaching branch uses.

MC copies it and it happens to fit him.

# 3. Platform response

Do NOT display:
> "Unknown technique. No match."

Better:
> "Technique lineage match: 92% — [standard movement family]."
> "Transition path: unregistered."
> "Historical-branch similarity: low-confidence / archival trace detected."

This tells reader:
- same technique;
- strange execution path;
- source/history clue.

Human coach may say:
> "架子是XX步法，但这一转不是现行教学版。"

This is cleaner and more believable.

# 4. Reciprocal value in first ancient contact

Protagonist can show:
> the mature standardized future edition.

Young creator gains:
- proof that his road survives;
- a view of the final mass-adoption form;
- centuries of downstream optimization / injury lessons.

Young creator can show:
> source reasoning + experimental branches + original constraints.

Protagonist gains:
- why the modern standard makes its compromises;
- a rare branch better suited to him;
- embodied creation logic not present in mass curriculum.

Thus neither side "has the complete answer."

Future side:
> broad maturity / accumulated outcomes.

Source side:
> causal understanding / variant richness / context.

# 5. Why a branch might disappear without being inferior

Possible reasons:
1. requires unusually fine control;
2. higher injury rate;
3. not suitable for standardized testing;
4. only useful under certain terrain / weapon conditions;
5. poor compatibility with most body types;
6. difficult to teach at scale;
7. later institutions chose another branch for simplicity / safety;
8. creator intentionally did not publish it.

A discarded branch can still be:
> better for one person / one scenario,
without being universally superior.

# 6. Ch1 wording correction

Old problematic idea:
> "platform cannot identify the move at all."

Replace with:
> "platform recognizes the parent technique but cannot classify the transition route."

This avoids:
- modern ignorance;
- ancient-is-always-better trope;
- contradiction with mature cyber-cultivation civilization.

# 7. Ch2 ancient shock correction

The ancient creator should be shocked by:
> the protagonist's mature FUTURE STANDARD FORM,
not because the protagonist knows one secret ancient move.

Meaning:
- ancient gives protagonist source branch;
- protagonist gives ancient future final form.

They are different values.

# 8. Long-term principle

For later anchors:
> ancient source is not automatically "better" than modern maturity.

Modern civilization may be better at:
- standardization;
- safety;
- diagnostics;
- mass teaching;
- statistical failure analysis.

Ancient/source era may be better at:
- original intent;
- special branches;
- missing context;
- direct creator experience;
- lost local conditions.

The book's pleasure comes from:
> asymmetric knowledge,
not "ancient good / modern stupid" or the reverse.
