# 1. Recommended underlying institution｜仙史实境

Modern mass-cultivation civilization uses **仙史实境** as a real educational / cultivation infrastructure.

It is built from:
- historical records;
- preserved Dao traces;
- relic data;
- descendant records;
- institutional archives.

Normal uses:
- historical combat training;
- technique origin study;
- decision reenactment;
- lineage / professional education.

Important:
> students are NOT literally expected to enter the past.
> society believes they enter high-fidelity historical reconstructions.

This lets the system exist naturally independent of protagonist.

# 2. Why famous figures are common / fixed

Historical simulation quality depends on:
1. record completeness;
2. number of surviving relics;
3. Dao-trace intensity;
4. cross-source agreement;
5. descendant / institutional archives;
6. event importance.

Therefore famous cultivators and famous events naturally produce:
> high-grade, stable, repeatable simulations.

Unknown people naturally produce:
> fragmented, noisy, low-confidence simulations.

No artificial game logic required.

# 3. Tier system

Working public grades:
> S / A / B / C / D / E

Not "combat power".

Grade means:
> **historical reconstruction value / confidence / training yield.**

## S
- major historical figure / event;
- extremely complete records;
- high-fidelity technique / combat reconstruction;
- expensive / competitive access;
- scenario highly stable.

## A/B
- important lineage / regional figures;
- good technique / event data;
- reliable educational return.

## C
- useful but incomplete local histories;
- some missing causal links.

## D/E
- obscure names;
- uncertain identities;
- broken records;
- missing endings;
- low known technique yield;
- high simulation noise.

Normal students rationally prefer higher grade.

# 4. Protagonist's inversion

The public system values:
> completeness / stability.

Protagonist's abnormal access values:
> **causal openness.**

He discovers:

> **the more incomplete the historical record, the easier his simulation interface can "fall through" the reconstruction and connect to the real living source in the upstream world.**

Therefore:

> high-grade history = better simulation, worse causal leverage.
> low-grade history = worse simulation, but potentially LIVE and changeable.

This is a structural underpricing, not "everyone is stupid".

Punch line:
> **别人抢S级，因为S级最完整。**
> **我抢E级，因为只有E级还没被历史写死。**

# 5. Random vs fixed｜best model

Reject:
- every session random NPC;
- protagonist freely chooses any famous historical person.

Use:

## Step 1｜semi-random low-tier entry
At first protagonist can only enter:
> low-grade 史缺 pool available to his school / access level.

The first person is partly accidental.

This preserves discovery.

## Step 2｜causal anchor
Once:
- protagonist returns to same node;
- both remember each other;
- one real decision has changed history;

that living person becomes:
> **私史锚 / persistent causal anchor**.

Protagonist can target him again.

## Step 3｜causal graph expansion
New accessible nodes are not random.

They unlock through:
- people he meets;
- places he enters;
- historical events he touches;
- letters / rumors / organizations;
- changed records.

Therefore:
> first random discovery → stable recurring cast → expanding historical network.

# 6. How ancient people believe him

Never:
> protagonist says "I am from the future" and they believe.

Use a four-stage proof ladder.

## Trust 0｜treat as anomaly
Ancient person sees protagonist's temporary historical projection / strange appearance.
He assumes:
- spirit;
- hidden technique;
- enemy;
- illusion;
- unknown cultivator.

No belief required.

## Trust 1｜near-term falsifiable prediction
Protagonist knows a documented event around the node:
- tomorrow's fire;
- an ambush;
- a market closure;
- a named person's visit;
- weather / monster tide / sect decision.

Ancient person can verify it within hours / days.

## Trust 2｜protagonist admits the record is incomplete
Ancient person asks a local secret.

Modern record does not know.

Protagonist says:
> "我不知道."

This is important.

He proves:
> not omniscient,
> but has access to a strange downstream record.

## Trust 3｜history changes after ancient person's own choice
The ancient person deliberately deviates.

Next contact protagonist brings:
> the NEW downstream result.

Now both know:
> information responds to his real choices.

Trust becomes rational cooperation,
not faith.

# 7. Communication interface

Modern student enters through:
> a temporary 史身 / historical projection.

Rules:
- can speak;
- can observe;
- limited physical interaction;
- cannot carry modern matter backward;
- cannot directly take ancient matter forward;
- session length / access costs exist;
- if disconnected, ancient world continues.

This makes:
> people / decisions / history
the core resource, not inventory teleportation.

Later upgrades may permit:
- short written "史笺";
- intentional summons from established anchors;
but not early.

# 8. Resource engine｜逆向遗产

Ancient side does not hand an item through a portal.

If someone wants to repay protagonist:
> he leaves it in history.

Possible returns:
- seals an item;
- plants / cultivates something;
- writes a manual;
- leaves a password;
- creates a right / promise;
- founds a lineage;
- orders descendants to recognize a token / phrase;
- preserves a location.

Time carries it forward.

This produces:
> **historical compound interest.**

Small ancient action can mature across centuries.

Strong line:
> **别人跨界倒货，我让历史替我存三百年利息。**

# 9. Option A｜史缺复利
label:
> strongest balance of logic / low-value-high-return / repeat pleasure

## Core fantasy
> Everyone competes for S-grade immortal history because it gives the safest, strongest standard training.
> Protagonist deliberately enters E-grade broken history because those are the only histories he can still change.

## Ch1
- class chooses practical history.
- high-grade slots are competitive / expensive.
- protagonist ends up with an E-grade broken node.
- meets a real obscure cultivator.

## Ch2
- checks records: the man dies soon.
- gives one falsifiable warning.
- ancient man partially believes after verification.

## Ch3
- ancient man survives by making his OWN choice.
- modern record updates.
- the E-grade node's completeness / public value rises slightly.
- one new piece of usable historical knowledge / legacy appears.

Immediate reader desire:
> what happens if another worthless historical fragment is changed?

## Ch4–10
- protagonist exploits the first new legacy / double-version knowledge in current school / cultivation life.
- gets a small formal revaluation.
- returns to same anchor rather than random new person.

## Ch10–20
- through the first anchor, unlocks a second low-grade person in the same historical event web.
- changing the second node unexpectedly alters the first man's new future.
- first true tradeoff:
  > cannot optimize every life simultaneously.

## Ch20 payoff
- one E-grade node rises into a meaningful C/B historical entry.
- modern world now recognizes a figure / technique / event that previously barely existed.
- protagonist holds one exclusive reverse inheritance / access right.
- recurring ancient cast is established.

## Ch100 fantasy
> the original E-grade nobodies now sit inside the origin story of a modern lineage / school / technique family.

Protagonist in present:
> still socially climbing.

Reader knows:
> part of the institution above him exists because of his hidden past interventions.

## Benchmark functions
- 九种魔药: low-value input → qualitatively different unknown payoff / next-slot desire.
- 天才俱乐部: changed action invalidates old future info.
- 没钱修什么仙: gain must become formal modern social access.
- 我有一个修仙世界: both sides accumulate relations / institutions.
- 仙道天骄: immediate cross-world revaluation without copying identity inversion.

## Main risk
> "historical investing" can become spreadsheet optimization.

Guard:
- human choices dominate;
- no visible future-value score;
- protagonist often misjudges because records omit what ancient people care about.

# 10. Option B｜祖师未成名网络
label:
> strongest identity contrast / relationship payoff

## Core fantasy
> The protagonist does not hunt random fragments.
> He keeps meeting people who modern history barely notices at first.
> His interventions gradually connect them.
> Centuries later they are the founders / legends modern society worships.

Working line:
> **别人刷仙史，是拜祖师。**
> **我刷仙史，认识的是祖师还没成为祖师的时候。**

## Access
Still uses low-grade nodes,
but selection is relationship-driven after first contact.

## Early cast
Keep 3–5 recurring figures:
- future sword founder;
- future alchemy reformer;
- future institutional founder;
but NONE are famous at first.

Do not reveal their future titles immediately.

## Identity contrast
Present:
> ordinary student with low institutional access.

Historical hidden identity:
> friend / adviser / rival / benefactor / critic remembered by several future founders.

Modern historians later give the recurring unknown figure a placeholder name:
> 史外客 / 无名先生 / other working title.

## First major payoff
Modern class visits a major institution.

Its founder's record contains a line the protagonist personally said in a low-grade session.

Nobody knows who the "unknown visitor" was.

Reader does.

## Ch100
A current top institution partly exists because of an ancient relationship chain protagonist helped create.

He stands outside its gate as an ordinary applicant / student.

Inside its founding archive:
> his hidden historical identity is treated as a major mystery.

This is the extreme contrast axis.

## Benchmark functions
- 仙道天骄: strong status contrast;
- relationship benchmark: recurring actors / different relationships;
- 我有一个修仙世界: long-run institutional residue.

## Main risk
> "everyone I meet becomes a founder" becomes cheap.

Guard:
- some fail;
- some become important for reasons unrelated to protagonist;
- one may become famous by rejecting protagonist;
- one may remember him negatively.

# 11. Option C｜把E级仙史刷成S级
label:
> strongest Fanqie-style visible progression / official social feedback

## Core fantasy
Modern society publicly grades immortal histories.

Protagonist's unique edge:
> he can turn broken live history into more complete history through real interaction.

He does NOT see hidden future value.

He discovers it by doing the work.

## Public progression
E-grade node
→ D
→ C
→ B
→ A

Grade rises because:
- records become richer;
- later lineage / technique / events survive;
- modern system suddenly has more cross-confirming evidence.

Everyone else thinks:
> protagonist is a freak historical researcher / simulator genius.

Only protagonist knows:
> he is not reconstructing history.
> he is causing it.

## Immediate social reward
Schools can grant:
- 史证积分;
- access credits;
- archive rights;
- cultivation resources;
- recommendation slots;
for genuinely improving historical model completeness.

Thus modern payoff is very fast and measurable.

## Ch1–20
One E history is enough.
Do NOT grind 10.

The first arc:
> low-grade history becomes visibly more valuable while protagonist's own school status rises with it.

## Long fantasy
He becomes known in modern society as:
> the student who can repeatedly "recover" lost immortal history.

Hidden truth:
> he is producing new history.

## Benchmark functions
- 没钱修什么仙: formal metrics → resource reallocation;
- 九种魔药: low input can generate extraordinary output;
- 仙道天骄: current society repeatedly revalues protagonist.

## Main risk
> too game-like / grade-chasing.

Guard:
- grades remain institutional shorthand, not a floating RPG UI;
- relationship / historical consequence matters more than grade itself after early arcs.

# 12. Option D｜仙史复利局
label:
> most ambitious / strongest two-world economic-and-institutional compounding

## Core fantasy
Protagonist realizes:
> the real advantage is not saving a person.
> It is giving small interventions centuries to compound.

Examples:
- a minor training correction becomes a complete technique lineage;
- one planted rare species becomes a protected spiritual grove;
- one informal pact becomes a lasting institution;
- one obscure group becomes a modern sect / company / public system;
- one warning prevents a lineage extinction.

He does not "bring resources back".
He creates:
> **historical compound assets.**

## Key distinction from merchant dual-world
Merchant:
> buy cheap / sell expensive.

Historical compounding:
> make one small causal investment
→ wait centuries automatically
→ retrieve a transformed social / material asset.

## Ch1–3
Same as Option A.

## Ch10
First tiny reverse inheritance matures.

## Ch20
First non-material compounding payoff:
> a promise / lineage / access right exists in modern world.

## Ch50+
Different ancient people compete over what future they want to leave.

Protagonist can no longer maximize:
> money / power only.

Some historical choices produce:
- stronger modern resource;
- worse lives for ancient friends;
or the reverse.

This generates real conflict.

## Ch100
A modern organization with substantial current power traces back to a small ancient decision protagonist participated in.

## Main risk
> civilization-management / historical business plan.

Guard:
- every compound asset must originate in a character conflict / life choice;
- no detached spreadsheet empire building.

# 13. Option E｜史缺会主动叫我
label:
> wild-card / makes other world autonomous

This is not a standalone first premise.
It can upgrade A/B later.

After protagonist has changed several living nodes,
some ancient anchors learn a method to:
> call the strange historical visitor back.

Modern仙史 interface begins showing:
> abnormal "unrequested session" invitations.

Now the other side can initiate:
- requests;
- warnings;
- negotiations;
- accusations.

This flips:
> protagonist刷历史
into:
> history sometimes comes looking for protagonist.

Why useful:
- proves other side agency;
- prevents simulator-vending-machine feel;
- creates urgency.

Use only after early rules are understood.

Not money necessarily.

Changing second person's fate makes first person's new future worse / different.

## Ch17–20｜first network payoff
Two figures meet / cooperate / conflict due prior changes.

Modern:

# 17. Anti-copy / benchmark use

- immediate other-world practical payoff;
- repeated identity / status revaluation.

- modern loser → ancient genius;
- item / resource arbitrage;
- exact school / sect status ladders.

《我有一个修仙世界》
Borrow:
- both worlds accumulate independent people / institutions / resources.

《没钱修什么仙？》
Borrow:
- formal social metrics must change how protagonist is treated.

《九种魔药》
Borrow:
- every new low-grade history is an open slot with unknown output;
- protagonist must actively seek the next one.

《苟在初圣魔门当人材》
Borrow:
- each intervention leaves a changed state; next run cannot be rerun.

《天才俱乐部》
Borrow:
- using downstream information changes the downstream state and forces renewed investigation.
Do not borrow:
- dream / future-city / exact temporal structure.
