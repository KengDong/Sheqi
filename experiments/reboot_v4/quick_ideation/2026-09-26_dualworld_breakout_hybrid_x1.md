# Working hook

> 现代修真学校把“仙史实境”当作训练副本。
> 全班匹配到的都是历史模拟。
> 只有主角匹配到的那个人，会记得他。
>
> 后来主角才发现：
> 对面不是模拟NPC。
> 是三百年前还活着的人。
>
> 更离谱的是：
> 他每改变对方一次，
> 现代的仙史、功法、宗门记录都会跟着更新。

# Why this is one engine, not feature stacking

Modern school needs historical / cultivation simulation for training.

The system is believed to reconstruct:
> historical Dao traces.

The protagonist's session accidentally locks onto:
> the LIVE upstream source instead of a reconstruction.

Therefore:
1. the other person is real;
2. he continues living between sessions;
3. modern records describe his downstream consequences;
4. warning / helping him changes those consequences;
5. modern records therefore update.

The causal loop is unified.

# Ch1–3

Ch1:
- normal school仙史实境 practical.
- protagonist's assigned "NPC" reacts off-script, asks an impossible question, and remembers the protagonist's previous action inside the same session.
- protagonist exits; system marks abnormal behavior.

Ch2:
- protagonist searches the public仙史 database.
- the NPC is a minor historical cultivator.
- record says he dies in several days in a known local incident.
- next session, protagonist warns him.

Ch3:
- the ancient cultivator survives by changing one decision.
- protagonist returns.
- public database has UPDATED:
  - death date gone / changed;
  - a minor cultivation note / technique / relationship now exists that did not exist before.
- only protagonist remembers the old version.
- school simulation content itself also changes.

End question:
> if he survives again, what will my world become?

# Repeat engine

Every cycle:
1. modern side has downstream record / theory / later consequences;
2. ancient side has real local context / people / uncertainty;
3. modern lead sends limited future information;
4. ancient lead chooses what to do;
5. result rewrites the downstream record;
6. old information becomes partly invalid;
7. modern lead must re-investigate.

Thus:
> using the information destroys the information advantage that produced it.

The gap self-refreshes.

# Why a second male lead works

Ancient lead cannot be a tool because:
- he owns the actual choice;
- modern record is not destiny once he knows it;
- saving himself may hurt people modern history never cared about;
- he can reject the "best future" because of local responsibilities;
- he continues living while modern protagonist is absent.

Five-chapter independence is natural:
> modern side investigates changed history / school consequences;
> ancient side deals with the consequences of having changed his fate.

# Why single-protagonist version also works

If dual-protagonist POV feels too risky:
- ancient lead remains a persistent autonomous core actor;
- most POV stays modern;
- his off-session life is discovered through:
  - changed history;
  - next session state;
  - messages / consequences.

Thus dual-lead intensity is tunable.

# Modern-world social payoff

Changes can affect:
- textbook answers;
- technique editions;
- historical reputation;
- school simulation datasets;
- family / sect records;
- modern organizations descended from ancient choices.

Important:
> not every small action rewrites the world.

Propagation should be bounded to:
> causal chains actually touched by the ancient lead.

# Information-gap categories

Early:
- future event vs local context.

Then:
- official history vs what really happened;
- final technique vs why it was invented;
- modern interpretation vs ancient participant memory;
- modern institution's legend vs actual person's motives;
- future "optimal result" vs present moral cost.

This prevents the engine from becoming simple prophecy.

# Why it may exceed the current benchmark geometry

Not a market prediction.

Structurally, compared with a simple two-world revaluation engine, X1 adds:
1. autonomous second actor;
2. downstream reality feedback;
3. information that self-invalidates when used;
4. historical / institutional residue, not only inventory;
5. natural mystery:
   > why is a school simulation connected to a real person?

# Main risks

1. causal paradox complexity;
2. history rewrite becomes omnipotent;
3. modern world changes too much and reader loses footing;
4. save-the-NPC repetition;
5. "simulation" feels gamey.

Needed future logic constraints:
- bounded causal propagation;
- modern protagonist keeps memory of prior versions;
- changes require real causal action, not words alone;
- ancient lead cannot be commanded;
- simulation is an interface, not a game system;
- early changes stay small / personal / local.

No winner selected.
