# Benchmark functions

## 《我正经学生，每天只吃九种魔药》
- one repeatable input gives an immediately distinct result;
- the result creates desire for the next input;
- early loop grows naturally into body / identity / world-scale change.

## 《苟在初圣魔门当人材》
- each failed route produces actionable information / gains;
- limited iteration prevents consequence-free omniscience.

## 《我的化身正在成为最终BOSS》
- multiple persistent identities can have independent social consequences;
- they are not disposable skill cards.

## 《没钱修什么仙？》
- cultivation choices are embedded in education, work, status and future life;
- the protagonist's route matters socially, not only in combat.

## 《原来我是仙道天骄》
- each cross-world interaction must immediately change present options;
- status payoff can be gradual.

# Candidate F｜Future Selves

## Title correction

Original:
> 《我每学一门功法，都会多一个未来的我》

Logic problem:
> learning minor techniques would generate too many future selves and make the mechanic cheap.

Preferred working title:
> **《每选一条仙路，我都会多一个未来的自己》**

Alternative:
> 《我每换一条仙路，未来都会多一个我》

The trigger should be:
> a genuine life-path commitment / 主修仙路,
not merely learning any spell.

## Core world fit

Modern mass-cultivation society forces young cultivators to make costly route choices:
- major / academy;
- root modification;
- 主修法;
- career certificate;
- sect / company track.

A wrong route can waste years.

This makes the future-self mechanic socially meaningful.

## Core rule｜未来回声

When protagonist genuinely commits to a new primary cultivation route,
he can contact:
> **the version of himself who kept walking that route until its first major endpoint / collapse.**

Examples:
- sword route → future sword cultivator;
- alchemy route → future alchemist;
- body route → future body cultivator.

Not every future is successful.

Some:
- become famous;
- become rich;
- cripple themselves;
- lose important relationships;
- die young;
- reach a route bottleneck.

## Why future selves are NOT strategy NPCs

### Rule 1｜They know only their own branch
A future self knows:
> what happened in the future that produced HIM.

He does not know other branches.

### Rule 2｜Once present changes, his information starts expiring
If protagonist follows / rejects advice,
the future branch changes.

The future self becomes:
> less certain,
then may fade or fork.

No infinite stock-market-spoiler behavior.

### Rule 3｜Every future self has self-interest
He wants his own life to remain possible.

A successful sword-self may push:
> abandon alchemy.

An alchemist-self may believe:
> sword path ruined the family.

A future self approaching disappearance may:
> lie / hide information / bargain.

They are characters,
not menus.

### Rule 4｜Borrowing future mastery has a price
Protagonist can temporarily borrow:
> **one embodied mastery state** from a future self.

Examples:
- ten seconds of future sword instinct;
- one furnace's worth of future fire-control;
- future-body version's breathing control.

But every borrow increases:
> **future anchoring**.

The more he borrows from one future:
> the more present body / habits / relationships are pulled toward that branch.

This gives an immediate action payoff AND consequence.

## Ch1–3

### Ch1
At a major route-selection point,
protagonist chooses one ordinary / practical path.

That night:
> an older version of himself appears.

Not a majestic immortal.

Maybe:
> exhausted, scarred, clearly having lived a difficult life.

First line:
> “你真选了这条？”

Immediate hook.

### Ch2
Future self warns about one very near, verifiable problem:
> a training mistake / test / practical choice.

Protagonist refuses to blindly trust him.

During a small real crisis,
he borrows several seconds of future embodied mastery.

It works.

### Ch3
After changing one decision,
future self notices:
> part of his memories have become fuzzy.

This proves:
> future is not fixed.

Then protagonist touches / commits to a second legitimate route or elective path strongly enough to generate:
> **a second future self.**

The two futures react to each other.

Reader's next desire:
> what kind of person did I become on this other road?

## Ch4–20 engine

Do not spawn ten selves.

Keep only 2–3.

The pleasure:
1. present problem;
2. future selves disagree;
3. protagonist borrows one limited mastery / insight;
4. present outcome changes;
5. one or more futures change physically / emotionally;
6. protagonist gains information about who he might become.

By Ch20:
> protagonist discovers that combining knowledge from two mutually exclusive futures creates a present solution neither future ever had.

This is the real uniqueness.

He is not:
> copying the best future.

He is:
> **creating a life no future self remembers.**

## Ch20 social payoff

Other students / cultivators must commit to one specialization.

Protagonist is still officially on one route,
but his practical ability shows impossible cross-route coherence.

A teacher / institution thinks:
> he is a rare interdisciplinary genius.

Reader knows:
> he is building a third path from mutually exclusive lives.

## Ch50+ escalation

New future selves only appear at true life forks:
- academy / sect;
- main Dao;
- body modification;
- major vow;
- career / organization;
- key relationship choice.

Some old futures vanish permanently.

Some merge.

Some become hostile.

A future version might become:
> someone the present protagonist absolutely refuses to become.

## Long-term fantasy

At first:
> ask future me how not to fail.

Then:
> borrow future mastery.

Then:
> combine mutually exclusive futures.

Eventually:
> the protagonist becomes the only version of himself none of the future selves had ever seen.

Core line:
> **别人选一条仙路赌一生，我把每一种可能的自己叫回来，当面问清楚再走。**

## Major collapse risks
- too much exposition / talking heads;
- futures become omniscient cheat sheet;
- too many futures dilute character attachment.

Hard limits:
- maximum 2–3 active early;
- future advice expires when acted upon;
- mastery borrowing has anchoring cost;
- each future has an emotional stake.

# Candidate L｜Living Dao Seed

## Working title
> **《全班绑定道种，我绑定的是异界活人》**

Keep.
It already has:
- normal group behavior;
- protagonist anomaly;
- concrete cultivation object;
- immediate question.

## World rule｜现代道种

In modern mass-cultivation society,
after a basic realm / age threshold,
cultivators bind:
> **道种**.

A Dao seed is a standardized cultivation kernel:
- defines route;
- unlocks techniques / adaptations;
- affects school / job / certification;
- can grow with the user.

Normal Dao seeds are:
> stable, non-personal cultivation templates.

Society treats them like:
> cultivation major + operating system.

## Protagonist anomaly｜活种

Protagonist's seed is rated:
> blank / defective / no known lineage.

After binding:
> someone speaks from the other side.

At exactly the same time,
a young cultivator in a traditional cultivation world discovers:
> a strange seed has appeared inside THEIR Dao foundation.

Both think:
> the other is an inner demon / possession.

The seed is not:
> one master + one student.

It is:
> **one Dao split across two living people.**

## Why this is logical

Modern Dao seeds are made from:
> stabilized cultivation patterns / Dao traces.

The protagonist's seed failed to match any completed stored pattern.

Instead,
for an unknown reason,
it resonated with:
> **a cultivation path that is still alive and unfinished in another world.**

Early characters do not know the cosmological reason.

All they know:
> this is a "live source" instead of a static template.

## Core engine｜共道，不共享修为

Hard rule:
> they do NOT directly share realm / qi / loot.

Otherwise the book becomes:
> "one side levels, the other freeloads."

They share only:
> **verified Dao breakthroughs / structural understanding.**

If either side truly solves a cultivation problem,
the Dao seed records:
> one shared Dao pattern.

The other side receives:
> comprehension access,
but must cultivate / execute it themselves.

This keeps effort and competence.

## Why both worlds matter

Modern protagonist has:
- standardized theory;
- measurement;
- thousands of public examples;
- safe / optimized techniques.

Ancient counterpart has:
- original methods;
- dense natural environment;
- real lineage context;
- dangerous, unsanitized cultivation;
- direct contact with things modern society only knows as theory.

Neither side is simply superior.

## First co-op payoff

They both practice versions of the SAME underlying method.

Modern version:
> safe, stable, weakened / standardized.

Ancient version:
> powerful, incomplete, dangerous.

Neither works perfectly for the current problem.

They talk / observe each other and create:
> **a third version.**

This third version:
- is safer than ancient original;
- stronger / more context-aware than modern standard;
- belongs to neither world before them.

That is the first real "共道" moment.

## Ch1–3

### Ch1｜Binding
Public Dao-seed binding / selection.

Others receive known categories.

Protagonist:
> unknown blank seed / low rating.

Immediate social consequence:
> his expected school / career route drops.

At night:
> “谁？”

Another voice answers:
> “这句话该我问你。”

### Ch2｜Mutual suspicion
They can only communicate during cultivation / meditation.

Both think:
> inner demon / possession / enemy.

They discover one impossible fact:
> they can see each other's cultivation circulation in limited form.

A near-term bottleneck appears for both.

### Ch3｜First co-created move
Modern safe method gives one half.
Ancient original gives the other.

They create a tiny third variant.

Both succeed.

The Dao seed changes from:
> blank / defective
to:
> **未命名活种·第一纹**.

Reader's next desire:
> if we keep solving problems together, what can this seed grow into?

## Ch4–20 relationship engine

The other person must be a genuine second protagonist:
- independent goal;
- independent enemies / family / sect;
- can refuse protagonist;
- sometimes has a better idea;
- sometimes creates trouble.

The pleasure loop:
1. each world creates a different cultivation / life problem;
2. they compare how each world solves it;
3. jointly create a third solution;
4. both must separately execute it;
5. Dao seed grows one shared mark;
6. their relationship changes.

Do NOT introduce a new partner every arc.

First 50 chapters:
> one counterpart is enough.

## Connection progression

The seed itself grows with their co-developed Dao.

Early:
> voice.

Then:
> partial senses / circulation view.

Then:
> short shared vision.

Then:
> one item's **Dao imprint** can be shown across worlds, but not physically traded.

Later:
> temporary projection.

Only after sufficient shared Dao completion:
> first short physical crossing.

This lets the dual-world promise grow instead of spending it instantly.

## Ch20 payoff

They create:
> their first complete technique / mini-path unknown in either world.

Modern side:
> protagonist uses it in a public test / competition and shocks people because it cannot be classified by existing seed categories.

Ancient side:
> counterpart uses the same Dao principle to solve a real sect / survival problem.

Both get a status rise,
but for different reasons.

The reader now follows:
> **two lives and one Dao.**

## Social/status fantasy

Modern:
> F / unknown seed
→ rare evolving live seed
→ founder of a new cultivation branch.

Ancient:
> ordinary / troubled young cultivator
→ creator / co-creator of a path no lineage owns.

Long-term:
> neither is "the other's pet".
They become:
> **the two endpoints of one Dao across two worlds.**

## Why this differs from 《原来我是仙道天骄》

That work's strong geometry:
> low-value modern student → ancient world revalues him as heavenly-root prodigy;
> physical dual-world crossing changes status / resources quickly.

This concept instead uses:
> two living people + one unfinished Dao.

No:
- instant genius revaluation;
- commodity arbitrage;
- ancient world worship;
- direct free cultivation transfer.

The repeatable reward is:
> **co-creation + relationship + two-world public proof.**

## Biggest collapse risks
- becomes long-distance chatroom;
- one side becomes helper NPC;
- shared Dao becomes "you level, I level";
- physical crossing arrives too early and destroys the unique relation.

Hard:
- both have independent plot pressure;
- only verified understanding crosses;
- growth requires execution on both sides;
- physical crossing is an earned later-stage upgrade.

# Comparative design note

These two concepts should feel different.

## Future Selves
Primary fantasy:
> **I can confront the lives I might become, borrow from them, then create a life none of them predicted.**

Main asset:
> self-identity / choice.

## Living Dao Seed
Primary fantasy:
> **Two ordinary cultivators in two different civilizations jointly create a Dao that neither world had.**

Main asset:
> relationship / co-creation.

Do not merge them.
