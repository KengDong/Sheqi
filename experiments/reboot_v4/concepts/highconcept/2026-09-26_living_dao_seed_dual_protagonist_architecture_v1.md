# 1. Shelf promise

Working title:
> **《全班绑定道种，我绑定的是异界活人》**

Core promise:
> everyone else binds a finished cultivation template;
> protagonist binds a living cultivator in another world.

Long fantasy:
> two ordinary young cultivators from completely different cultivation civilizations jointly create a Dao neither world possessed.

# 2. Modern-world Dao seed logic

## 2.1 What a normal Dao seed is

In the modern cultivation civilization,
young cultivators reach a "种道" life stage after basic cultivation.

A normal Dao seed is:
> a compressed, verified cultivation topology.

It contains:
- a core circulation logic;
- compatibility constraints;
- safe growth directions;
- profession / school / certification classifications.

It is not a soul.
It is not sentient.
It is closer to:
> a standardized cultivation operating kernel / major.

This explains why:
- schools care about seed grade;
- employers care about seed category;
- seed selection changes future life.

## 2.2 Why society uses them

Modern cultivation has:
- low natural spiritual density;
- huge population;
- high safety expectations;
- massive databases.

Trial-and-error cultivation is too expensive.

Dao seeds let ordinary people:
> start from routes already proven to work.

The strength of the civilization is:
> standardization.

Its hidden weakness is:
> it increasingly relies on completed answers.

# 3. Why protagonist gets a living person

## 3.1 Modern protagonist's condition

Do NOT copy:
> low artificial root + ancient world heavenly genius.

Working condition:
> protagonist's base aptitude is ordinary, but his Dao compatibility is unusually diffuse.

Meaning:
> he can cultivate,
but no standard seed matches him cleanly.

His options:
- force-bind a mediocre standard seed;
- choose a public low-grade **空白种** designed for self-adaptation.

He chooses / is assigned the blank seed.

This gives low social price without making him physically useless.

## 3.2 Ancient protagonist's condition

Traditional-world protagonist is also NOT a hidden chosen one.

He has:
- usable natural aptitude;
- real combat / survival experience;
- no high-grade lineage;
- a cultivation problem no inherited technique cleanly fits.

He is trying to condense:
> an **unnamed Dao embryo / 无名道胚**.

He has:
> a road but no complete answer.

Modern protagonist has:
> a blank container but no road.

At the same moment,
the two incomplete structures resonate across the "Dao layer".

Result:
> the blank seed anchors to the ancient cultivator's still-living unfinished Dao.

This creates:
> **活种**.

Character causality:
> they are connected because both are trying to walk an unowned road,
not because a god randomly chose two people.

# 4. Long mystery behind the logic

Do NOT explain early.

Working truth:
> modern "Dao-seed mapping" technology reads stable patterns from a metaphysical **Dao Sea / 道层**.

Modern civilization assumes:
> all readable patterns are local historical cultivation data.

Actually:
> Dao patterns can resonate across worlds.

Normal seeds lock onto:
> stable / completed patterns.

The protagonist's blank seed had no predefined lock.

The ancient protagonist's unnamed living Dao had no world-recognized lineage.

They become:
> the first observed live cross-world resonance.

This is enough to explain:
- why normal seeds are static;
- why this one speaks;
- why the pair is rare;
- why later institutions care.

# 5. THE key asymmetry

> **Modern world: many answers, poor conditions.**
> **Ancient world: good conditions, few accessible answers.**

## Modern civilization strengths
- public technique databases;
- diagnostics;
- cultivation medicine;
- statistical failure records;
- simulation / training;
- standardized safe techniques;
- broad low-level education.

Weaknesses:
- low-quality natural spiritual resources;
- expensive high-level materials;
- techniques optimized for safety / efficiency, sometimes losing original extremes;
- credentials / seed grade can lock social mobility.

## Ancient civilization strengths
- dense natural qi;
- wild spiritual phenomena;
- rare natural materials;
- living lineages;
- original versions of techniques;
- real high-pressure combat / survival environments.

Weaknesses:
- knowledge monopoly;
- oral / lineage transmission;
- high mortality;
- poor diagnostics;
- local masters know deep niches but lack broad cross-case data.

Neither side is "backward".

They are strong in different dimensions.

# 6. Information-gap pleasure

Do NOT reduce information gap to:
> modern textbook wins / ancient resource wins.

Six repeatable forms:

## 6.1 Modern failure database vs ancient live experiment
Ancient protagonist encounters a dangerous breakthrough.
Modern protagonist knows:
> thousands of recorded failure patterns.

But records cannot tell exactly what to do under ancient high-qi conditions.

They use:
> modern error map + ancient live sensation
to build a third solution.

## 6.2 Ancient original context vs modern standardized edition
Modern protagonist knows a safe standardized technique.

Ancient protagonist sees:
> why the original move exists under real pressure.

Modern protagonist finally understands:
> the logic that was lost during standardization.

## 6.3 Modern precision vs ancient abundance
Modern side can:
> measure.

Ancient side can:
> actually test in a natural high-energy environment.

One supplies variables.
One supplies the world.

## 6.4 Modern breadth vs ancient depth
Modern protagonist:
> can search / learn many schools' public basics.

Ancient protagonist:
> has deep embodied knowledge of one real tradition.

They correct each other's blind spots.

## 6.5 Social information difference
Modern success requires:
- classification;
- certification;
- reproducibility.

Ancient success requires:
- lineage recognition;
- combat proof;
- oath / reputation.

The same jointly created technique must survive two completely different approval systems.

## 6.6 Risk information difference
Modern protagonist is more cautious because modern society has safety data.

Ancient protagonist has better danger intuition because he actually lives where cultivation kills people.

They will often disagree.

That disagreement is story fuel.

# 7. Core mechanic｜共道, not shared levels

Hard ban:
> ancient protagonist breakthroughs → modern protagonist receives realm for free.

They do NOT share:
- qi;
- realm;
- lifespan;
- physical loot;
- whole memories.

They CAN share:
1. deliberate voice communication during resonance;
2. limited perception of each other's cultivation circulation;
3. verified Dao understanding;
4. later, controlled sensory / projection access.

# 8. What becomes shared｜共道纹

When one side has an idea,
it is NOT automatically written into the seed.

A **共道纹** forms only when:

1. one side proposes / discovers a principle;
2. both understand it;
3. both independently execute / verify it in their own world;
4. the principle survives BOTH environments.

Then the living seed records:
> a cross-world verified Dao pattern.

This makes every shared gain EARNED.

It also creates a powerful fantasy:
> anything both worlds independently prove may be more fundamental than either world's local technique.

# 9. Common trunk + local branches

To avoid the two protagonists becoming copies:

The live seed has:

## Common trunk
Shared:
- principles both have verified;
- 共道纹;
- connection level.

## Modern branch
Only modern protagonist has:
- his local techniques;
- modern body adaptation;
- social certificates / roles;
- modern combat style.

## Ancient branch
Only ancient protagonist has:
- his local lineage;
- ancient body adaptation;
- sect / social ties;
- his own techniques.

Thus:
> same Dao, different people.

They can increasingly resemble philosophical partners
without becoming stat clones.

# 10. Communication rules

## Early
Connection only activates when:
> both enter cultivation / deliberate resonance.

No constant telepathy.

Benefits:
- privacy;
- no cheap omniscient surveillance;
- communication itself requires timing.

## Bandwidth
Early:
> voice / intent only.

Cannot:
- upload a library;
- send videos;
- send files;
- instantly transmit a textbook.

Cultivation concepts that are hard to describe must be:
> demonstrated through later shared circulation perception.

## Translation
The seed translates:
> intent / concept,
not exact vocabulary.

This permits communication while preserving funny / meaningful terminology differences.

## Time
Use approximately:
> 1:1 time flow.

No "sleep one night, ancient side trained 30 years" loophole.

## Relationship
Not:
> master + student.

Not:
> owner + pet.

> system + host.

> two stubborn young men forced to test whether the other's world knows something theirs does not.

# 12. Relationship progression

## Stage 1｜心魔
Both assume:
> the other voice is possession / cultivation deviation.

## Stage 2｜互证
Each provides one small fact the other can verify but could not reasonably know.

Trust:
> low but rational.

## Stage 3｜交易
Simple:
> "你帮我看这个，我帮你试那个."

## Stage 4｜共创
They create first solution neither inherited from their own world.

This is the relationship turning point.

## Stage 5｜分歧
Modern lead prioritizes:
> safety / reproducibility.

Ancient lead prioritizes:
> opportunity / decisive breakthrough.

They have their first real ideological conflict.

## Stage 6｜承担
One accepts a real local cost to protect the other's path.

Now:
> no longer just mutual utility.

## Stage 7｜共道人
They explicitly recognize:
> one Dao, two lives.

Still no subordinate relationship.

# 13. First 20 chapters

## Ch1–3｜活种

Ch1:
- public / school seed binding;
- modern lead obtains unclassified blank seed;
- social future visibly worsens.

Ancient side:
- young cultivator attempts to condense unnamed Dao embryo under pressure.

End:

Ch2:
- mutual suspicion;
- they test whether the other is real;
- discover limited view of each other's cultivation circulation.

Ch3:
- both face a small cultivation bottleneck;
- modern safe method + ancient original practice each lack one piece;
- first tiny joint correction succeeds on BOTH sides.

Seed:
> **未命名活种｜0→1**

Not a realm jump.
A real useful improvement.

## Ch4–7｜证明另一个世界值得继续联系

Modern lead gains:
> practical performance improvement in class / training.

Ancient lead gains:
> survives / succeeds in a local trial or bottleneck.

Both now have external proof.

Introduce:
- one modern recurring classmate / teacher;
- one ancient recurring peer / elder.

## Ch8–12｜first true information-gap win

Ancient lead faces a dangerous technique / body problem.

Modern side:
> has broad failure data but no direct solution.

Modern lead talks him through diagnostics.

Ancient lead adapts using local qi conditions.

They jointly solve it.

Return favor:
Ancient lead later provides embodied combat / qi sense modern simulations lack.

Modern lead uses it in a practical test.

Both win something LOCAL.

## Ch13–16｜first conflict

They disagree on a breakthrough.

Modern:
> data says risk too high.

Ancient:
> if he does not take the chance now, he loses an irreplaceable opportunity.

Neither is stupid.

The resolution:
> they must create a safer but still powerful third route.

This is the first time "共道" is a necessity,
not a cute gimmick.

## Ch17–20｜第一道纹

Both execute the new principle in their own worlds.

It works.

First true 共道纹 forms.

Modern:
> public test cannot classify protagonist's seed / technique.

Ancient:
> local elder notices his Dao is no longer any known lineage.

Both receive:
> first persistent status change.

Ch20 reward:
> connection upgrades from voice to a few seconds of **借眼 / shared sight**.

For the first time,
they SEE the other world.

End beat:
> "原来你那边的天，是这个颜色。"

This is a relationship + world-expansion payoff,
not a cosmic reveal.

This is the first major dual-protagonist relationship test.

## Modern offer

> replace the unknown living seed with a certified high-grade seed.

Benefits:
- scholarship;
- career;
- safe future.

Cost:
> live connection must be cut.

## Ancient offer
A major lineage / elder offers:
> full orthodox inheritance.

Benefits:
- resources;
- status;
- protection.

Cost:
> his unnamed Dao embryo must be erased / rewritten.

Both receive:
> exactly what they originally wanted.

This prevents the story from being:
> "golden finger obviously better, no choice."

They may initially try partial acceptance.

The strain teaches them:
> shared trunk does not require identical local branches.

This formally creates:
> **common Dao trunk + two local paths.**

By Ch100:
> both choose to keep the living Dao,
but not by rejecting their worlds.
They learn to integrate local opportunities without surrendering the common trunk.

# 17. Discovery / exposure engine

Modern side:
> a seed that changes without registered updates is a scientific / regulatory anomaly.

Pressure grows:
- teacher notices;
- lab wants data;
- company / university interest;
- some want protection, some control.

Ancient side:
> techniques appearing without lineage are socially dangerous.

Pressure grows:
- elder asks who taught him;
- rivals suspect secret master;
- major lineages care.

Thus:
> their private relationship creates two public mysteries.

# 18. Information gap should produce SMART scenes

Bad:
> modern guy gives ancient guy Wikipedia.

Good:
> modern guy has 10,000 failure cases but doesn't know which applies;
> ancient guy can feel the live body state but lacks statistical context;
> together they identify the correct risk.

Bad:
> ancient guy sends rare herb, modern guy sells it.

Good:
> ancient guy encounters a natural phenomenon modern society can only simulate;
> modern guy uses the public theoretical model to interpret it;
> ancient guy tests the missing condition;
> both gain a new principle.

# 19. Long-term world logic

The two worlds should NOT be secretly the same timeline by default.

Working model:
> independent cultivation worlds connected through a shared metaphysical Dao layer.

This preserves:
- genuine cultural difference;
- no time-travel paradox;
- no "modern history should know ancient protagonist" contradictions.

Later discovery:
> modern seed technology has unknowingly been reading stable cross-world Dao patterns for generations.

Most patterns are static / completed,
so modern people never realized their deeper source.

The pair is the first:
> live, bidirectional resonance.

This creates a late-stage conflict without invalidating early life:
- can live seeds be replicated?
- should worlds exchange Dao?
- who owns a cultivation principle discovered by two people?
- can institutions monopolize a cross-world path?

# 24. Main collapse risks

1. ancient protagonist becomes helper NPC.
   - fix: independent goal, cast, enemies, status ladder.

2. modern protagonist becomes answer machine.
   - fix: information broad but often insufficient without ancient live context.

3. shared-level freeloading.
   - banned.

4. chatroom pacing.
   - connection only at chosen times; each side has independent action.

5. too many partners.
   - first 100+ chapters preserve the same pair.

6. cross-world commodity trade swallows premise.
   - no matter transfer in early/mid story.

7. "two worlds" doubles exposition.
   - first 20 chapters stay in one modern social circle + one ancient local circle.

8. physical crossing too early.
   - delay until both worlds are already loved.
