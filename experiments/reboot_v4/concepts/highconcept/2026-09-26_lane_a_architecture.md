# A1｜《魔门杀我九十九次，我把每一世都炼成了神通》

## 1. Promise Lock

Frozen title:
> 《魔门杀我九十九次，我把每一世都炼成了神通》

Frozen hook:
> 每死一次，杀我的东西就会成为下一世的一门神通；九十九条死法以后，魔门才发现他们一直在亲手养我。

Must remain:
- death is real pressure, not cheap respawn;
- death itself produces power;
- exactly ninety-nine deaths matter;
- the sect is unknowingly / partially knowingly cultivating the protagonist through death;
- an old book already contains his ninety-nine deaths.

## 2. Ch1–3

### Ch1｜第一次死
- Protagonist enters a demonic sect as the lowest disposable trial-medicine disciple.
- Before he understands the rules, he is deliberately fed an experimental pill.
- He dies painfully.
- He wakes back on the morning he entered the sect.
- Physical evidence remains inside him: the poison that killed him can no longer kill him, and he can produce a trace of its lethal property.

### Ch2｜不逃，先验证
- He tries to avoid the original pill route.
- The sect has multiple disposal mechanisms; avoiding one danger pushes him toward another.
- He realizes the reset is not ordinary immortality: a mark has appeared on his soul, showing **1/99**.
- The first death cannot be repeated for a second gain. A death mechanism only enters him once.

### Ch3｜第一次反杀
- The person / mechanism that killed him the first time is confronted again.
- The protagonist uses the lethal property left by the first death to reverse the situation.
- He does not become strong enough to destroy the sect; he merely crosses a line that should have been impossible for a disposable disciple.
- At the end, he finds an old, locked sect ledger.
- One visible line is enough:
  > 第一道死劫：药死。
- The second line is already written, although it has not happened yet.

By Ch3:
> title promise is fully working.

## 3. Why He Cannot Walk Away

Personal:
> He needs to know why a book older than his birth already contains his deaths. Leaving without the answer means living under a death sentence whose next line can appear anywhere.

External:
> Entry into the sect creates a soul registry / sect seal. A registered trial disciple cannot simply walk out; outside enemies and sect law both make escape without power nearly suicidal.

## 4. Long Magnet

Concrete magnet:
> **What is death number 99, and who wrote the ninety-nine deaths before he ever entered the sect?**

Hard rule:
- deaths are not farmable;
- only a genuinely new fatal causal mechanism counts;
- repeated suicide or deliberately recreating an old death produces nothing and still consumes life if it crosses the real boundary;
- after surviving / overturning a major destined death, the "life anchor" advances. The whole novel does not reset to the same first morning forever.

This preserves:
> fixed number + escalation + mystery.

## 5. Escalation Ladder

### Stage 1｜试药人的前三条死法
Drug / curse / beast.
Goal:
> prove death can become usable power.

### Stage 2｜外门死劫簿
The protagonist learns that several coming deaths correspond to real sect systems:
- trial medicine;
- blood sacrifice;
- mission bait;
- sect competition.

Now:
> surviving means reading how the sect consumes people.

### Stage 3｜有人开始故意“养劫”
A senior figure notices that the protagonist survives impossible situations.
They do not know the exact mechanic, but begin arranging fatal situations to test him.

The sect shifts from:
> unknowingly feeding him
to
> partially weaponizing his anomaly.

### Stage 4｜死法开始超出宗门
The ledger contains deaths involving:
- rival sects;
- ancient ruins;
- heavenly tribulation;
- an unnamed hand.

This proves:
> the ninety-nine deaths are larger than one sect.

### Stage 5｜前面的死法开始组合
The protagonist can no longer win through "one death = one trick".
Older death-derived abilities must combine:
- poison + formation;
- curse + sword;
- beast + soul.

The book remains:
> I turn what killed me into what I use.

### Stage 6｜第九十九页
The final death does not name a weapon.
It names:
> **自己。**

Whether literal or a deeper identity truth is withheld until late.

## 6. Ch20 State

By around Ch20:
- 4–6 distinct death marks exist;
- at least 2 have already combined in a visible way;
- the protagonist knows deaths are finite and non-farmable;
- one recurring senior figure is actively testing / exploiting him;
- one old ledger page has proven predictive beyond coincidence;
- the protagonist has moved from disposable material to a dangerous anomaly the sect cannot casually kill.

## 7. Ch100 Proof

At Ch100:
- he is still converting genuine deaths into power;
- the death count still visibly matters;
- the ledger mystery has expanded beyond the first sect;
- previous death-derived powers are being recombined against stronger causes of death;
- the protagonist has not become a generic cultivator who merely happens to have a resurrection backstory.

The book is still:
> **What killed me becomes mine, and all ninety-nine deaths were written before I knew them.**

## 8. Character Engine

Protagonist desire:
> not "become strongest", but **refuse to die according to a life somebody else has already written for him.**

Fear / temptation:
> once he realizes death grants power, the temptation is to accept danger too easily and gradually become someone who treats his own life as expendable.

Core relationship 1｜同批试药弟子
Independent desire:
> escape alive and never become a cultivator's material again.

Collision:
> the protagonist increasingly stays near danger because his death list points inward; the companion wants to leave.

Core relationship 2｜负责试药 / 死劫测试的 senior
Independent desire:
> create a body that can survive a specific sect catastrophe.

Collision:
> sees the protagonist as the missing solution, not merely a victim.

The senior must be competent and have a reason to keep him alive sometimes.

## 9. Opposition

Recurring pressures:
- sect extraction systems;
- competent senior experimenters;
- rival disciples who understand resource competition;
- future deaths already written;
- the protagonist's own increasing willingness to gamble with death.

No "new idiot villain every arc".

## 10. Treadmill Risk

Main failure:
> new arc → protagonist dies in a new way → gets new power → repeats.

Fix:
- death type must correspond to a larger causal system, not a monster-of-the-week;
- old death powers combine and acquire new meanings;
- each major death reveals why the ledger knows him;
- the fixed 99 count creates irreversible cost;
- some deaths must be strategically avoided rather than "collected".

## 11. Benchmark Backcheck

Against 《苟在初圣魔门当人材》:
- title remains at least as concrete;
- first death / reset / benefit arrives immediately;
- fixed 99 count adds visible pressure;
- old ledger adds a more concrete long mystery.

Against 《诡异世界，我的神通无限升级！》:
- power acquisition remains immediate;
- death powers are causally tied to threats rather than random drops.

Did architecture make premise smaller?
> NO.

## 12. Biggest Unverified Assumption

> Can readers continue to feel death as danger after several resets, even with a fixed 99 limit?

## Result

> **STRUCTURALLY VIABLE**

Critical execution rule:
> death must become more frightening as the count falls, not more routine.

# A2｜《仙门把我当炉鼎，我把整座宗门炼进了丹田》

## 1. Promise Lock

Frozen title:
> 《仙门把我当炉鼎，我把整座宗门炼进了丹田》

Frozen hook:
> 他们想拿我给天骄续命，我却发现每一次被抽走的修为，都会在丹田里长出一个属于我的“仙门”。

Must remain:
- protagonist begins as institutional fuel;
- extraction creates internal mirrored inheritance;
- the internal sect becomes increasingly real;
- long term points toward the entire sect existing inside him;
- the question of which sect / ancestor is "real" remains central.

## 2. Ch1–3

### Ch1｜第一次被抽
- Protagonist is a low-status "support disciple" raised under a formal rule: his cultivation exists to support chosen core disciples.
- A favored disciple suffers a cultivation collapse.
- Protagonist is selected as emergency fuel.
- His qi / blood / lifespan are forcibly extracted.
- Everyone expects him to become a cripple.

### Ch2｜丹田里多了一座峰
- He survives.
- Inside his dantian appears a miniature but real structural "shadow" of the favored disciple's lineage: not a copied technique, but the root circulation / Dao structure behind it.
- When he operates it, he can do something that only that peak's direct lineage should be able to do.

### Ch3｜他们再抽一次，我再长一峰
- A second extraction happens for a different need or under sect procedure.
- Another internal structure appears.
- The protagonist realizes:
  > the sect is not emptying him.
  > every official act of taking from him is unknowingly stamping part of the sect into him.
- End question:
  > why does the sect's ancient altar react to his internal "fake" peak as if it recognizes the original?

By Ch3:
> title promise is already literal, not metaphorical.

## 3. Why He Cannot Walk Away

Personal:
> He was raised among support disciples and has people still on the extraction list. Escaping alone means leaving them to be consumed in his place.

External:
> support disciples are bound by a formal sect contract / soul register; their bodies and cultivation are legally / magically defined as sect resources.

## 4. Long Magnet

Concrete magnet:
> **When every peak, hall, inheritance and ancestral authority has been mirrored inside him, which one will the founding ancestor recognize as the true sect?**

The internal sect must become more literal over time:
- internal peak;
- internal furnace;
- internal sword tomb;
- internal scripture hall;
- internal ancestral hall.

Eventually:
> he is not "a man with many techniques".
> he is a walking second sect.

## 5. Escalation Ladder

### Stage 1｜一个炉鼎，一座影峰
First extraction creates first internal peak.

### Stage 2｜供养院里的人开始被他反向“接走”
He learns internal structures can shelter / preserve what the outer sect intends to consume:
- cultivation imprint;
- life fire;
- technique lineage.

This converts personal survival into a reason others matter.

### Stage 3｜五峰入体
Different peak systems begin conflicting inside him.
He must make them coexist rather than collect them as slots.

### Stage 4｜外门发现“真传在他身上更完整”
A core inheritance unexpectedly responds more strongly to the protagonist than to official heirs.

Now suppression becomes rational:
> he threatens legitimacy.

### Stage 5｜体内祖庭
The internal sect develops an ancestral hall / authority core.
An ancient sect artifact recognizes him.

### Stage 6｜两座仙门只能有一个“正统”
The conflict becomes:
> mountain sect vs walking sect.

Not merely protagonist vs elders.

## 6. Ch20 State

By around Ch20:
- at least 3 internal sect structures exist;
- one internal structure can visibly protect / preserve another support disciple's cultivation imprint;
- at least one official heir has discovered that the protagonist's version of their lineage is disturbingly "purer";
- the sect's extraction system has a concrete administrator and rationale;
- an ancestral object has reacted to the internal sect.

## 7. Ch100 Proof

At Ch100:
- protagonist still grows because the actual sect's systems are being mirrored and contested;
- old peaks remain active internal territories, not forgotten abilities;
- legitimacy conflict has grown;
- support disciples and official heirs both have reasons to choose sides;
- the title is becoming more literal:
  > an entire sect really is forming inside him.

The novel has not become generic "copy all techniques".

## 8. Character Engine

Protagonist desire:
> **never again let a person be born into a role where their life is somebody else's cultivation resource.**

Fear / temptation:
> as his internal sect grows, he may begin treating people as "components" exactly like the outer sect did.

Core relationship 1｜a favored core disciple who survives because of his extracted life
Independent desire:
> live, keep their cultivation, and prove they deserve their position.

Collision:
> they are both beneficiary and evidence of the exploitation system; may hate the system while still fearing life without it.

Core relationship 2｜a support disciple close to protagonist
Independent desire:
> simply escape and live an ordinary life.

Collision:
> protagonist increasingly wants to overturn the entire system, which may endanger everyone.

## 9. Opposition

Recurring pressures:
- sect's formal extraction bureaucracy;
- core heirs whose survival / status depends on the system;
- reformist figures who want to change the system without destroying the sect;
- ancient ancestral logic that may reveal the extraction system had an original purpose;
- internal peak conflicts inside protagonist.

Opposition is not:
> every elder is stupid and evil.

## 10. Treadmill Risk

Main failure:
> new peak extracts him → new internal peak unlocks → repeat.

Fix:
- not every extraction creates a new structure;
- structures conflict, merge, and require choices;
- the real escalation is legitimacy / ownership:
  > who owns the Dao lineage?
- people begin defecting or staking identity around the internal sect;
- the outer sect changes tactics once mechanism becomes visible.

## 11. Benchmark Backcheck

Against 《没钱修什么仙？》:
- exploitation remains concrete, not abstract social commentary;
- every act of extraction produces visible reversal.

Against 《苟在初圣魔门当人材》:
- opening danger is equally immediate;
- long magnet is not "escape", but "which sect is real".

Did architecture make premise smaller?
> NO.

## 12. Biggest Unverified Assumption

> Can "a sect inside the dantian" stay visually and emotionally concrete rather than becoming abstract cultivation jargon?

## Result

> **STRUCTURALLY VIABLE**

Critical execution rule:
> every internal "building" must correspond to a real person, lineage, conflict or cost outside, or the concept becomes a skill menu.

# A3｜《全宗门都在修仙，只有我在给天道办丧事》

## 1. Promise Lock

Frozen title:
> 《全宗门都在修仙，只有我在给天道办丧事》

Frozen hook:
> 他们抢灵气、争飞升，我却每天都能看到一条“天道讣告”——而下一条，写的是整个修仙界。

Must remain:
- protagonist literally deals with death / funerary logic;
- mountains, rivers, spiritual veins, techniques and heaven itself can die;
- world-scale death countdown exists;
- ascension may be connected to old heaven's final harvest;
- the endgame is whether to save old heaven or bury it.

## 2. Ch1–3

### Ch1｜死人讣告很正常，灵脉讣告不正常
- Protagonist works in the lowest corpse-handling / burial hall.
- He can see death notices attached to true dying things.
- Human corpses are unsurprising.
- Then he sees one on a local spiritual vein:
  > name;
  > death date in three days;
  > funeral method.
- No cause is given.

### Ch2｜所有人都在抢救灵脉，他却准备下葬
- The sect / local powers detect the vein weakening and pour resources into "saving" it.
- The protagonist realizes the funeral notice is not optional superstition:
  > when a thing has entered true death, refusing burial turns its remains into catastrophe.
- He chooses to prepare the burial even though it looks like destroying valuable sect property.

### Ch3｜第一场大丧
- The spiritual vein dies exactly on time.
- Because he completes the correct burial action, the backlash that should destroy the city / sect region is contained.
- The dead vein leaves behind a "legacy": not loot in the game sense, but one usable remnant / final law that proves dead things can pass something on.
- At the end, he sees a much larger obituary:
  > **某年某月某日，天道卒。**
- A second line mentions:
  > **殉葬者：飞升者。**

By Ch3:
> the novel is no longer "undertaker with supernatural eyesight"; the cosmic premise is active.

## 3. Why He Cannot Walk Away

Personal:
> he has already seen his own name once in a funeral record, but the date is blank. He needs to know whether he is the mourner, the corpse, or part of the burial.

External:
> once he performs a true burial for a nonhuman world-structure, dead things begin "recognizing" him as a funeral executor. He cannot simply stop seeing the notices.

## 4. Long Magnet

Concrete magnet:
> **The death date of Heaven is fixed. Every major obituary brings the world closer to that funeral.**

Second magnet:
> **Why are ascenders listed as burial companions / sacrifices?**

End decision:
> preserve old Heaven and its order;
or
> complete the funeral and survive the birth of a new order.

## 5. Escalation Ladder

### Stage 1｜给人收尸
Establish protagonist, burial rules, small death notices.

### Stage 2｜给灵脉下葬
First proof that nonhuman structures die.

### Stage 3｜给功法 / 道统办后事
A once-great inheritance is dying.
The protagonist discovers:
> ideas / lineages can leave wills and corpses too.

### Stage 4｜一宗之讣
An entire sect appears in a death notice before its people realize they are doomed.
The protagonist must determine what "the sect dying" actually means.

### Stage 5｜飞升者皆是殉葬者
Evidence accumulates that ascension drains / feeds the dying heaven.

This directly attacks the world's highest aspiration.

### Stage 6｜天葬
World factions split:
- save heaven;
- replace heaven;
- accelerate the funeral;
- exploit the corpse.

The protagonist is no longer merely observing death:
> he becomes the person whose funeral decision can determine the next era.

## 6. Ch20 State

By around Ch20:
- the protagonist has completed at least one human and one nonhuman true burial;
- the first spiritual-vein burial has permanently altered local power / terrain;
- a dead thing's "legacy" is in active use;
- at least one senior figure believes the protagonist's funeral sight is real;
- the Heaven obituary exists and its date is concrete;
- ascension has already shown one disturbing inconsistency with mainstream doctrine.

## 7. Ch100 Proof

At Ch100:
- funerary logic still drives the major conflicts;
- larger things are dying:
  > local vein → inheritance → sect / region → heaven order;
- the fixed death date is closer;
- ascension mystery is deeper;
- the protagonist's role has evolved from corpse handler to someone world powers need for the death of institutions / laws / heaven.

The novel is still:
> **everyone else cultivates toward Heaven; I am preparing Heaven's funeral.**

## 8. Character Engine

Protagonist desire:
> **understand what deserves to be saved, what must be allowed to die, and refuse to let powerful people make the living pay forever for a system that is already dead.**

Fear / temptation:
> he may become too comfortable declaring things "already dead" and stop fighting for what could still live.

Core relationship 1｜a gifted cultivator whose only dream is ascension
Independent desire:
> ascend and prove the world's highest promise is real.

Collision:
> protagonist discovers ascension may be sacrificial.

Core relationship 2｜a corpse-hall elder / undertaker
Independent desire:
> preserve the old funeral rules because breaking them once caused catastrophe.

Collision:
> knows more about previous failed "heaven funerals" than they initially admit.

## 9. Opposition

Recurring pressures:
- sects invested in ascension;
- beneficiaries of the current heavenly order;
- people who sincerely believe saving Heaven is worth sacrifice;
- factions that want to kill Heaven early and loot the corpse;
- death notices that give date / rite but not full cause.

Important limitation:
> the obituary must not solve the mystery for the protagonist.

## 10. Treadmill Risk

Main failure:
> every arc becomes "new thing has obituary → protagonist explains funeral rule → solves it".

Fix:
- not every death can / should be prevented;
- some funerals conflict politically and morally;
- notices reveal date and rite, not culprit or full causal chain;
- deaths scale from object to institution to world-order;
- previous funerals alter what future deaths mean;
- the Heaven countdown is always moving.

## 11. Benchmark Backcheck

Against 《剑烛大荒》:
- cultural / mythic scale remains large;
- protagonist's growth is tied to increasingly grand world structures rather than a catalogue.

Against 《没钱修什么仙？》:
- the world system itself becomes the thing being questioned and resisted;
- "ascension" is turned from default aspiration into a concrete suspect mechanism.

Did architecture make premise smaller?
> NO.

## 12. Biggest Unverified Assumption

> Can the story keep "funeral / death of systems" emotionally concrete enough that it does not drift into abstract metaphysical exposition?

## Result

> **STRUCTURALLY VIABLE**

Critical execution rule:
> every cosmic idea must first appear as something physically happening to people, land, sects, or relationships before anyone explains the cosmology.
