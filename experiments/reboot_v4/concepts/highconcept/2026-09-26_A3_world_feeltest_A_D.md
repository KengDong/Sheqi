# A｜现代全民修仙文明

## Benchmark function

Primary:
- 《没钱修什么仙？》 — modern social system itself becomes cultivation conflict.
- 《捞尸人》 — death-handling profession gives access and authority.
- 《1984：从川菜馆开始》 — small jobs leave concrete daily changes.

Borrow:
> familiar social infrastructure + one impossible professional advantage + visible results.

Do not copy:
> finance / loans / platform vocabulary as the main gimmick.

## Working title

> **《全民修仙把天道吸干了，我来给它办丧事》**

## Shelf hook

> 这个世界修仙已经像水电一样普及。直到我在一座仍给三万人供灵的聚灵塔上看见一行字：**已死三年，仍在服役。**

## 120-char package

> 穿越全民修仙时代，主角成了城市灵能回收站最底层的临时工。学校靠灵网修炼，医院靠灵阵续命，整座城市一天也离不开灵气。可他的系统却告诉他：那些还在运行的灵器、灵田、灵脉，有些早就死了。  
> 别人拼命修，他负责送终。直到他发现——这座繁华城市不是缺灵气，是在拿所有人的未来给天道续命。

## First view of the world

The protagonist wakes on a morning commuter flying rail.

Around him:
- office workers meditate with public qi ports;
- students wear standardized spirit-root monitors;
- station screens display:
  > 今日城市灵网负荷 91%
- an advertisement promises:
  > “家庭聚灵套餐升级，三年筑基不是梦。”

Nobody finds any of this strange.

To the protagonist:
> this is an advanced civilization powered by cultivation.

His new body works as:
> temporary recycling staff at a municipal spirit-equipment disposal station.

His job is humiliating and low-status:
> dismantle expired spirit tools nobody wants.

## First “dead three years, still working”

The station receives an emergency notice.

A thirty-year-old public qi tower serving an old residential district is overheating.

Replacement cost is enormous.
Shutting it off means:
- 30,000 residents lose household qi supply;
- nearby clinic loses emergency formation power;
- students lose exam-season cultivation support.

The maintenance company insists:
> patch it for another three years.

The protagonist touches the tower.

System:

> 【青禾七号聚灵塔】  
> 【状态：已死】  
> 【死亡时间：三年前】  
> 【当前：强制运行】  
> 【代偿来源：辖区生灵微量寿元】

Meaning:
> it has been “working” by taking tiny amounts of lifespan from everyone connected to it.

No one notices because each person loses very little.

But 30,000 people together keep a corpse running.

## Ch1–3 pleasure

Ch1:
> protagonist sees the impossible diagnosis.

Ch2:
> everyone has rational reasons not to shut the tower down.

Ch3:
> he proves the tower cannot be repaired; correctly closes it and returns its remaining spirit circulation into local underground channels.

Immediate payoff:
- one night of city-block blackout / crisis;
- the old district's long-dead natural spirit spring unexpectedly restarts;
- the clinic and residents can transition to a lower-output but self-renewing local system;
- protagonist receives first 余荫.

End hook:
> system scans the city skyline.

Hundreds of functioning towers briefly display faint gray death marks.

## Daily story flavor

Possible daily worlds:
- university cultivation lab;
- spirit hospital;
- apartment qi network;
- industrial pill farm;
- municipal protection array;
- artificial spirit-root nursery;
- public flying transit;
- old residential spirit wells.

The core scene is always:
> society thinks “functioning = alive.”
> protagonist knows some things are corpses.

## Long-line flavor

At first:
> maintenance failure.

Then:
> the city has built prosperity on corpse infrastructure.

Then:
> every city is doing it.

Then:
> national / world cultivation output keeps rising while Heaven's lifespan collapses.

Ascension later becomes:
> Heaven's emergency recovery program.

## What feels good

- easiest version of “全民修仙把天道吸干” to understand;
- ordinary modern needs make “cannot simply shut it down” natural;
- daily cases endless;
- public reaction / status growth very clear.

## What could feel wrong

- if written with too much company / school / city-system vocabulary, can feel too close to 《没钱修什么仙？》;
- funeral / mythic flavor weaker;
- later must deliberately return to mountains, old sect ruins, Heaven and mythic imagery.

# D｜双界：现代修真盛世 + 被遗忘的古修“葬界”

## Benchmark function

Primary:
- two-world / contrast fiction provides immediate world comparison and information asymmetry.
- 《捞尸人》 — forbidden world access.
- 《剑烛大荒》 / 《夜无疆》 — ancient world mystery raises life/world scale.
- 《没钱修什么仙？》 — one civilization's normal system contains the crisis.

Core design rule:
> second world cannot be a warehouse or trading map.
> It must be half of the Heaven-death mystery.

## Working title option 1

> **《全民修仙把天道吸干后，我打开了上一个修仙界的棺材》**

## Working title option 2

> **《两个修仙界都快死了，只有我会给天道送终》**

## Working title option 3

> **《我给天道送终，却挖出了上一个修仙纪元》**

## Shelf hook

> 我给一件死了三年的灵器送终时，棺后竟然打开了另一个修仙世界——那里的人告诉我：**你们现在拜的天道，是从我们这个世界的尸体上长出来的。**

## 150-char package

> 穿越全民修仙盛世，主角成了灵器回收站的底层杂工。第一天，他就在仍运行的聚灵塔上看见：**已死三年，仍在服役。**  
> 他按照系统提示完成第一次送终，本以为只是拿到一份遗泽，却在死物归还天地的瞬间打开一道“葬门”。  
> 门后，是一个仍停留在古老宗门时代的残破修仙界。  
> 那里没有全民修仙，没有灵能都市，却保存着早已从现代世界消失的“归还之法”。  
> 更恐怖的是——古界碑文记载，他们的天道，很多年前已经死过一次。

## What the two worlds actually are

Do NOT make them two random universes.

Working high-concept:

Thousands of years ago, one complete Heaven underwent a catastrophic failed funeral.

The cycle split.

### Modern world inherited:
> 生 / 取 / growth / extraction

Therefore:
- prosperity;
- mass cultivation;
- industrial spiritual energy;
- fast growth;
- almost no proper return.

It looks alive,
but is consuming its future.

### Ancient “burial world” inherited:
> 死 / 还 / ending / memory

Therefore:
- traditional sects;
- powerful funeral / return knowledge;
- ancient spirits / dead laws;
- very slow population / resource renewal;
- many things can end correctly, but new growth is weak.

It looks old and declining,
but remembers how things should die.

Both worlds are incomplete.

The protagonist can cross because:
> his outsider soul + 万物送终 system is the missing bridge between the two halves.

## First view of the modern world

Same opening strength as A:
- mass cultivation infrastructure;
- low-status recycling job;
- public qi tower.

System:
> 【已死三年，仍在服役。】

## First crossing

After the protagonist properly closes the dead tower:

The returned vitality should flow into the modern land.

But half of it disappears.

A black door appears only to him.

On the other side:

Not another futuristic city.

A vast traditional mountain gate.

No neon qi grid.
No public cultivation towers.

An old man is burying a dead spirit spring by hand.

He looks at the protagonist's modern worker uniform and says:

> “你那边的人，已经连东西死了以后该还给谁，都忘了吗？”

This is the first world contrast.

## Ch1–3 pleasure

Ch1:
> modern dead tower mystery.

Ch2:
> rational modern society refuses shutdown; protagonist solves terminal cause.

Ch3:
> tower is sent off → local modern environment changes → “葬门” opens → protagonist sees ancient cultivation world.

This gives an extra Ch3 explosion:
> the system premise + a whole second world.

## What the ancient world gives the story

Not:
> cheap herbs / old techniques to resell.

It gives:
- lost funeral laws;
- old records of the previous Heaven-death event;
- living examples of correct return cycles;
- ancient factions who disagree over whether the two worlds should ever reconnect;
- clues that the modern Heaven may literally have grown from the corpse / remains of the old cycle.

## What the modern world gives the ancient world

Not:
> phones / industrial products for comedy.

It gives:
- powerful growth / repair techniques;
- abundant organized cultivation knowledge;
- methods to keep the ancient world from simply withering.

Therefore neither world is superior.

Modern:
> knows how to make things grow,
but not how to let them end.

Ancient:
> knows how to let things end,
but has lost the ability to renew at scale.

The protagonist's eventual answer:
> reunite growth and return.

## Daily story engine

A modern case can have an ancient mirror.

Example:
Modern:
> city river spirit-network is dying.

Ancient:
> corresponding old river deity died correctly centuries ago, leaving a “return channel”.

The protagonist has to understand the ancient ending,
then solve the modern corpse-system.

Or reverse:
Ancient:
> a sect insists on burying a still-saveable inheritance because tradition says “its lifespan is over”.

Modern technology / cultivation knowledge proves:
> this one can still be saved.

This is important:
> the protagonist is not automatically “ancient wisdom good, modern civilization bad.”

Both worlds can be wrong.

## Long mystery

Reader keeps asking:
1. Why did one Heaven split into two world-cycles?
2. Who prevented the previous proper funeral?
3. Is the modern Heaven a new Heaven, or a reanimated corpse of the old one?
4. Why does every correct send-off make the two worlds slightly more connected?
5. If the two worlds reunite, will both survive—or will one swallow the other?

## Mid-book explosion

The protagonist eventually discovers:

> Modern “ascension” is not leaving to a higher world.

Ascenders are crossing toward the burial world.

But almost nobody arrives as a living person.

Their cultivation is stripped during passage to feed the broken boundary between the two worlds.

Ancient cultivators call these falling lights:
> **天尸。**

This gives “飞升 = 回收” a visible second-world consequence.

## Final-scale promise

Modern Heaven is dying.

Ancient burial-world Heaven is already half-dead.

The two are actually:
> two halves of one failed cycle.

The protagonist's final job is not merely bury one Heaven.

It is:
> **complete the funeral that failed thousands of years ago, then decide whether one new Heaven should be born from both worlds.**

## What feels good

- much bigger mystery;
- traditional xianxia + modern cultivation both available;
- “送终” becomes literal inter-world access;
- Heaven funeral acquires ancient historical weight;
- Ch3 has a second-world shock.

## What could feel wrong

- much harder to write;
- risk of two-world trading / sightseeing;
- two world systems may dilute first-book simplicity;
- if ancient world appears too much, modern systemic crisis may lose focus;
- must control information so the reader does not need a textbook.

# Pure-feel contrast

## A feels like:
> “这个繁华修真社会有哪里不对，而我越来越看得懂它为什么会死。”

Main pleasure:
> familiar society + competence + system-level reveal.

## D feels like:
> “我在给现代修真文明收尸，却意外挖出了上一整个修仙纪元的尸体。”

Main pleasure:
> profession + double-world mystery + ancient cosmic secret.
