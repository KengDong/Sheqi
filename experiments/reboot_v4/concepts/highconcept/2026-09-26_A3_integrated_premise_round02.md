# Benchmark design law

Current strong references repeatedly show:
> one core rule should generate opening reversal, repeated scene pleasure, personal growth, social identity and endpoint.

Functional references:
- 《我正经学生，每天只吃九种魔药》: side-effect reversal does every layer.
- 《从水猴子开始成神》: water domain does every layer.
- 《这个地下城长蘑菇了》: fungus / corpse ecology does every layer.
- 《我的化身正在成为最终BOSS》: character + fame engine does every layer.
- 《苟在初圣魔门当人材》: finite restart does every layer.
- 《我能加载旧日设定》: world threat / hidden identity inversion is the same mechanic.

Reject:
> identity fantasy + separate cultivation system + separate funeral cases + separate Heaven mystery.

# R1｜《全世界都在修仙，只有我知道仙道已经死了》

## One rule

> **现世所有人修的“仙道”，本身已经死了。**

The world is not using a healthy Heaven-and-Earth cycle.

It is:
> keeping the corpse of an old cultivation order running.

This explains EVERYTHING.

## Old price

The protagonist is diagnosed as:
> 无灵根 / 不受道 / cultivation incompatibility.

Society says:
> he cannot connect to Heaven's Dao.

Truth:
> **he is the only person not compatible with a dead Dao.**

His “defect” is the first evidence the world is wrong.

## Ch1 revaluation

At a standard cultivation test,
everyone's techniques / roots receive normal compatibility.

His result:
> 0.

System appears.

It does not say:
> “SSS hidden root”.

It shows:
> **【当前仙道：已死亡】**
> **【死亡时间：三千一百二十七年前】**
> **【当前状态：强制运行】**

This is the first click.

## Repeated daily engine

The dead Dao expresses through concrete things:
- a technique that still circulates but no longer regenerates what it consumes;
- a spirit field whose growth rule has died;
- a formation that works by cannibalizing nearby life;
- a lineage whose “Dao” ended generations ago;
- a spirit vein held together by old Heaven-law.

The protagonist can:
> identify which piece of the old Dao is truly terminal;
> complete its send-off;
> free the life / rule trapped inside it.

Every case is not a random dead object.
It is:
> **another organ of the same dead cultivation system.**

## Personal growth

When a dead Dao fragment is correctly returned:
> the world creates a tiny amount of genuinely NEW Dao.

The protagonist is the first person compatible with it.

So:
> he does not loot dead skills.
> he becomes the first cultivator of the next cultivation order.

Small:
> first new circulation.

Middle:
> a local new rule others can also begin using.

Large:
> a complete “新仙路”.

His strength and world repair are literally the same act.

## Social rise

At first:
> “废灵根.”

Then:
> people discover areas / techniques he “buried” begin producing a new stable path.

Now the question becomes:
> is he destroying cultivation,
or inventing the only cultivation that can survive?

Later:
> sects do not only ask him to diagnose death.
> they fight over whether to follow the new Dao he opens.

## World crisis

Why is everything dying early?

Because:
> the entire civilization is extracting power from a Dao corpse.

Breakthroughs, formations and ascension all keep the corpse moving.

## Endpoint

The last dead object is not a mountain or city.

It is:
> **the old Heaven / old Immortal Dao itself.**

The protagonist must decide whether to:
- keep the corpse running;
- salvage it again;
- or finally bury it and allow the next Dao to be born.

## Why this is more integrated than old H1/H2/H4

One rule explains:
- why protagonist was low-value;
- why he alone can act;
- why every case matters;
- why he grows;
- why society revalues him;
- why the world deteriorates;
- why final Heaven funeral exists.

## Biggest risk

Too abstract if first 20 chapters talk about “Dao” rather than show physical consequences.

Hard rule:
> every dead-law reveal must first appear as a familiar concrete failure.

# R2｜《我给万物送终，历史里却活了三千年》

## One rule

> **正确送终一件“死不干净”的古物，会把主角拉回它真正死亡的那一刻；完成那场死亡以后，这件事本来就会成为历史。**

Not alternate timeline.
Not free time travel.
Not second living world.

The present always contained the consequences.
The protagonist just did not know HE caused them.

## Old price

Present-day protagonist is ordinary / unknown.

No grand lineage.
No famous master.
No special social status.

## Ch1 revaluation

He sends off a worthless ancient object.

In the terminal echo,
he meets a dying cultivator.

That person sees him and says:
> “终于等到送终人了。”

He returns.

The worthless object is now properly dead.

But a museum / sect archive contains an ancient mural:
> a figure wearing exactly what protagonist wore in the echo,
standing beside that ancient death.

The date:
> 3,000 years ago.

Reader question:
> why was he already in history?

## Repeated daily engine

Present:
> something is dead-but-unresolved.

Send-off:
> enter its terminal moment.

Past:
> complete a specific final causal act.

Return:
> present history / relationships / institutions reveal what his intervention always caused.

Every loop pays four times:
1. present mystery;
2. terminal-event adventure;
3. immediate present-world consequence;
4. growing historical legend of the same mysterious undertaker.

## Personal growth

Do NOT collect ancient skills.

Each completed terminal event gives:
> **历史锚.**

Function:
- lets protagonist remain longer / enter larger terminal events;
- allows his body / cultivation to retain “what history genuinely remembers he did”;
- unlocks access to places / relics / authorities that recognize the ancient undertaker.

So growth is:
> **the more history truly contains him, the more real his cross-era identity becomes.**

At first:
> one mural.

Later:
> unrelated sect records describe him.

Then:
> entire ancient disasters have a missing “送终人” role that matches him.

## Social rise

Present sects begin to discover:
> their own ancestral records mention this living nobody.

Some think:
- reincarnation;
- fraud;
- immortal;
- time demon;
- ancestor.

His social identity grows because HISTORY already has weight.

This borrows the public-legend compounding function of multi-identity hits,
but uses one continuous mysterious identity.

## World crisis

Why are terminal echoes open now?

Because:
> the previous Heaven funeral was never completed.

Thousands of unresolved deaths remain caught between past and present.

Current world deterioration is the pressure from that unfinished end.

## Long mystery

At first:
> why did one ancient person expect him?

Then:
> why did several unrelated deaths expect the same “future undertaker”?

Eventually:
> ancient people were not predicting him.
> they had already MET later versions of him in terminal echoes.

The causality closes on itself.

## Endpoint

The greatest terminal echo:
> the day the previous Heaven died.

The protagonist discovers:
> his strange appearances across 3,000 years were all steps leading him to finish that one failed funeral.

Current Heaven survives or is reborn based on what he does there.

## Biggest risk

Time rules become nonsense.

Hard:
- no free history rewriting;
- only unresolved terminal events are accessible;
- protagonist cannot prevent the death that defines the echo;
- he can only determine HOW it ends and what remains.

# R3｜《这个修仙界，已经三千年没人死干净了》

## One rule

> **这个世界丢失的不是灵气，而是“真正死亡 / 归还天地”的法则。**

Bodies can die.
Tools can break.
Sects can disappear.

But:
> nothing completes the final return.

Therefore dead power, karma, law and vitality remain stuck in the world.

The world is becoming a room that never takes out its trash.

## Old price

Protagonist is born / arrives with a feared abnormality:
> **死脉 / 短寿 / no longevity blessing.**

In a civilization obsessed with longevity,
his body is considered defective.

Unlike everyone else,
his life has a true “end”.

## Revaluation

System / lost law awakens.

Truth:
> he is not cursed with death.

He is:
> **the only living carrier of the world's missing “终” authority.**

Everyone else can prolong.
Only he can make something truly end.

## First-screen hook

A powerful spiritual object breaks.

Experts say:
> repairable.

System says:
> **【已死七年】**
> **【因天地无“终”，无法归还】**

The protagonist touches it.

For the first time in centuries:
> something truly dies.

At the same moment,
dead land around it produces new life.

And protagonist's “死脉” opens its first cultivation circulation.

One action proves:
- his defect is power;
- true death is beneficial;
- world ecology is broken;
- personal cultivation begins.

## Repeated daily engine

Every arc asks:
> should this thing be granted a true end?

Targets rise:
- tool;
- beast;
- field;
- curse;
- person;
- lineage;
- city;
- law.

Some want death.
Some fear it.
Some powerful people have profited for centuries from things being unable to truly end.

This produces direct conflict.

## Personal growth

His lost “终” authority expands by scale.

He cannot collect skills from corpses.

He gains:
> the ability to complete larger and more abstract endings.

As more endings complete,
his own “死脉” becomes:
> a full life-death circulation.

Irony:
> the world's supposedly shortest-lived man develops the most complete life cycle.

## Social rise

At first:
> unlucky short-lived freak.

Then:
> terrifying person who can end things immortals cannot kill.

Later:
> essential figure because dead systems are poisoning whole regions.

His reputation naturally contains both:
- fear;
- need.

## World crisis

Why is Heaven dying?

Because:
> Heaven itself has been unable to complete old deaths for thousands of years.

Every “immortality” technique and forced continuation adds another layer of unreturned burden.

The pursuit of immortality did not defeat death.
It:
> **broke death.**

## Endpoint

Heaven reaches terminal state.

But it cannot die.

If it continues:
> all life is dragged with it.

Only protagonist can:
> restore “death” to Heaven.

After a proper Heaven death:
> the world can finally enter a new life cycle.

## Biggest risk

Can become philosophical / grim.

Hard:
> every return must produce visible practical renewal and satisfying relief,
not only funerary solemnity.

# R4｜《所有人都在飞升，只有我见过他们的尸体》

## One rule

> **“飞升成功”的修士并没有去更高世界；他们的尸体会落入一个只有主角能进入的终点层。**

This turns the world's highest aspiration into the protagonist's forbidden access lane.

## Old price

Protagonist has a strange failure:
> he cannot sense / answer “天门”.

In a civilization where every cultivator eventually aims to ascend,
this means:
> his road has no endpoint.

He is regarded as a cultivator who can never complete cultivation.

## Revaluation

One celebrated great cultivator publicly ascends.

The world rejoices.

That night,
protagonist's terminal system /归处 opens.

A corpse falls in front of him.

It is:
> the cultivator who just “ascended”.

The corpse's cultivation is almost completely stripped.

System:
> **【飞升：未完成死亡】**
> **【归还：被截留】**

Now the protagonist has something no one else in the world has:
> proof of where ascension really goes.

## Repeated daily engine

Do not wait only for rare top ascenders.

The same “terminal layer” receives:
- failed heavenly tribulations;
- abandoned Dao bodies;
- dead spirit-vein cores;
- things Heaven tries to reclaim.

Protagonist learns:
> Heaven is using the terminal layer as an emergency recycling yard.

Each case:
- exposes a piece of Heaven's survival mechanism;
- lets him properly return what was intercepted;
- expands his access deeper into the terminal layer.

## Personal growth

Properly completing a confiscated return:
> restores part of Heaven-Earth circulation and produces余荫.

His cultivation is powered by:
> what Heaven attempted to intercept but he returned correctly.

He becomes stronger as he sees deeper into the same mechanism.

## Social rise

At first:
> a nobody with an impossible story.

Then:
> he produces knowledge / relic evidence only an ascender could have carried.

Later:
> living top cultivators seek him BEFORE ascending.

Question:
> “If I go through that gate, what happens to me?”

This creates huge authority.

## World crisis

The Heaven is not evil.

It is dying.

Ascension became:
> an emergency resource-recovery protocol.

The more the world deteriorates,
the more aggressively Heaven recycles its strongest cultivators.

## Endpoint

Eventually the “terminal layer” receives:
> not another cultivator,
but a piece of Heaven itself.

The protagonist realizes:
> Heaven has started to fall into its own recycling yard.

Final:
> complete the first real Heaven funeral.

## Biggest risk

Revealing ascension truth too early spends a potentially huge mid-book twist.

Alternative:
> first terminal corpse is an unknown ancient ascender, and only later correlate with a modern public ascension.

# Round 02 observation

These are now four different BOOK ENGINES, not four jobs or four identity toppings.

R1:
> dead Immortal Dao → protagonist pioneers next Dao.

R2:
> send-off → terminal past → protagonist accumulates a 3,000-year historical identity.

R3:
> lost Death law → protagonist alone can make the world truly end / renew.

R4:
> ascension corpses → protagonist enters Heaven's hidden recycling endpoint.

All four preserve the original A3 attraction:
> death / send-off / Heaven crisis.

But each uses ONE causal rule to carry more of the book.

# No final selection

AUTHOR should judge:
- which one still feels exciting AFTER reading the mechanism;
- which one feels like it could produce 200 chapters without changing the core fantasy;
- which one has the strongest title-level click before any explanation.
