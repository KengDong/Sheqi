# X8 Alpha｜前两位古界行动者 V1

date: 2026-09-26
status: STRESS-TEST / WORKING CHARACTERS / NOT CANON

# Design requirement

The first two ancient actors must:
- want something independent of protagonist;
- continue acting while protagonist is absent;
- have something protagonist genuinely lacks;
- be able to refuse protagonist;
- create different return categories.

Do NOT make them:
> future-NPC reward vendors.

---

# Actor 01｜未来基础身法改革者

## Working public identity in modern era

Modern society remembers him as:
> one of the major reformers behind a widely taught foundational movement system.

His name / portrait is familiar enough that an ordinary university student can recognize him.

Modern myth:
> calm founder, master theorist, standardized a practical movement tradition.

Source-era reality:
> young, abrasive, under-resourced, experimentally minded, still arguing with everyone.

This gap itself is pleasurable.

## Source-era status

Age:
> early twenties.

Current social position:
> talented but not prestigious;
> attached to a small regional cultivation organization / practical fighting circle.

He does NOT have:
- a great lineage;
- a powerful teacher;
- institutional protection.

He DOES have:
- real fight experience;
- obsessive understanding of body mechanics / spiritual-force transitions;
- willingness to discard beautiful but useless inherited forms.

## Independent desire

He wants to prove:
> a movement technique should work for ordinary cultivators in real combat,
not just for elite bodies / ceremonial demonstrations.

His frustration:
> established schools optimize for their own lineages and body types.

This later explains why his method eventually becomes widely taught.

## Immediate problem when protagonist arrives

He is stuck on one transition:
> the technique can switch direction quickly,
but either loses force or overloads one meridian / joint.

He has failed repeatedly.

The modern standardized version solved the problem,
but also discarded a narrow branch.

Protagonist knows:
> the final form.

He does NOT know:
> why this compromise was necessary.

## First conflict

Founder assumes protagonist:
> stole an unpublished draft / is spying for a rival.

Protagonist demonstrates the mature transition.

Founder instantly sees:
> it is not a copy of his current method.
It contains years of development he has not yet done.

He tests protagonist with "why".

Protagonist fails.

Relationship becomes:
> neither master-disciple nor worship.
> creator ↔ impossible downstream witness.

## What protagonist can give him

1. mature descendant form;
2. known long-run injury / failure patterns;
3. broad fact that the method survives;
4. later comparisons between standardized and niche uses.

## What he can give protagonist

1. source logic:
   > why every movement exists;
2. an early branch that later disappears;
3. real combat adaptation drills;
4. referrals to his creator / practitioner circle;
5. later a founder-level relationship residue.

## Why he cannot become obedient

He values:
> whether a method is right NOW,
not whether history says it survives.

If modern protagonist says:
> "history says you should join organization X,"

he may answer:
> "history doesn't have to live with the people I abandon."

This actor is the first proof:
> future knowledge ≠ authority over other people's lives.

## Long-term relation

Stages:
suspicious opponent
→ co-experimenter
→ trusted technical peer
→ person who asks protagonist about downstream outcomes
→ first ancient recommender
→ later founder who deliberately leaves a future access path.

He may eventually disagree with protagonist on:
> public teaching vs lineage control;
> safety vs combat ceiling;
> whether future success justifies present compromise.

# Actor 02｜originally forgotten recovery practitioner

## Modern old-history status

Before protagonist interferes:
> almost no historical importance.

Possible record:
- one obscure name in the first founder's early notes;
- method later listed as unsafe / abandoned;
- disappears young.

Important:
> protagonist does NOT pick this person because they are a guaranteed future founder.

## Source-era status

Age:
> similar generation.

Current social position:
> low-status medical / recovery practitioner serving ordinary cultivators.

Not glamorous.

Independent desire:
> make post-training recovery affordable for people who cannot buy elite pills / treatment.

Why:
> she / he sees ordinary cultivators ruin themselves by copying training loads built for richer lineages.

This mirrors protagonist's modern money / recovery problem,
but is not created for him.

## Immediate technical problem

Current cheap recovery method:
- works quickly;
- is accessible;
- but builds a hidden long-term injury / spiritual residue.

Modern history knows:
> later practitioners abandoned it after serious complications.

Protagonist knows the failure,
but not the ancient materials / mechanism.

Actor knows:
> materials / bodies / local practice,
but not centuries of downstream failure data.

Perfect reciprocal fit.

## First reaction to protagonist

Does not believe "future witness."

Believes:
> first founder is either fooled or conducting some experiment.

Protagonist proves value by giving:
> one precise contraindication / failure pattern
that the actor has observed but never connected.

This earns:
> a trial, not trust.

## What protagonist gives

1. later failure cases;
2. broad replacement direction used by future medicine;
3. modern safety concepts;
4. information about which body types fail first.

## What actor gives

1. cheap source-level recovery method;
2. diagnosis logic;
3. knowledge of local herbs / soul-body interaction;
4. later a preserved resource / medical lineage return.

## First real investment

Old history says:
> this person does not matter.

After one successful joint correction,
protagonist still chooses to spend another scarce source visit helping.

Why:
> he believes the person's problem-solving ability / goal is valuable,
not because history guaranteed a return.

This is X8's first true "bet."

## Potential transformed future

Because protagonist changes survival / method:
this person MAY become:
- founder of an affordable recovery lineage;
- co-founder of an institution;
- still relatively obscure but leave one crucial method.

Do not decide immediately.

Her / his future should emerge from source-era choices.

## Long-term relation

Possible:
skeptic
→ transactional collaborator
→ person who proves protagonist can be wrong
→ friend / critic
→ one of the first actors to consciously build something "for the person in the future."

This actor is ideal for the first:
> "Then I'll leave it for you."

# Interaction between Actor 01 and Actor 02

They must know each other independently of protagonist.

Possible geometry:
- Actor 02 has treated Actor 01 after reckless experiments.
- Actor 01 respects Actor 02's results but mocks the caution.
- Actor 02 thinks Actor 01's movement method causes half the injuries being treated.

They already have friction.

Protagonist enters an existing relationship,
not a blank party.

This creates a Ch13–20 conflict:

Founder wants:
> publish / test the movement aggressively.

Recovery practitioner warns:
> current bodies will be damaged.

Modern protagonist knows:
> the movement eventually becomes mainstream and safe,
but also knows early versions caused injuries.

Now he cannot simply support one.

This is richer than:
> new NPC asks new question.

---

# Why these two are good first actors

Actor 01 gives:
> recognizable famous-future identity + immediate ancient status contrast.

Actor 02 gives:
> uncertain investment + money / recovery relevance + proof history is not only about famous people.

Together they create:
- technique;
- health / cost;
- relationship conflict;
- one known future;
- one unknown future.

By Ch20 Reader understands:
> protagonist can exploit known history,
but the more interesting future is what he helps create that never existed before.
