Make one actually writable early-book version under the current tendency:

> P early Earth-origin + M1 university backbone + M4 money/work secondary line + X8 patron network.

This does NOT delete P/Q/R or M1–M4 alternatives.

- Ch1 current pressure is concrete;

- Ch10 changes protagonist's current social allocation;
- Ch20 leaves permanent modern + ancient residue;
- Ch20–50 expands reward categories;

# 1. Protagonist's exact modern opening position

Age:
> 18–20, first-year student at an ordinary public university with cultivation education.

Background:
- ordinary salaried family;
- no famous lineage;
- no private teacher;
- not extreme poverty;
- enough money to live, not enough to casually buy every individualized training service.

Earth-origin:
> crossed / reincarnated years earlier and has been fully socialized in this world.

Reader does NOT need a long Earth flashback.

Current visible ability:
- theory above average;
- cultivation and practical combat around middle of cohort;
- one recurring movement / circulation mismatch makes him look capped.

He is NOT a trash-tier loser.

Core social problem:
> the next school training allocation is about to happen.

Specific near goal:
> enter the university's **core training roster / team reserve list**.

Why it matters:
- free coach time;
- recovery / training subsidy;
- better venue hours;
- access to school team selection;
- later exchange / competition recommendation.

This converts one ranking into:
> money + training + access + future status.

Current obstacle:
> protagonist misses the automatic cutoff by a small margin because his standardized footwork / circulation has a persistent adaptation problem.

There is one open challenge / supplementary assessment within several days.

Paid individualized correction likely solves it,
but costs too much / has no remaining low-cost slot.

So protagonist does the most ordinary thing:
> self-study.

# 23. Benchmark-function audit

## 《没钱修什么仙？》
Borrowed function:
> money / training / work / school status form one causal loop.

Implementation:
> better ability → roster → subsidy → paid sparring → more training → city opportunity.

No copy of:
- exact debt;
- sick family;
- high-school interview chassis.

## 《原来我是仙道天骄》
Borrowed function:
> switching worlds must create immediate value / status contrast.

Implementation:
> ordinary modern freshman becomes an impossible downstream witness to a future founder by Ch2.

Still weaker than public "天骄" worship,
so ancient reputation must become socially visible by Ch50.

## 《我有一个修仙世界》
Borrowed function:
> both sides have independent pressures and institutions.

Implementation:
- modern: roster / money / city selection;
- ancient: founder's local opportunity, peer circle, organizations, independent choices.

## 《苟在初圣魔门当人材》
Borrowed function:
> repeat on changed state.

Implementation:
> first founder rejects old optimal history;
> wrong bet proves answer sheet expires.

## 《九种魔药》
Borrowed function:
> next interaction should produce a qualitatively different kind of reward.

Implementation:
> knowledge → access → money → preserved resource → relationship gate.

## 《我的学习群里全是真大佬》
Borrowed function:
> remote high-value actors must need protagonist too.

Implementation:
> future founders want downstream outcome / failure data;
> protagonist wants source reasoning / current-era conditions.

# 24. Red-team failures to watch

1. First founder becomes too obedient after one proof.
2. Main character gives exact future biography and trivializes choices.
3. Modern university exists only to stage face-slaps.
4. Paid services become cartoonishly predatory.
5. Every ancient contact becomes famous.
6. Every source branch is automatically superior to modern standardization.
7. Reverse inheritance appears too often and becomes treasure farming.
8. Founder token bypasses competence rather than opening a chance.
9. City-level win comes from secret move nobody can counter, rather than integrated growth.
10. The Earth-origin /界痕 mystery starts explaining itself before the early social engine is established.
