# D-0114｜Field #1 / Field #2 Horror Rebuild Review Pack

# Core Correction

Old mistake:
> Returned Rule = Field theme.

New rule:
> **Field = giant failed-future catastrophe.**
>
> **Returned Rule = one small survivable law extracted at the end.**

Therefore:
- Field #1 is NOT “name dungeon”;
- Field #2 is NOT “door dungeon”.

---

# FIELD #1 PRIMARY CANDIDATE
# 《第十八夜，整座城开始重叠》
### internal shorthand: 南门坠城

## One-Line Situation

> **程野醒在十八年后的同一座城市。这里正在经历最后十八夜：每天午夜，一整片城区都会被另一个“失败版本”覆盖，建筑、道路、居民和灾难互相碾进现实；第十八夜之后，南门枢纽是唯一仍存在于所有版本里的出口。**

This Field is about:
> **surviving a city whose reality is coming apart.**

R1 is only ONE minor survival law encountered inside it.

## Why It Looks Impossible

On Day 1 Cheng Ye is told / sees:
- eighteen nights remain;
- each midnight one district becomes unstable;
- the city map loses more usable area every night;
- South Gate is far away;
- normal routes do not stay normal;
- shelters that are safe in one version may not exist in the next.

Reader understands:
> **18 days / 18 collapses / one shrinking route.**

## Governing Catastrophe｜Branch Overlap

At midnight:
> one area is overwritten by a different failed future of the same city.

Not illusion.

Physical effects:
- an overpass can become a collapsed overpass while people are still on it;
- a hospital can become its burned-out future shell;
- a dry road can become a flood channel;
- a station can gain / lose platforms;
- a residential block can suddenly contain people who insist they have lived there for years;
- two mutually incompatible buildings can occupy the same footprint for minutes before one wins.

No one can negotiate with this.

## Threat Ecology

### Threat A｜Spatial Overwrite
The environment can kill instantly.

### Threat B｜“Wrong-Version” Survivors
People from overlapping branches can appear.

Some are ordinary.
Some know things they should not know.
Some are desperate to preserve THEIR version of the city.

They are not the main supernatural threat.
They create human conflict.

### Threat C｜Collapse Ecology
Certain things survive branch overlap better than people.

Candidate manifestations:
- emergency lights that remain lit across versions;
- abandoned public-service machines that continue executing obsolete instructions;
- transit / evacuation mechanisms that treat humans as route data.

Keep this uncanny / mechanical, not chatty monsters.

### Threat D｜Night Rescue Rules
Different future districts have developed emergency survival laws.

One of these is R1:
> after dark, a correctly bound true name gives listeners the target location.

It is useful for rescue in a city where streets keep changing.
It is NOT the Field's central horror.

## Four+ Cheng Ye Near-Death Set Pieces

### Near Death 1｜Overpass Shear
On Night 1:
- Cheng Ye is crossing an elevated road;
- the district overlaps;
- half the overpass becomes a future collapsed version;
- the road literally ends under moving people;
- Cheng Ye survives by noticing one piece of infrastructure that exists in BOTH versions.

Purpose:
> immediate proof that the Field itself can kill without warning.

### Near Death 2｜False Shelter
A safe hospital / shelter survives two nights.

On the third:
> its footprint becomes a future incinerated shell during overlap.

People who treated “safe location” as permanent die.

Cheng Ye barely escapes through a service route planted earlier.

Lesson:
> safety is version-dependent.

### Near Death 3｜Mercy Costs Someone
Early Cheng Ye is still basically a warm / optimistic 27-year-old.

He catches a survivor who has deliberately redirected strangers into an unstable block to protect their own family group.

Cheng Ye refuses to abandon / permanently neutralize them.

Later that person repeats the behavior.
A named ally dies or is permanently lost into another branch.

This is NOT:
> “people are evil.”

It is:
> the exact event that creates Cheng Ye's second face.

After this:
> confirmed predators receive a different Cheng Ye.

### Near Death 4｜The Name Rule Backfires
R1 is first shown as a RESCUE tool during a branch collapse.

Later a hostile group weaponizes it.
Cheng Ye sees its mature danger.

Still:
> R1 remains one threat among many.

### Near Death 5｜Version Collision Train
In late Field:
- the group boards a train that exists in two future branches at once;
- one branch's tunnel is intact;
- the other's tunnel has already collapsed / flooded;
- the carriage begins switching between versions while moving.

They must determine which physical facts persist across both versions before the next switch.

This is a pure high-intensity logic-action set piece.

### Near Death 6｜Final Convergence
Night 18:
> multiple failed city versions stack around South Gate simultaneously.

The skyline becomes impossible:
- different towers occupying the same coordinates;
- rail lines entering buildings that do not exist in the other layer;
- whole districts visible through each other.

The group has minutes to reach the one exit anchor.

Cheng Ye's previous safe routes stop working.

He must recombine:
- persistent infrastructure clues;
- people;
- one emergency rule;
- a fallback built much earlier.

## Cheng Ye Personality Origin

### Before Field
Baseline:
- optimistic / light;
- jokes under stress;
- willing to help strangers;
- assumes most people can be reasoned with.

### During Field
He does NOT become grim to everyone.

He splits:

#### Allies / innocent people
He stays:
- funny;
- warm;
- reliable;
- emotionally easier to be around.

#### Confirmed predators
After the mercy-cost incident:
> he becomes cold, cautious, decisive.

This is the birth of the duality.

Important:
> the Field does not kill his warmth.
> it teaches him to choose who gets it.

## R1's Place

R1 appears around mid / late Field as:
> one emergency-rescue law already used by one future district.

Cheng Ye learns mature tactics:
- identity binding control;
- hearing control;
- route vs direction;
- compartmentalized names;
- propagation danger.

At exit:
> R1 is one of several black-plaque candidate laws.

He chooses it because:
- it has real rescue value;
- he understands its counterplay;
- other candidates are worse.

But the Reader would NEVER summarize Field #1 as:
> “the name副本.”

## Field #1 Hidden Foreshadowing

### Hard Seed 1｜Qingheli Is Already There
During one overlap:
> a future skyline contains the completed Qingheli tower / district.

Not highlighted.

Later Field #2 makes it meaningful.

### Hard Seed 2｜One Shared Infrastructure Mark
A symbol / emergency route code appears:
- South Gate;
- Qingheli;
- later Fields.

At first it looks like boring municipal branding.

Later reveal:
> it marks infrastructure that survives multiple future branches.

### Soft Seed 3｜Terminal Date
Evacuation / maintenance records stop after the same future date.

No term “归一日”.

### Soft Seed 4｜Prior Human Interaction
At the exit / black plaques:
- one selection slot is already empty;
- an old human tool / blood trace / scratch is present.

Later:
> proof someone else returned rules before Cheng Ye.

### Optional Deep Seed｜A Person Who Should Not Exist Yet
One NPC / face / voice can later turn out to be:
- a future version of someone Cheng Ye meets in reality;
OR
- the child / parent of a recurring character;
OR
- a Future-Wanter connection.

Do NOT lock identity now.

## Why This Can Feed Later Reveals

Later reveals can retroactively reframe:
- why South Gate persisted;
- why Qingheli appeared;
- why one municipal symbol repeated;
- why some overlap objects survived;
- why one plaque slot was empty;
- why the same terminal date keeps appearing.

Field #1 becomes a mine of delayed answers.

# FIELD #2 REBUILD
# 《青禾里二号楼正在掉出世界》
### abandon “封门楼” as reader-facing concept

## One-Line Situation

> **程野进入未来青禾里二号楼时，这栋四十层住宅已经成为一座被“外面”逐层吞掉的孤岛：每次楼内钟声响起，较低的一段楼层就会被从现实中剥离；住户的家暂时算“里面”，楼道、电梯、空置房和失去住户承认的空间都会逐渐变成“外面”。他们必须在整栋楼消失前，把活人送到一个根本不存在于当前建筑图纸上的出口。**

Door logic is now:
> one sub-rule of “inside / outside / recognized shelter.”

The Field is about:
> **a vertical residential world being eaten out of reality.**

## First Impact

Do NOT start with normal smoke.

Start with:
- current site is nine floors;
- Field tower is fully inhabited forty floors;
- Cheng Ye reaches ground exit;
- someone opens the lobby door;
- outside is NOT the construction site.

Possible image:
> the road and city below are hundreds of meters beneath them / missing / replaced by an impossible black vertical void.

Then the bell rings.

A lower floor disappears.

Reader immediately understands:
> **this building is not merely locked. It is being removed from the world.**

## Governing Catastrophe｜“Outside” Is Advancing Upward

Each cycle:
- a band of floors is reclassified / consumed;
- corridors and common spaces become unsafe first;
- inhabited homes can resist temporarily;
- once the last recognized household connection leaves, that apartment loses “inside” status.

This creates:
> movement pressure + home/shelter conflict.

## Threat Ecology

### Threat A｜Floor Erasure
Lower floors physically vanish / become unreachable.

### Threat B｜Outside Pressure
Common spaces touched by the advancing “outside” become lethal / impossible.

Do not anthropomorphize it.
It behaves like reality pressure, not a monster with motives.

### Threat C｜Resident Conflict
Upper residents know:
> opening their homes may keep corridors alive / allow evacuees through,
but may also expose their only safe space.

People have defensible reasons to refuse.

### Threat D｜The Tower Is Not Geographically Where It Should Be
Higher windows show:
- a Second Skyline;
- roads from Field #1;
- impossible districts.

This is macro shadow, not explanation.

## Cheng Ye Near-Death Ladder

### Near Death 1｜Lobby Exit
Someone tests the front door and is nearly / actually lost because “outside” is not ground-level reality.

### Near Death 2｜First Floor Band Erasure
Cheng Ye barely gets into a recognized apartment before the corridor segment ceases to exist.

He learns:
> room safety is real but conditional.

### Near Death 3｜Wrong Door Assumption
Invitation gets Cheng Ye / teammate into a room,
but when original recognized resident leaves:
> the room's status changes.

This preserves the old 803/804 logic, but now the cost is catastrophic:
> the room itself begins turning into Outside.

Cheng Ye's fallback saves only part of the group.

### Near Death 4｜Elevator from a Deleted Floor
An elevator opens.

Inside are residents / objects from floors already erased.

Using it seems like a shortcut.
It is not.

This is a major uncanny set piece and a future-clue carrier.

### Near Death 5｜Private Safety Becomes Collective Death
Midpoint:
> every household that seals itself preserves its apartment,
but forces “Outside pressure” into corridors / stairs.

If everyone chooses private safety:
> the evacuation route dies.

This is the upgraded version of the old ventilation idea.

It is supernatural and structurally tied to the Field.

### Near Death 6｜The 28F Sacrifice Problem
Keep the moral kernel:
> two homes / specific door numbers may need to be opened or abandoned to preserve a route.

But now the stakes are:
> those homes may be permanently reclassified as Outside.

“哪两户？说门牌” can survive here,
but it is one human beat inside a much larger catastrophe.

### Near Death 7｜Final Tower Shear
Near top floors:
> the tower begins to separate from the city's version entirely.

Floors shift / shear.
The roof is not the exit.

The only viable exit appears:
> through a space that briefly overlaps a future infrastructure route seen in Field #1.

This becomes the first hard proof that:
> Fields share one impossible future geography.

## Returned Rule Candidate

At exit, the door/recognized-inside rule can be ONE candidate.

It is not the Field identity.

Other candidate rules may derive from:
- shelter recognition;
- occupancy;
- common-space survival.

Do not finalize until full Arc Card.

## Tang Ji / Co-Lead

Tang Ji remains excellent for this rebuilt Field because:
- Cheng Ye wants to preserve structural survivability;
- Tang Ji reacts to people who will die in the next 30 seconds.

But the environment must repeatedly make BOTH approaches insufficient alone.

## Why This Is Bigger Than Current “封门楼”

Old:
> smoke + doors + evacuation.

New:
> **entire inhabited tower is being peeled out of reality floor by floor.**
>
> doors are only one of the ways humans temporarily define “inside.”

Reader should remember:
> disappearing floors / black Outside / impossible skyline / homes being sacrificed,

not:
> “there was a clever door rule.”

# FIELD HORROR STANDARD FOR ALL FUTURE MAJOR FIELDS

Each major Field must answer YES:

1. Is there an impossible-looking objective visible early?
2. Can the environment kill Cheng Ye even if every human cooperates?
3. Does Cheng Ye nearly die at least 4 times for different reasons?
4. Does one safe solution later become dangerous?
5. Is there at least one non-human threat that cannot be negotiated with?
6. Does the Field contain 3+ memorable physical set pieces?
7. Would the Field still be excellent if no rule were exported?
8. Is the exported rule only a fragment, not the whole identity?
9. Does at least one hidden detail gain a new meaning 50+ chapters later?
10. Does the Field change Cheng Ye / a relationship permanently?

If <8 YES:
> reject before prose.
