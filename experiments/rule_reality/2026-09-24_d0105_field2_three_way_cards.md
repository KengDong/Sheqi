# CARD A｜《封门楼》
## Motherboard
Boundary / Permission

## One-line pitch
> **整栋四十层住宅楼都在往外逃，但天黑以后，每一扇彻底关上的门，都只能由“里面的人”打开。**

## Visual identity
- unfinished high-rise;
- emergency lights;
- stairwell packed with people;
- smoke/water rising through floors;
- hundreds of doors clicking shut one after another.

## Human situation
A fire / toxic smoke / flood-like structural emergency is moving upward.

Closed flats are initially safe.
But:
> safety becomes imprisonment.

A child, an old man, a suspected criminal, an injured pregnant woman and a firefighter-like survivor all need contradictory things.

## Local rules｜prototype
1. after a specified bell, a fully closed door cannot be opened from outside;
2. “inside person” can explicitly invite one outsider through;
3. once the last inside person leaves, the room stops recognizing prior permissions.

No more than these three foreground rules.

## Main human conflict
- people behind doors fear letting strangers in;
- rescuers need doors opened;
- one survivor argues resources should only save rooms with the highest survival probability;
- another keeps risking the group for individual rescues.

## Cheng Ye personality test
Someone says:
> “牺牲两户总比整栋楼死强。”

Cheng Ye does NOT answer morally first.
He asks:
> “那两户谁去告诉？谁签这个决定？”

His insistence forces responsibility into the open.

## Midpoint reinterpretation
The danger is not “ghost opens doors.”

The building itself is using closed rooms as isolated survival cells.
The more rooms remain sealed:
> the safer each room is individually,
> but the entire building's shared ventilation/escape system fails faster.

Thus:
> individual safety accelerates collective death.

## Recurring cast seed
A practical emergency-medicine survivor:
- not sentimental;
- repeatedly disagrees with Cheng Ye;
- useful enough to recur.

A second survivor:
- excellent at exploiting permission boundaries;
- charming and selfish;
- possible future rival/friend.

## Climax
The group must choose between:
- preserving a chain of perfectly safe closed rooms;
- intentionally opening/connecting them to restore a shared escape route.

Physical action:
> moving upward through doors, smoke, broken stairs, permission relays.

No meeting climax.

## Candidate return plaques
Prototype only:
A. “关上的门，外面的人无法强行打开。”
B. “被屋内人明确允许的人，可以无视一次门的阻隔。”
C. “一间房里只要还有人愿意留下，外界危险不能主动进入。”

All attractive in-field.
All dangerous in reality.

## Reality consequences
- home security;
- fire rescue;
- domestic violence;
- hospitals;
- prisons;
- shelters.

## Future-fragment clue
The lobby plaque says:
> the tower was completed in a future year.

In current reality:
> that address is still a construction pit / approved project.

## Strength
Highest:
- physical spectacle;
- rescue;
- easy rule;
- human disagreement;
- reality payoff.

## Risk
Could become an “emergency policy simulation.”
Human anchors must dominate.

# CARD B｜《夜班急诊》
## Motherboard
Promise / Role

## One-line pitch
> **午夜以后，急诊科里所有“我来负责”“我保证”“交给我”的话，都会变成必须兑现的规则。**

## Visual identity
- fluorescent hospital;
- storm night;
- overloaded ER;
- patients who should not exist in present records;
- red triage tags.

## Human situation
A mass casualty night.

Doctors/nurses/family members make ordinary comforting promises:
> “我一定救你。”
> “你先进去，我不会走。”
> “费用我负责。”

The Field treats them literally.

## Local rules｜prototype
1. a promise spoken while touching a red triage tag becomes binding;
2. until fulfilled, the promisor cannot leave the hospital;
3. if the promise becomes impossible, an equivalent obligation transfers to the nearest willing witness.

## Main human conflict
People must decide:
- stop promising anything and become brutally cold;
- or promise and risk being trapped/killed.

Patients also exploit promises.

## Cheng Ye personality test
He is the person least willing to say:
> “我保证。”

This makes others initially think he is cold.

But once he says it:
> he will keep it even when the cheaper interpretation exists.

## Midpoint reinterpretation
The hospital is not punishing lies.

It is collapsing because:
> too many unfulfilled obligations have accumulated across staff/patients.

The “monster” is obligation debt.

## Recurring cast seed
A nurse/doctor survivor who believes:
> people need promises to keep moving, even if promises are dangerous.

Strong recurring relationship candidate.

## Climax
To save a ward, Cheng Ye must make ONE deliberately narrow promise whose exact wording costs him something real.

Not lawyer trick:
> wording matters because he refuses to promise what he cannot carry.

## Candidate return plaques
A. “主动承诺的事，必须尽力完成。”
B. “被明确交付的责任，在完成前不能转给他人。”
C. “当事人共同确认的承诺，在一方死亡前不得单方面撤销。”

All socially explosive.

## Reality consequences
- employment;
- romance/family;
- contracts;
- medicine;
- police;
- promises to children.

## Future-fragment clue
Patient system contains:
> Cheng Ye's own name / family member as a future patient,
or
> hospital wing not yet built.

Use only one clue.

## Strength
Highest:
- character revelation;
- emotion;
- Cheng Ye's personality naturally central.

## Risk
Rules may feel abstract/legal.
Needs strong bodily danger and vivid patients.

# CARD C｜《一口价商场》
## Motherboard
Ownership / Price

## One-line pitch
> **这座停业商场里，任何贴上价格的东西，只要有人付得起，就必须成交——包括药、出口、记忆，甚至人的伤。**

## Visual identity
- dead mall;
- digital price signs changing;
- abandoned supermarkets;
- luxury floors;
- service desk;
- escalators running with no power.

## Human situation
Everyone receives limited physical tokens / value tickets.

They need:
- medicine;
- food;
- keys;
- safe routes.

But people begin pricing things that should never be commodities.

## Local rules｜prototype
1. clearly priced goods must transfer when exact payment is made;
2. the seller cannot destroy a priced item to avoid sale;
3. only something genuinely “owned” can be priced.

The third rule creates the mystery:
> who owns a wound? a memory? a door? a person’s time?

## Main human conflict
One survivor starts pricing essential supplies high.
Another sells their own future safety to save family now.

## Cheng Ye personality test
He hates:
> “先定价再说.”

He insists:
> knowing the hidden cost before calling something cheap.

Black comedy can be strong here.

## Midpoint reinterpretation
The Field does not care about money.

It cares about:
> recognized ownership.

The real horror is determining what people truly own.

## Recurring cast seed
A charismatic entrepreneurial survivor:
- brilliant;
- funny;
- shameless;
- sometimes saves everyone by treating rules as a market.

Ideal rival/foil candidate.

## Climax
Exit itself receives a price.

The group can afford it only by deciding:
> what permanently valuable thing one of them is willing to sell.

No simple token grind.

## Candidate return plaques
A. “公开标价且付款完成的商品，卖方不得拒绝成交。”
B. “只有真正属于你的东西，才能被你出售。”
C. “双方确认的价格一旦支付，交易不能撤销。”

## Reality consequences
- online listings;
- scams;
- property;
- medical supplies;
- auctions;
- contracts;
- black comedy.

## Future-fragment clue
Mall contains brands/shops that do not yet exist,
plus one future municipal map.

## Strength
Highest:
- memorable;
- funny;
- commercial;
- strong rival introduction.

## Risk
Most likely to feel “designed clever.”
Could become finance/legal puzzle.
Must keep bodily survival central.
