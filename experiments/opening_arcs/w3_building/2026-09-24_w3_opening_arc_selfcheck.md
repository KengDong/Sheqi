## 2. Opening promise -> consequence

Ch1 promise:
- Chen Yi can take the hospital itself.

Arc consequence:
- a hospital is not equivalent to a loot box;
- it consumes fuel, medicine, blood, road quality, staff attention and social trust;
- once its lights are visible, patients come;
- once patients come, the owner has to decide boundaries, priority, movement and responsibility.

## 3. Concrete problem created by owning the hospital

Settled in this arc:
- Lao Zhou needs antibiotics that were carried east;
- the mobile hospital attracts more patients;
- post-op patients cannot take the rough road;
- Liao Heng understands the hospital's dependency chain and pressures it through **fuel + road + priority rights**, not by blindly grabbing the building core;
- Chen Yi's side must create a safe exit that lets the existing hospital leave with its patients.

Immediate fruit:
- one antibiotic box is recovered;
- Lao Zhou's near-term antibiotic course is reconnected;
- multiple patients receive real care;
- the hospital exits the freight yard without accepting exclusive convoy control.

Cost remains:
- medicine is still insufficient long-term;
- blood stock is zero;
- diesel is only about 32 L;
- Ye Tang is still the only doctor;
- the hospital now has more patients and followers than before.

## 4. Character agency check

### Ye Tang
Not a function piece.
- independently triages patients;
- explicitly rejects ownership by Chen Yi or Liao Heng;
- refuses convoy-priority medicine;
- chooses to stay with the functioning operating room, not with a person;
- determines who is medically urgent and sets the boundary of her own authority.

### Xiao Liang
Moves beyond helper.
- manages the first crude triage queue;
- learns to distinguish urgent vs non-urgent;
- becomes the person organizing bodies, lights and passage;
- directly questions Chen Yi about who counts as "ours".

### Lao Zhou
Not passive cargo.
- reveals Chen Yi's long-standing "take responsibility for what enters his hands" character trait;
- provides the key memory of the weighbridge maintenance lane;
- reframes Chen Yi's behavior from pure engineering competence into a personal compulsion / ethic.

### Zhao Hai
Not a one-scene medicine thief.
- returns one antibiotic box when his companion needs care;
- has a real dependent relationship with A-Qiang;
- chooses to remain because his own person cannot survive the rough road;
- physically helps create the hospital's exit.

### Liao Heng
Not a paper villain.
- correctly identifies the hospital's real dependencies: fuel, road, doctor, blood, medicine;
- offers a rational convoy bargain;
- wants scarce medical capacity to prioritize his own people;
- pressures through logistics and governance rather than cartoon violence;
- remains partially correct about the future burden Chen Yi is taking on.

## 5. Chen Yi texture check

Added texture beyond "correct engineering decisions":
- Lao Zhou identifies his old habit: once Chen Yi accepts a broken machine / trapped vehicle / responsibility, he treats it as an account he has to close;
- Chen Yi does not like making promises he will later violate;
- this is why he refuses Liao Heng's fuel-and-medicine offer when the price is exclusive bed priority;
- he is dry, materially minded, not verbally sentimental;
- his responsibility expands reluctantly: first "whoever is on the vehicle counts", then people begin following the vehicle.

This should remain an engine of both strength and future trouble rather than a pure virtue.
