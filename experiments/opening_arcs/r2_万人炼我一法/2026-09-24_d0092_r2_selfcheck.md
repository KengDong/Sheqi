## 1. Mission Check

### HUMAN-LAYER GAP
**Cleared in draft execution, subject to external reader judgment.**

The first effective change does not come from an abstract testing pool. It comes from **乔青禾**, whose daily life makes the standard method unusable: she must push a bean mill before dawn and wants to reach 感气 so she can apply for an East River warehouse weighing job, reducing her father's physical load and changing her own work life.

Her father is not a prop around the cultivation result. His damaged leg comes from a prior unsafe “quick method”, so his resistance has its own lived basis. The father/daughter conflict exists even if the protagonist's special inheritance is removed.

The protagonist/乔青禾 relationship also changes: he first benefits from her change, then is accused—fairly—of having the luxury to tell her to slow down after he has already gained. His safety intervention helps her but also violates her agency, and she does not instantly forgive him.

## 2. Mandatory Arc Elements

1. **Opening promise** — the cheapest ubiquitous method may be incomplete because it was written around the lives of people with time/quiet, not ordinary workers.
2. **Protagonist concrete desire** — 许照 wants to make his own cheap method actually work after three stagnant years; by the end this expands naturally into making it workable for ordinary lives rather than demanding ordinary people fit the book.
3. **First meaningful fruit** — 乔青禾's work-rhythm breathing lets 许照 retain qi, later saving the three coins he normally spends on qi soup and finally helping him complete a small circulation.
4. **Fruit creates cost** — “walking can cultivate” spreads too simply; 小陶 overtrains while carrying water and becomes physically ill.
5. **Relationship changes** — 许照/乔青禾 trust is strained and partially rebuilt; 乔青禾/乔父 move from prohibition/conflict toward permission after the climax.
6. **World/social interface expands** — copy shop -> tofu mill -> city streets/workers -> 青元讲堂 -> East River warehouse recruitment / formal cultivation institution.
7. **Pressure learns/intensifies** — 宋成 begins as a safety gate, rejects simplistic propagation, then changes the validation method after observing the technique in its native life context.
8. **Protagonist choice** — he gives up the fastest route to two hundred practitioners by halting the exhausting public demonstration and refusing to turn 乔青禾's body into proof.
9. **Mini-climax/payoff** — while doing ordinary mill work, 乔青禾 spontaneously adjusts the method again and achieves 感气; a formal 青元观 instructor publicly states that the old record is wrong. An ordinary practitioner's change overturns the expected result.
10. **Specific larger desire remains** — test the method across different real lives/bodies and discover what happens when ten, a hundred, ten thousand ordinary people each add a piece.

## 3. Frozen Concept Check

- the method's growth depends on other people's real practice, not protagonist solo theorycrafting.

## 5. Prose / Story Risk Check

### Risk A — mechanism may still feel “too convenient” if every valid change instantly benefits protagonist.
Mitigation in arc: the inherited change creates responsibility and propagation danger; the protagonist cannot simply maximize headcount because transfer speed and human safety are in tension.

### Risk B — the climax could read as “institution immediately admits defeat.”
Mitigation in arc: 宋成 is already safety-oriented rather than status-defensive; his concession is narrow and evidence-based (“today works”, future needs more people), not wholesale endorsement.

### Risk C — 乔青禾 could become only the first successful case after this arc.
Open obligation for later continuation: she must retain her own work/career/family trajectory and not disappear once 感气 is achieved.

### Risk D — large-scale compounding could regress into episodic case-of-week.
Open obligation for later continuation: new practitioners should change persistent relationships, institutional rules, labor/cultivation access, and the evolving shared method—not reset after each person.
