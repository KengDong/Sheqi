## Highest findings

No single genre or comedy style was selected.

Strongest cross-sample pattern:

> low-friction consumption promise
> × premise-in-action
> × fast tangible proof
> × fixed reusable story engine
> × human/social second engine
> × permanent state compounding
> × newcomer-sustainable production.

Important correction from current reader evidence:

> Repetition itself is not an automatic failure.

Readers can tolerate and even enjoy repeated progression loops when each loop also changes:
- protagonist status;
- recurring relationships;
- organization/social position;
- assets/abilities;
- enemy knowledge;
- visible next-layer expectation.

The dangerous form is:

> repetition + state reset + no relationship compounding + no pre-signaled next rung.

## Newcomer-risk findings

High theoretical ceiling does not equal high first-book probability.

Default high-risk production burdens:
- multiple concurrent avatars/POVs;
- bespoke fair puzzle every arc;
- all-genius character chess;
- hard-science mystery;
- research-heavy historical/political writing;
- new-world anthology per arc;
- multi-timeline causal bookkeeping;
- giant early ensemble;
- one-off joke production as the primary engine.

## Comedy finding

Comedy may raise probability when generated by:
- character;
- relationship;
- stable social arena;
- world-logic mismatch.

Comedy is not required where another strong reward engine exists.

Comedy is harmful when it:
- cancels stakes;
- forces character stupidity;
- replaces progression;
- homogenizes voices;
- depends on disposable internet memes;
- requires constant standalone jokes.
