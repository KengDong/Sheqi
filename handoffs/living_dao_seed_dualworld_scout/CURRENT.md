## HIGHEST FINDINGS
1. The durable unit of two-world pleasure is not “crossing once”, but “the other world's information changes the protagonist's current decision”.
2. Resource / price gaps are fast but decay quickly; knowledge-production, verification, qualification, status, institutional and relationship gaps are longer-lived.
3. Strong two-world structures are not necessarily “advanced vs backward”; both worlds can dominate different dimensions while retaining exclusive bottlenecks.
4. Ch1–20 can use visible resource / method gaps to prove the mechanic. Ch20–100 must compound identity, relationship, organization and institutional access.
5. Both worlds need independent pressure, status ladders and recurring consequences; otherwise one becomes a warehouse world.
6. Long-run hits compound resource capital + knowledge capital + identity capital + relationship / institutional capital.
7. “Merchant collapse” happens when every difference can be priced; once the protagonist is rich, the core mechanism stops changing choices.
