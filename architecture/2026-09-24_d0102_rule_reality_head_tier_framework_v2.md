# 《把一条副本规则带回现实》｜Head-Tier Framework V2
## 目标不是“规则点子新”，而是成为完整的头部级长篇发动机

# 0. Commercial Positioning

## One-line shelf promise

> **程野每次从死亡副本活着回来，都必须带一条规则回现实——后来他才发现，别人也在带，而这些副本可能就是现实未来已经毁掉的样子。**

Reader immediately receives:
- dungeon;
- rule;
- irreversible reward/cost;
- other returners;
- terminal mystery.

## Three-sentence retell

1. 通关副本，必须把一条规则永久带回现实。
2. 规则会真的改变家人、城市和所有普通人的生活，而且其他幸存者也在做同样的事。
3. 程野后来发现，副本不是异世界，而可能是现实自己的失败未来；每带回一条规则，都在把某个未来拉近。

If a reader cannot retell these three layers:
> the commercial surface has failed.

# 1. Six Interlocked Engines

This is the central V2 correction.

## Engine 1｜RULE FIELD SURVIVAL
Each Rule Field must be worth reading as a strong standalone survival story.

Food:
- horror;
- puzzle;
- human choice;
- betrayal/trust;
- spectacle;
- local ecology.

## Engine 2｜IRREVERSIBLE RETURN CHOICE
Survival is not the final reward.

The more important question:
> **which rule is allowed to return?**

A useful rule may be terrible for normal life.
A safe rule may weaken future survival.

## Engine 3｜REALITY TRANSFORMATION
Returned rules become public physics.

They change:
- home;
- police;
- work;
- crime;
- housing;
- markets;
- relationships;
- language;
- city habits.

But always through recurring humans first.

## Engine 4｜CHENG YE AGENCY GROWTH
The protagonist must visibly gain more control over the core action of the novel.

Not combat stats.
Not a second system.

Progression:
> 被拖进去 -> 能提前进 -> 能识别不同入口 -> 能选入口 -> 能和可信队友同步进入 -> 能付出巨大代价阻止一条规则回来 -> 最终尝试写出一条不属于任何失败未来的新规则.

## Engine 5｜RECURRING PEOPLE / RIVALS
Family, Fang Cen, survivors and returner rivals must carry arcs independent of new rules.

Reader should sometimes continue because:
> “I want to see what this person does.”

## Engine 6｜FAILED-FUTURE TERMINAL SPINE
Every major phase pays one physical clue.

The mystery changes:
> 谁造副本？
to
> 这是未来吗？
to
> 我们是不是正在把失败未来拉回来？
to
> 为什么所有未来都会失败？
to
> 能不能写出一个从未失败过的新规则？

# 2. Three Rule Layers｜Cognitive Control

To avoid rule encyclopedia, the book has only three conceptual layers.

## Layer A｜Field Rules
- temporary;
- local to a Rule Field;
- many can exist inside one Field;
- most die with the Field.

Reader only needs 2–5 active rules at one time.

## Layer B｜Returned Rules
- ONE important public law crosses from a completed Field;
- permanent within its current reality zone;
- may later collide/spread/merge.

Only 1–3 Returned Rules may be foregrounded in a major arc.

## Layer C｜Returner Access
Sparse permissions related to entering/choosing fields.

This is Cheng Ye's personal progression.

No fourth RPG layer.

# 3. Return Rule Selection｜Do Not Over-Lock Yet

The root remains:
> every completed Rule Field must cause one rule to cross back into reality.

The exact multi-survivor selection procedure is NOT canon yet.

Prototype options to test in Field Design:
- one designated Returner has final choice;
- one exit-object carries the chosen rule;
- survivors' actions determine which candidate survives to the exit;
- only one of several candidate rules physically remains at completion.

Hard:
> avoid meeting/vote fiction as the primary climax.

The final selection should be dramatized through action and sacrifice.

# 4. Terminal Spine V2｜The Second City

The macro secret needs an ICON, not only an explanation.

Recurring image:
> **现实城市后面偶尔会出现一层“错开的城市重影”。**

Early:
- reflection in glass shows a station sign that does not exist;
- a street in a Field has a building planned but not yet constructed;
- an object has a future date.

Later:
- one building briefly has two floor plans;
- two people remember different shop names;
- a street becomes its failed-future version for minutes.

Much later:
> two skylines remain visible together.

This visual is the physical face of the terminal line.

# 5. Failed-Future Logic｜Deep Canon

Do NOT expose early.

V2 working truth:

> **Rule Fields are not simulations or alien worlds. They are fragments of future branches in which a simple rule-set was allowed to grow until that branch became unlivable.**

The mechanism is not necessarily evil.

Working deeper hypothesis:
> present reality is unstable and is attempting to “repair” itself by sampling laws that humans have already survived somewhere.

Returners are effectively:
> human viability tests.

The tragedy:
> all sampled branches are failed futures.

Therefore:
> importing survivable local rules can still make the total world converge toward failure.

# 6. Terminal Event｜归一日

Do NOT reveal the term/page-one countdown early.

Through future-fragment evidence, returners eventually discover:

> **many different future branches stop leaving records after the same date/window.**

That point becomes known as:
> 归一日.

Interpretation:
> by then, incompatible reality branches can no longer coexist; one coherent rule-world will overwrite the rest.

This gives:
- a terminal secret;
- a terminal crisis;
- a future destination;
- a reason choices matter now.

# 7. Endgame Promise

The protagonist's progression mirrors the whole book:

## Beginning
> 必须从坏规则里选一条带回来。

## Middle
> 能决定进哪个Field、和谁进去、阻止哪条规则回来。

## Endgame
> **第一次尝试不从任何失败未来里选，而是让现实写出一条从未存在过的新规则。**

This is the full-book payoff.

# 8. Cheng Ye｜Protagonist Reforge

Current human strength:
- responsible;
- grounded;
- family-first.

Current weakness:
> replaceable by another competent male lead.

## Personality Core

> **程野习惯先问“最坏结果谁来承担”，极烦含糊条款；自己吃亏可以，替别人签风险不行。**

This is not morality wallpaper.
It repeatedly changes decisions.

## Scene-Generation Bias

When someone says:
> “应该没事。”

Cheng Ye instinctively asks:
> “谁负责证明‘没事’？”

When someone proposes a powerful returned rule:
> he asks who pays for it at 2 a.m. in an ordinary apartment, hospital, shop or family.

This creates:
- conflict;
- dry humor;
- responsibility;
- rule-choice identity.

## Flaw

He over-assumes responsibility.

He often thinks:
> if I chose the rule, I must personally solve every consequence.

Long-term growth:
> learns to let affected people choose, disagree and take ownership.

This protects supporting-cast agency.

## Private Desire

Not “save humanity.”

Early:
> **get his family back to a life where one bad night does not control the next day.**

Later he realizes:
> “normal” itself is changing.

# 9. Optional Occupation Calibration

Before P3, test one mundane profession that supports—not replaces—personality.

Best functional option:
> insurance/accident claims investigator OR urban incident-response worker.

Why:
- evidence;
- responsibility;
- “who bears cost” vocabulary;
- ordinary-city access.

Do NOT turn the novel into workplace procedure.

If profession makes prose feel more designed:
> keep occupation ordinary and let personality carry the function.

# 10. Core Cast V2

Do not create a giant ensemble early.

## 周芸｜Mother
Function:
> ordinary life / household economics.

Independent desire:
> keep the family/store/life functioning instead of becoming permanent refugees of Cheng Ye's choices.

She should sometimes oppose “safest” solutions because:
> safe but unlivable is not life.

## 小满｜Sister
Current debt/exposure remains useful.

Upgrade:
> she becomes an active ordinary-person adaptation engine.

Possible functions:
- online/social information;
- “夜名” culture;
- young-person rule hacks;
- refuses protective confinement.

She must solve at least one serious problem without Cheng Ye.

## 方岑｜Public-Safety Counterweight
She is not an exposition police officer.

Desire:
> make rules usable for thousands of ordinary people, not only clever survivors.

Conflict with Cheng Ye:
- emergency utility;
- evidence;
- public safety;
- when is a returned rule acceptable?

Chemistry:
> dry, practical, sometimes funny because both hate vague instructions in different ways.

## Recurring Survivor A｜Safety Human
Someone with concrete dependent/family stakes.

Desire:
> choose the least harmful rules.

Can become Cheng Ye's ally while disagreeing with him.

## Recurring Survivor B / Rival｜Power Human
Charismatic, useful, often correct.

Belief:
> reality is already losing; choosing weak “comfortable” rules only makes humanity less prepared.

This person should save Cheng Ye at least once.

# 11. Long-Term Rival｜The Future-Wanter

Upgrade the rival beyond ideology.

Core personal wound:
> **in current reality, someone they loved is dead/lost; in one failed future fragment, that person is alive.**

Therefore they genuinely want:
> that branch to become more real.

They choose rules that increase similarity to that future.

Reader should sometimes think:
> “If that were my family, I might do the same.”

This turns terminal conflict from policy debate into human desire.

Later mirror:
> Cheng Ye finds a failed future in which someone from his own family is alive/safe in a way current reality cannot promise.

Now his principle is tested.

# 12. Rule Field Design OS

Every Field must pass ALL seven.

## 1. One-Line Human Situation
Not:
> “a building with 11 rules.”

But:
> “a whole apartment tower is trying to evacuate before sunrise, but every closed door becomes absolute.”

## 2. Visual/Ecological Identity
Reader remembers the PLACE:
- flooded mall;
- vertical apartment;
- night hospital;
- abandoned station;
- wedding district;
etc.

No generic rooms.

## 3. One Dominant Rule Family
Examples:
- names/identity;
- doors/permission;
- price/exchange;
- memory;
- sequence/time;
- promises/obligation.

Do not combine five unrelated clever rules.

## 4. Human Conflict
Someone wants something besides “survive”:
- save child;
- hide crime;
- reach lover;
- protect evidence;
- make money;
- abandon group.

## 5. Mid-Field Reinterpretation
The apparent rule is not simply wrong.

Reader discovers:
> a second consequence / different subject / boundary condition.

No cheap wording gotcha.

## 6. Exit Choice
The rule most useful inside the Field should NOT automatically be best for reality.

This is the signature moral-commercial payoff.

## 7. Future Fragment
One physical fact connects the Field to terminal spine:
- date;
- building;
- old photo;
- person's future identity;
- impossible map.

Only ONE strong clue per major Field.

# 13. Rule Field Variety Map

Rotate story grammar, not only scenery.

## Type A｜Trust Field
Social deduction / lies / names.

## Type B｜Spatial Field
doors / zones / line-of-sight / refuge.

## Type C｜Exchange Field
price / debt / ownership / trade.

## Type D｜Identity/Memory Field
who counts as whom / memory mismatch.

## Type E｜Sequence Field
time / order / causality.

## Type F｜Collective Obligation Field
promise / vote / group responsibility.

## Type G｜Open-City Field
large area; rule impacts hundreds rather than 6 players.

Hard:
> do not run the same type twice consecutively.

# 14. Reality Arc Design OS

Every Returned Rule goes through a lifecycle.

## Phase 1｜Shock
one family/person pays first.

## Phase 2｜Exploit
someone uses it intentionally.

## Phase 3｜Adapt
people invent workaround/culture.

## Phase 4｜Institution
police/business/housing etc changes.

## Phase 5｜Normalization
the rule becomes boring daily infrastructure.

## Phase 6｜Revaluation
later rule collision makes the old rule valuable/dangerous again.

Do NOT dramatize every phase equally.

Rule:
> recurring humans first; society second.

# 15. Reality Food｜What Reader Gets Besides Rules

## A. Household life
food / shop / debt / school / relationships.

## B. Black comedy
normal people rationally adapt to impossible physics.

## C. Assets
- prep base;
- safe routes;
- analog records;
- contacts;
- future-fragment evidence;
- equipment selected for Fields.

## D. Status
from:
> weird survivor
to
> trusted specialist / feared returner / person whose choice matters.

## E. Economy
rules change:
- property value;
- privacy;
- security;
- logistics;
- insurance;
- labor.

Follow through named people/assets, not lecture.

# 16. Personal Progression V2

No level numbers.

## Stage 0｜Dragged
forced entry.

## Stage 1｜Early Entry
can choose to enter before countdown zero.

Reward:
> preparation agency.

## Stage 2｜Differentiate
can sense that two nearby openings lead to different Field families.

Reward:
> selection agency.

## Stage 3｜Choose
can actively choose one of multiple openings.

Reward:
> strategic agency.

## Stage 4｜Synchronize
can pull ONE consenting proven teammate into the same Field under strict conditions.

Reward:
> relationship/team agency.

## Stage 5｜Reject
can pay a major accumulated cost to prevent ONE candidate Returned Rule from crossing.

Reward:
> negative choice becomes a real prize.

## Stage 6｜Author
endgame attempt:
> create/allow a rule that has no failed-future origin.

This is the ultimate progression.

# 17. Reality-Overlap Adaptation｜Single Secondary Faculty

To keep personal progression visceral without a second system:

Repeated Returners gradually develop ONE deepening phenomenon:

> **they remember contradictions when reality briefly overlaps failed futures.**

Early:
- Cheng Ye notices a shop sign is wrong while others don't.

Mid:
- retains both versions of an event for minutes.

Late:
- can deliberately keep one agreed-upon fact/person anchored through a short overlap at severe cognitive/physical cost.

This is not a skill list.
It is one faculty growing with terminal-spine exposure.

Hard:
> no “memory power level” UI.

# 18. Reward Rotation

A major arc can pay in 2–4 currencies:

1. survival;
2. new Returned Rule;
3. prevention of a worse rule;
4. Access Authority;
5. recurring teammate;
6. changed family relationship;
7. public status;
8. prep asset;
9. future-fragment answer;
10. money/resource;
11. rival relationship;
12. overlap-memory growth.

Never pay 3 consecutive arcs mainly in “new rule.”

# 22. Ch21–50｜Second Field

Must prove:
> the dungeon side alone is head-tier readable.

Design rule:
> 20–30 chapters maximum if it cannot sustain character/world depth.

Goals:
- recurring survivor A/B;
- a Field with its own ecology;
- R1 matters once but cannot solve it;
- Cheng Ye's personality creates a conflict;
- Rule-return choice is harder than final monster/puzzle;
- one future clue.

End fruits:
- second Returned Rule (prototype until field design);
- one recurring person;
- one Access upgrade;
- reality liability immediately visible.

# 23. Ch51–80｜Old Rules Carry the Book

No new Returned Rule for most/all of this segment.

Mode:
> reality adaptation + crime/rescue + family + rival.

This segment is the CR6 kill test.

It must be enjoyable because:
- people changed;
- asset values changed;
- Fang Cen has a real case;
- sister/mother act;
- rival appears;
- old rules collide.

If this segment feels like “waiting for next dungeon”:
> ROOT ENGINE WARNING.

# 24. Ch81–120｜Third Field + Rival

Field type must differ radically from second.

Now Cheng Ye enters around/with an ideological rival.

Goals:
- reader likes rival;
- each makes a defensible rule choice;
- one cannot fully beat the other;
- Field clearly resembles a future version of real city.

End:
> first city-block reality overlap.

This is the first macro-spine spectacle.

# 25. Ch121–200｜City Rule Ecology

The book stops being “private survivor story”.

But scale is entered through people:

- residents move;
- property changes;
- families choose zones;
- rule brokers emerge;
- safety services change;
- a teammate leaves or joins for a personal reason.

Cheng Ye gains:
- choice between openings;
- small-team leadership;
- stronger public identity.

Macro mystery:
> future-fragment theory becomes credible but not complete.

# 26. Ch201–320｜Regional Convergence

Mode changes:
> exploration / migration / competing returners.

Different cities have different rule stacks.

This creates:
- travel;
- commerce;
- dangerous borders;
- incompatible daily life;
- new Field strategies.

Important:
> no need to add many new rules; old rule combinations define city identity.

Terminal clue:
> multiple fragments end records near the same future date.

“归一日” becomes a hypothesis.

# 27. Ch321–500｜Choose a Future?

Now factions emerge AFTER people and rules have made them inevitable.

Not political-party lecture.

Three human positions naturally exist:

## Minimal-Harm
choose least disruptive rules.

## Strength-First
choose rules that make humans stronger enough to survive convergence.

## Future-Wanters
deliberately align reality with a branch where something they lost still exists.

Rival becomes emotionally central.

Cheng Ye finds:
> one failed future that tempts HIM personally.

# 28. Ch500+｜No Failed Future Is Enough

Reveal:
> all sampled futures end.

The repair mechanism has never found a branch that survives after 归一日.

Final shift:
> stop choosing among failed answers.

The target becomes:
> create a rule with no failed-future source.

The first chapter says:
> you MUST bring one rule back.

The finale asks:
> can humanity return something the Field never offered?

Structural circle complete.

# 31. Comparison Target After V2

We do NOT claim achieved superiority.

The framework is designed to seek:

## Against 《十日终焉》
Match:
- macro recontextualization;
- recurring cast.
Differentiate:
- game result permanently rewrites outside reality.

## Against 《诡舍》
Match:
- forced cadence;
- strong Field ecology.
Differentiate:
- between Fields, reality is not rest—it is half the novel.

## Against 《灵境行者》
Match:
- dual world/field engine;
- protagonist visible agency growth;
- organizations/status.
Differentiate:
- reward is public law, not mainly personal skill.

## Against 《神秘复苏》
Match:
- rules remain dangerous and costly.
Differentiate:
- humans choose/import the rules and therefore bear responsibility.

## Against 《异兽迷城》
Match:
- terminal truth penetrates family.
Differentiate:
- every arc can literally alter social reality.

## Against 《惊悚乐园》 / 《时停》
Match:
- protagonist must become recognizable enough to carry mediocre-good arcs.
Differentiate:
- black-comedy reality adaptation is premise-generated.

# 33. Final V2 Standard

This book is ready for serious serialization only when prose proves all of:

> **我想看下一个Field**
> + **我想看这群人**
> + **我想看旧规则怎么继续害/帮现实**
> + **我想看程野到底能变成什么**
> + **我想知道第二层城市是什么**
> + **我想知道归一日前会发生什么**

If any one engine is absent for too long:
> rotation must bring another forward.

That is the intended full-spectrum head-tier shape.
